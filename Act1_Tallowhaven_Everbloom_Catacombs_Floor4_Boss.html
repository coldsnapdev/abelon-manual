Catacombs, Floor 4: The Lake
A sealed door, the Officiant's Stole, and a Rot Monstrosity held in its lake by fire
ExplorationBoss encounterRest and attuneLevel 3–4 party

Floor 4 is where the theme becomes something the party has to act on. The stairs down arrive at the lower hall, where the dead adventurer who came this way before sits slumped against a heavy door. The door is fused shut by hardened Rot, and the way through is salt or fire. Behind it is the altar chamber, which holds the Officiant's Stole and the one place in the campaign where the ring and the stole can first be attuned. A party that stops to rest and attune, if it occurs to them and they are brave enough, walks into the fight stronger. The arena below is a lake of Rot with a circular platform and a Rot Monstrosity that the keepers' torches keep in the water. Flanking the hall are narrow maze wings with spike traps, trap doors and hidden doors that open onto dead ends.

At a glance
Arrival and the Door
Arrival: The stairs from floor 3 land near the lower hall. The dead adventurer is the first thing the party sees, slumped against the sealed door with a page clutched to his chest.
The adventurer: Medicine DC 12: no killing wound, the body simply gave out. He carries the salt crock from floor 3's cache, though he had no idea what it was for. His page is fixed to the body and sits outside the handout order.
The heavy door: Rot has fused the frame. The seal has 20 HP. Physical damage counts in full, fire counts double and salt clears it instantly. Torches in brackets beside the door are fire.
Touching it: Striking the seal barehanded, including unarmed strikes, means a Constitution save at DC 10 (+1 per stack). Weapons do not count.
Beyond the Door
The altar: The Officiant's Stole lies on the altar. Aeron's half is embroidered in gold, Zareth's in shadowed grey-black. It is attunable only here, and the party has time to rest and attune before the fight.
Reactions: The ring warms faintly in the wearer's hand, the first reaction it has ever shown. The box's hum is louder here than on floor 3.
The arena: A semicircular platform lined with torches, a narrow stair, a circular platform in a lake of Rot on every side. The Rot Monstrosity waits in the lake.
The Maze Wings
Shape: Narrow corridors that feel like a maze but reward attention. Warm air drifts toward the lake and dead ends are still and cold.
Hazards: Rot-soaked spike traps and trap doors into Rot sludge. Hidden doors lead through the maze or to dead ends. Corner bone piles are older, nameless dead.
Location Descriptions
The lower hall

The first place the party sees on arrival: a wide, low hall with torches in brackets either side of a broad, iron-banded wooden door at the far end. Hardened black matter fills the gap around the frame, and faint steam seeps from the seams. Two grates sit in the floor.

Loot

The adventurer: a skeleton in rotted leather armour, slumped against the door with a travel-worn pack and one bony hand still clutching a torn yellowed page against its chest. The page is the adventurer's own note, in a different hand from Adelina's. It opens with "Her notes stop at six..." and covers what happens past stage 6. It stays on the body and is not part of the handout order.

The pack holds the salt crock. Salt destroys frozen Rot, cleans contaminated objects and breaks Rot seals such as this door. The crock is the answer to the door that the adventurer never reached.

Optional belongings, not confirmed: empty healing potion vials, a light purse of 20 to 30gp, a dagger, a coil of rope and a set of thieves' tools. Everything ordinary, and none of it enough.

The heavy door

Broad and low, iron-banded, and fused shut by the Rot. Black matter fills the gap around the frame. Brute force works only slowly. The torches either side of the door are in reach of anyone standing at it.

The maze wings

Two narrow wings flank the hall. Corridors turn sharply, the walls are close enough to touch and dead roots creep along the upper stonework. Warm air drifts along the true route toward the lake with a faint haze of steam. Dead ends are still and cold. Bone piles sit in the corners and hold nothing of value.

The spike traps

The same keepers' mechanism as on floors 2 and 3, rotted further. The spikes have been sitting in Rot, so the trap is the same one the party already understands, but deadlier.

The trap-door corridor

One corridor has trap doors in its floor. A seam in the flagstones or a slab that rings hollow underfoot gives them away. Beneath each is a ten-foot drop into a pit of Rot sludge.

The altar chamber

A lit chamber at the back of the central structure, behind the heavy door. Quiet and reverent, with an altar at its centre. It is the one place both gods are still present, and it is the only place the ring and the stole can attune.

Loot

The Officiant's Stole lies flat on top of the altar: a sash-thick ribbon of aged fabric, folded once at the centre, with Aeron's half embroidered in warm gold thread with growing leaves and budding vines, and Zareth's half in deep grey-black thread with withering leaves and bare roots. Religion DC 13: the two halves are Aeron and Zareth, a vestment for a rite.

While attuned: advantage on saves against the Rot, and +1d4 to every instance of magical healing the wearer receives. Examination shows it needs attunement and is dormant until then.

The arena

A vast chamber below the altar, reached by a narrow stair. A semicircular platform lined with torches sits before a circular platform with one narrow stair on and off. The lake is thick, glossy and black and moves in slow swirls under steam. Beyond the circle, darkness swallows the far walls. The torches are why the Monstrosity stays put in the lake. They do not stop it once the fight has started.

NPCs and Creatures
The Rot Monstrosity Large ooze. A literal chunk of Zareth given form. See the combat guide.
▼
Role

The dungeon's final encounter. It guards nothing in the way a locked door guards something. It is the Rot that pooled at the bottom of the temple and it lives in the lake the keepers' torches enclose.

Combat

AC 14, 136 HP, speed 50 ft, CR 5, Rot DC 17, three legendary actions. Vulnerable to radiant and fire. Resistant to bludgeoning (also Slowed), slashing, piercing and psychic. Immune to necrotic (heals it). Two Surge attacks a turn at +7, reach 10 ft, 14 (3d6+4) necrotic and a DC 17 Constitution save or a stack of Rotten. Corroding Presence 5 ft, Harden recharge 5 to 6.

Full stats and tactics

See the Rot Monstrosity combat guide for the stat block, legendary actions, Effluent triggers and the Forge JSON.

Encounter Beats
1 Arrival and the adventurer ▼

Open the scene at the arrival point that matches the staircase the party took. The adventurer is the first thing they see. Let the party take in the posture: a body slumped against a sealed door with a page held to the chest. Do not explain how he died. A Medicine check at DC 12 shows no killing wound, only a body that gave out.

Searching the body is the most searched spot on the floor. The pack holds the salt crock. The page is the adventurer's own note. Let the player who finds the salt remember the empty ring in the dust on floor 3, if they searched the cache.

2 The heavy door ▼

The door is sealed by Rot, not by a lock. Brute force works slowly, and anyone who strikes the seal barehanded must save against the Rot. Salt clears the seal at once. Fire counts double, so 10 fire damage clears it, and the torches in the brackets are right there. A party that has not found the salt can still get through with the torches, fire spells or a long time hacking.

The door's job is to teach the Rot's vulnerabilities one door before the boss. The salt from floor 3 pays that off. A party that read Adelina's pages will connect it to the door without being told.

3 The maze wings ▼

The wings are optional exploration. Use them to spend resources and to reward attention, not to block progress. Warm air drifts along the true route toward the lake. A party that notices the draught with passive Perception 12 finds its way. A party that does not wanders, triggers more traps and arrives worn down.

If a hidden door is the only way forward, make the dead end in front of it obviously wrong: a corridor that runs into a blank wall with the warm air flowing into it. Hidden doors that open optional rooms can be as hard to find as you like. After a failed active search, tell them they cannot shake the feeling they missed something.

The trap doors teach the Rot track the hard way. Landing in the sludge means a Constitution save at DC 14 on landing and each round spent in it.

4 The altar chamber ▼

Behind the opened door, the chamber is quiet and lit. The ring warms faintly in the wearer's hand, the first reaction it has ever shown, and the box hums louder than it did on floor 3. Nobody tells the party what to do with that. A player who has read Dohyuk's letter can put it together: a relic needs a source of its own god, and this is where both gods are still present.

Attuning the ring or the stole takes a short rest. The Monstrosity stays in the lake while the torches burn. The party can rest here and walk into the fight with Healing Word working and the stole on, if it occurs to them and they are brave enough. Do not prompt them.

5 The arena ▼

Describe the semicircular platform and the torches first, then the circle in the lake, then the dark that swallows the far walls. The black surface moves slowly in swirls with steam rising from it. The Monstrosity is not visible until it chooses to rise.

Use the combat guide for the fight. The lake is the other hazard: anyone knocked or shoved in makes a Constitution save on entry and at the start of each turn in it.

6 After the fight ▼

Defeating the Monstrosity gives access to the small sliver of remaining Aeronic power embedded in the walls and soil beneath the temple, alongside the abundant Zarethi power that remains. This is the location where the ring can attune, and it is open to a party that skipped the altar.

What happens after the party leaves is still open.

DM Notes
No replacement trigger.The altar does not require leaving something in return for the stole. "You cannot remove, you must replace" is Marek's interpretation of how the world responded to the Shattering, and the dungeon should never confirm it. Taking the stole carries no consequence.
What is on floor 4.Very few made it this far, so there is almost nothing here besides the stole. Do not add loot. The bone piles stay empty because it is better that not every corpse pays out.
Ring and box.The ring has 3 charges in a single shared pool. One charge is a free Healing Word with no slot. All three is a free Revivify with no slot and no material component. Charges return after a long rest once attuned. Using Revivify cracks the ring and breaks attunement. The box attunes with Rot, at the surface shrine, on floor 3 or at the base.
The stole.The +1d4 applies to healing the wearer receives. It is attunable only here, where both gods are present, and it may never be possible to attune again elsewhere. That gives the party a reason to attune it on the spot.
Where the stairs arrive.The adventurer sits in front of the sealed door and the stairs from floor 3 arrive close to him. If your map has the staircases landing in side rooms, adjust so the adventurer is still the first thing the party sees.
The two grates.Optional: the two grates in the lower hall could be drains down to the lake, letting the Rot seep up through them mid-fight so the room the party thought was safe stops being safe. Not locked.
Rot rules.Use the Rot DC by source (Dreg 10, Skulker 12, Beast 14, Monstrosity 17, non-creature 10, submerged 12, 14 or 17), with +1 per existing stack. Six stacks is the cap, and any further infestation drops the character to 0 HP. Magical healing clears every stack. Rest clears none.
