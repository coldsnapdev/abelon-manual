Mission Reference — Act 1 / Everbloom Path / Second Mission
The Everbloom Shrine
Where the ring and the box came from — and where the party first learns the basement is real
Social encounter, light explorationNo required combatLevel 3 partyHands off into the Otho disappearance

Otho has told the party where the ring and the box came from: a modest, still-active shrine called Everbloom, which reveres the full lifecycle rather than either Aeron or Zareth alone. Arriving, the party finds a community quietly falling apart -- not through drama, but through a council too fractured by its own grief, denial, and fear to act on what it already half-understands. If they show either relic, they'll learn there are more like it below, and that the basement holds more than items -- it holds power. Getting past that requires the Shrinekeepers' Diary, which Otho has taken from the shrine and which the party does not have yet. Returning to the Threadbare Spine to press him, they find the shop half collapsed and Otho gone. Huw, Alric's valet, is in the infirmary with a heavily bandaged shoulder, and the Diary is in his pack beside the bed.

At a glance
Part 1 — The Shrine
Arrival: Tamsin is first to see the party approaching and makes contact -- welcoming, not gatekeeping.
First point of contact: Dagny is the natural first point of real conversation. Honest but not open -- “it's not a museum” closes the relics line without her needing to lie.
Costin, on the way out: Costin repositions so the party has to pass him on the way out. He's overheard everything and offers a path forward -- at a price -- if Dagny's said no.
Rue's prayer offer: Rue invites the party to a short prayer ceremony on Aeron and Zareth's dual harmony. They'll likely decline; that's the correct outcome, not a failure state.
Aldous, in the background: seen early, entering the outhouse (stowing something in his own small hatch -- unrelated to the true basement). Unlikely to engage unprompted.
What they actually need: entry to the true basement (not Aldous's mundane hatch) requires the Shrinekeepers' Diary. Otho has it, and Otho is missing -- this thread doesn't resolve here.
Part 2 -- Return to the Threadbare Spine
The Spine: destroyed as a going concern. One side of the building still stands with shelves and stock intact and searchable. The other side is rubble. Otho is gone and has nowhere to return to.
Asking around: leads to the infirmary, where Huw lies unconscious, his shoulder heavily bandaged.
The Diary: in Huw's pack beside his bed, with Isolde's Marvellous Pigments. Once awake, Huw gives the party the Diary readily, without being asked. The pigments come if the party asks or has built enough rapport.
The shoulder: glass underneath. A skill ladder (Beat 9) lets the party work out why.
Location Descriptions
Everbloom Shrine

An open-air sanctuary rather than a grand temple -- worn stone columns wrapped in withered vines support a modest wooden roof over a stone altar lit with candles. A carved statue bears Aeron's Spark: a diamond offset within concentric circles. The ring set into the statue's hand is plain stone -- the real one is missing, and the party is carrying it. The clearing feels tended but tired; the lush countryside visible at the horizon only sharpens how faded things are up close.

Tamsin's outhouse

Part caretaking shed, part personal space, attached to the shrine itself. This is where Aldous keeps his own small hatch of personal trinkets -- mundane, harmless, and entirely separate from the true basement beneath the shrine.

The Threadbare Spine, collapsed

Otho's shop, half collapsed. One side of the building still stands, with shelves and stock intact and searchable. The other side is rubble, with beams down and dust still settling. Quiet in the specific way a place is quiet right after something happens in it.

The infirmary

Small, plain, a handful of beds behind curtains. Huw lies in one of them, unconscious, his shoulder heavily bandaged. His pack sits beside the bed with the Shrinekeepers' Diary and Isolde's Marvellous Pigments inside.

NPCs
Rue Ashdown Eldest of the council — denial as devotion
▼
Visual description

Elderly human woman, silver hair pulled back tightly, worn ceremonial robes in faded earth tones. Stern, composed, upright despite frailty.

Personality points

Speaks first out of habit rather than consensus. Doubt reads as betrayal to her -- conversations that edge toward questioning the gods get shut down before they go anywhere.

Goals for this encounter

Convinced the answer is more devotion, not different devotion. Will invite the party to a short prayer ceremony on Aeron and Zareth's dual harmony.

What she knows

Knows the ritual to enter the true basement (via the Shrinekeepers' Diary) but will never act on it or reveal it -- facing the basement risks confirming prayer alone hasn't been enough.

Dagny Holt Council elder — quiet practical grief
▼
Visual description

Bronze dragonborn, scales dulled and slightly tarnished rather than gleaming. Still carries herself with dignity, worn down underneath it.

Personality points

Talks about the decline the way you'd talk about weather -- matter of fact, no anger left in it. The easiest elder for the party to get real information from, since she isn't defending anything.

Goals for this encounter

Assumes the party are there to worship and greets them as such. If they're frank about carrying relics from the shrine, her tone cools quickly -- not hostile, just closing the door gently: “it's not a museum, they're not on display.”

What she knows

Knows the shrine has been, and continues to be, home to a wide array of relics -- donations and curated pieces both. Knows the ritual for the true basement but won't act on it; she's stopped believing action changes anything.

Costin Marrow Council elder — wary of outsiders, closest to the truth
▼
Visual description

Half-elf, appears middle-aged but subtly ageless. Close-cropped greying hair, watchful and guarded expression.

Personality points

Not hostile, just worn down by watching things leave with strangers over the years. Assumes the party's here to take something too, until they prove otherwise -- a learned pattern, not cruelty. Repositions himself during the visit so the party has to pass him on the way out.

Goals for this encounter

Gathers as much information as he can about what the party knows and carries. Unimpressed by how little they understand, but encouraging rather than dismissive -- he's quietly testing whether they're worth his time.

What he knows

Understands intellectually what's wrong -- “you cannot remove, you must replace” -- and fears what's in the basement, but is too politically powerless and personally afraid to act alone. Reveals the basement holds more than items, it holds power. If the party hasn't succeeded with Dagny, he offers a path forward: securing the Shrinekeepers' Diary from Otho, currently undecided whether he wants it returned to the shrine properly or kept for himself, outside the council's sanction.

Tamsin Rook The caretaker
▼
Visual description

Dwarf, stocky and practical build, sleeves rolled up, hands roughened from physical work.

Personality points

Spends more time physically tending the shrine than sitting in council. Answers come through what she's doing rather than what she believes -- good texture, not exposition.

Goals for this encounter

First to see the party approaching. Welcoming, not gatekeeping -- mentions the elders are nearby without it feeling like a summons.

What she knows

Practical knowledge of the shrine's upkeep. No deep theological insight, and no reason to volunteer any if she had it.

Aldous Vey Youngest council elder — background presence
▼
Visual description

Young human man, thoughtful and uncertain expression, plain robes marking him as junior among his peers.

Personality points

Suspects something close to the real truth but lacks the standing or nerve to act on it. Seen early entering the outhouse (stowing something in his own small hatch of personal trinkets -- unrelated to the true basement). Unlikely to be proactive in engaging the party.

Goals for this encounter

Stays largely passive. May emerge from the outhouse at some point but won't approach unprompted.

What he knows

Knows the ritual for the true basement but, like Rue and Dagny, won't reveal it. His silence reads as uncertainty rather than denial or fear specifically.

Huw Alric's valet — found in the infirmary, hurt
▼
Visual description

Same young, earnest bearing as ever, undercut by a heavily bandaged shoulder and the stillness of someone who has been through a collapse.

What he knows

He found the Diary himself while searching the Spine for clues about how the Isoldean pigments work. Otho caught him holding it and reacted with sudden, pointed fury. Otho tried to reason with him, then fired a very mild spell almost on instinct to make him drop it. Huw reacted defensively, Otho took that as aggression and fired again, and Huw's protective magic backfired and brought the building down. Otho escaped. Huw was buried under rubble with the Diary still in his hand.

Personality points

He has not lied once, but the true story sounds coincidental and the party already half suspects him of lying. That is consistent with the town's pattern of misplaced suspicion. He gives the party the Diary readily, even unasked.

The glass shoulder

DC 12 Investigation or Medicine: a wound from the collapse shows the shoulder is glass underneath. DC 15 on the same checks: a lump of clay sits at its centre. DC 12 Insight: the clay was placed on purpose, part of the original installation. DC 15 Religion (DC 10 if the party has read Dohyuk's letter to Mira): the clay is powering the shoulder. A roll of 18 or higher (13 with the letter) on that check: it is specifically Isolde's power, which would also explain how Huw's pigments work. The party can try to remove the clay quietly. Doing so leaves the shoulder permanently useless.

Encounter Beats
1 Arrival & Tamsin ▼

Let the environment do the initial work -- muted colour, a quiet that's noticeable rather than peaceful, the boundary trees looking almost too vivid in the distance behind the party as they arrive. No dialogue needed yet.

Tamsin is the natural point of entry, since she's the one actually out and working rather than in council. Practical, a little guarded but not hostile, more curious about strangers than suspicious of them specifically. She can mention the elders are nearby without making it feel like a summons.

2 Dagny at the altar ▼

Dagny assumes the party are there to worship and greets them warmly as such. If the party is frank about having relics that came from this shrine, her tone becomes weary quickly -- realising they aren't worshippers, she closes up a little, but not so much that she misses the opportunity to have capable people onside in a trying time. She'll readily reveal that things aren't going well in the area, that the once lush, vibrant land nearby is fading.

On relics specifically, she's honest without being open: the shrine has been, and continues to be, home to a wide array of relics, donations from those who wish to worship in their own way, others curated from various sources. But it's not a museum, and they're not on display.

3 Costin's approach ▼

If the party looks around, Costin tries to speak with them quietly about what relics they have and what they know about them. He doesn't reveal much at first, but gathers information -- unimpressed by how little they understand, but encouraging them to learn more rather than dismissing them outright.

He repositions during the visit so the party needs to pass him on their way out, having overheard what they've discussed with the others. This is where he offers a path forward if Dagny's given them nothing: retrieving or securing the Shrinekeepers' Diary from Otho -- the only known route to unlocking the true basement.

4 Rue's prayer offer ▼

Rue tries to preach the dual harmony of Aeron and Zareth -- that a lack of balance calls for more worship, not less. She invites the party to join her in a short prayer ceremony. Expect the party to decline; that's the intended, correct outcome, not a missed opportunity.

5 Aldous, glimpsed ▼

Aldous is seen early entering the outhouse -- he's taking something into his own small hatch of personal trinkets, unrelated to the true basement. He may emerge at some point but is unlikely to proactively engage. If a sharp-eyed player clocks which door he actually goes through and asks about it later, that's an available thread, not something to force.

6 The real basement, and what's actually needed ▼

Between Dagny and Costin, the party should come away knowing the basement is real, holds more than relics, and is not accessible through anything they currently have. The true entry (not Aldous's mundane hatch) requires the Shrinekeepers' Diary, a huge log kept by successive shrine keepers across hundreds of years, containing the secret method of entry.

The party believes Otho has it. When they go back to see him, the thread picks up at the Threadbare Spine rather than resolving at Everbloom.

7 Return to the Threadbare Spine ▼

The party returns to press Otho on the Diary and finds the shop half collapsed. One side still stands, shelves and stock intact. The other is rubble. Otho is gone and the building is no longer somewhere he can return to. Let the wrongness land before anyone starts theorising.

8 Searching the Spine ▼

The standing side is searchable and the wreckage can be dug through if the party chooses. Stock on the surviving shelves is intact. The Diary is not here, and the party can learn that a person was pulled from the rubble that night and carried to the infirmary.

9 Huw in the infirmary ▼

Asking around Tallowhaven leads to the infirmary. Huw is unconscious. His pack is beside the bed with the Shrinekeepers' Diary and Isolde's Marvellous Pigments in it. Once he is awake, he hands the Diary over readily, even without being asked. The pigments come if the party asks or has built enough rapport.

The shoulder is the thread to leave open. Run the skill ladder on Huw's card if someone examines it. What the party leaves with is the Diary, confirmation the basement is real and dangerous, and a second look at Huw that deepens suspicion of someone who has not lied once.

DM Notes
Don't run all five elders at equal weight.Dagny should get the most airtime; Tamsin and Costin are the two who actually move and act; Rue and Aldous can exist more as background presence the party seeks out if they choose to.
Restraint over exposition.None of the elders should ever say the words “removal without replacement” or explain the theology directly. Let the party infer it from Dagny's grief, Costin's fear and Rue's devotion.
Why Otho reacted.Otho frames the relics he has collected as donations or incidental finds from a scholar with a genuine interest in the divine, which is survivable and deniable. The shrine's own Diary is a different category of object. Nobody donates their own records, and holding it shows systematic, deliberate taking from an active shrine. That is the one truth he cannot have exposed. Keep this behind the scenes. Huw tells it as it happened and nothing in it needs a hidden answer.
Costin's motive stays undecideduntil it is needed at the table: whether he wants the Diary returned to the shrine properly (working within the council) or kept for himself. He will not take part further whatever happens.
Keep the shrine human, not gothic.Washing lines and woodpiles crowd right up to the stone ring. This is a lived-in community in slow decline, not a haunted ruin. The wrecked Spine should read the same way in miniature, a damaged shop rather than a horror set piece.
