Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Bardic Interlude
Cooper's Square
Marek returns — Pen argues back — the party decides what to do
No combat Between second and third missions Marek reappears Seeds Act 2 direction
Environment

Cooper's Square is one of Tallowhaven's older open spaces — wide enough for a market, paved with uneven stone, ringed by the backs of guild buildings and the fronts of modest shops. It takes its name from the barrel-making trade that once operated here. Nobody makes barrels in Cooper's Square anymore but the name has outlasted the reason for it.

At the north end of the square is a low stone plinth — unadorned, unmarked by any official signage — that performers have been using for as long as anyone can remember. Someone at some point called it Sylas's Step and it stuck. Sylas himself has no monument here beyond the fact that people keep standing on it and making music.

The party arrives to find a small crowd gathered around the Step. Morra is among them, watching with the mild pleasure of someone whose afternoon has turned out more interesting than expected.

Sylas's Step The low stone plinth at the north end. Pen is on it. Marek is standing at its base, not on it — he has not taken the stage, he has interrupted someone else's.
The crowd Small but attentive. Festival week has made people more disposed to gather. Some are amused. Some are uncomfortable. A few are genuinely listening to both sides.
Morra Near the edge of the crowd, arms folded, watching with the expression of someone getting more than they paid for. She spots the party and will give them the précis if they approach her.
The timing Late afternoon or a quieter morning — whichever fits the session. The party is not on their way anywhere urgent. They have a moment to stop and watch.

Appearance

Old in the way of someone who has been everywhere and kept very little of what they found there except the experience. Battle-scarred — the face tells stories their mouth does not. Travel-worn clothing in several layers, none of them matching. A flute tucked at the belt that has been repaired so many times the original instrument is mostly memory. Their presence on Sylas's Step has the quality of someone returning to a familiar place rather than performing for the first time.

Their manner
On stageEntirely at home. They have been doing this longer than most of the crowd has been alive. When Marek interrupts they do not step down. They listen, they consider, and they answer from where they are standing.
Their argumentHard-won warmth rather than innocence. They have seen the worst the world offers and still concluded that the balance is positive. When they say look around you, they are speaking from experience, not optimism.
On being argued withThey are not easily rattled. They have had this conversation before in various forms. They find Marek genuinely interesting rather than threatening or annoying. They give him the same consideration they give anyone who stands up in a square and means what they say.
Their response to Marek

Their view of MarekThey think he is probably right about more than they would like him to be. They find his argument harder to dismiss than most. They will say this honestly if asked. They do not think it changes what they do on Sylas's Step.

Why he is here

He passed through Cooper's Square and heard Pen's song — a performance about the persistence of small things, the way people find each other, the way meaning endures. He did not find it false. He found it incomplete. He stopped. He waited for a natural pause. He spoke.

His manner
Not angryHe is never angry. He is precise. He is not attacking Pen. He is adding the part that Pen has not accounted for.
Not performingHe is not trying to win the crowd. He is making an argument he believes is true. Whether anyone listens is not his primary concern.
The missing fingersVisible as always. The party sees them again. The crowd may notice them. Nobody says anything.
His response to Pen
Marek A bird with a broken wing can still fly.

He raises both hands. The linked-thumb gesture — the shape his missing fingers make, the sign the Broken Wing movement has adopted without his blessing. He holds it for a moment so the crowd can see it. Then:

Marek But neither high enough nor far enough to survive for very long.
If the party engages him
On the godsHe does not claim to know what has happened to them. He knows what he feels, which is absence. He has felt it since long before he lost his fingers. The fingers simply gave him something visible to point to.
On CrossroadsIf the party gives him any opening he mentions that even in Crossroads, where four roads meet and no one stays long, people are beginning to ask whether the ground beneath the junction is sound. He says this as a statement of fact, not an invitation. He moves on immediately after.
On the Broken WingIf the party asks about the gesture he acknowledges it is his. He did not name it. He did not organise it. He does not know most of the people who use it. He finds their existence mildly interesting and largely beside the point.
On being arrestedHe does not resist. He does not thank anyone for anything. He goes where he is taken with the same mild patience as before. If it is the party arresting him he looks at them with the expression of someone noting an interesting development and filing it away.
In the gaol — end of Act 1
The conversationHe is already there when the party arrives after the festival collapse. He does not comment on the irony. He waits to see if they have anything worth saying. If they do he tells them about Crossroads with the specificity of someone who has been there recently. He describes what he saw not as a problem to be solved but as a symptom to be understood. He does not ask them to go. He simply describes the world clearly enough that they want to.

Encounter Beats
Purpose of this sceneMarek reappears in a context that shows who he is more fully than the arrest in Brindle Hollow did. The exchange with Pen is the thematic heart of Act 1 stated plainly in a public square. The party chooses how to respond. The arrest that follows positions Marek for the Act 1 finale. The mention of Crossroads plants the seed for Act 2.
01 Arrival — Morra gives the précis ▼

The party arrives in Cooper's Square and finds a small crowd gathered around Sylas's Step. Pen is on the plinth. Marek is at its base. The argument is already underway.

Morra is at the edge of the crowd. She spots the party and gives them her précis without being asked. Some guff about gratitude and small things. More entertaining than the actual song. She does not move from her spot — she wants to see how it ends.

If a player identifies Marek she takes a closer look. She has heard the name.

02 The exchange ▼

The party arrives in time to hear Pen deliver their response to something Marek has already said. Pen is composed, unhurried, speaking from where they stand on the Step rather than descending to argue at Marek's level.

PenYou say this, sir, but look around you. The world still turns. People find life, love, and meaning all over this apparently god-forsaken world.

Marek listens to this. He does not dismiss it. He raises both hands — the linked-thumb gesture, visible to the whole crowd, the shape his missing fingers make — and delivers his response with the quiet certainty of someone stating something they have known for a long time.

MarekA bird with a broken wing can still fly.

A beat. The gesture held so the crowd can see it.

MarekBut neither high enough nor far enough to survive for very long.

The crowd is quiet for a moment. Some of them have seen the gesture before. Some of them are making that connection for the first time. Pen watches him from the Step with the expression of someone who finds the argument harder to dismiss than most.

03 The party decides ▼

The moment after the exchange. The crowd is deciding what to do. Some are drifting away. Some are watching to see what happens next. A civic guard nearby has noticed the disturbance and is moving toward the square without urgency — not a crisis, just something that may require managing.

The party has several options. They can watch and do nothing. They can engage Marek in conversation. They can engage Pen. They can approach Morra. They can arrest Marek themselves. They can leave.

If nobody acts within a minute or so the civic guard arrives and begins the process of moving Marek on. Marek does not resist. He will be taken to the Aldermen's Hall cells if the guard decides the disturbance warrants it, which it does.

04 If the party engages Marek ▼

He receives them with the same patient detachment as before. He answers questions precisely and briefly. He does not perform for them.

If the conversation gives him any opening he mentions Crossroads. He does not linger on it. He describes it as a place where people are beginning to ask the right questions without having arrived at any of the right answers. He moves on immediately after, as if it were not particularly significant. It is significant.

If the party asks about the gesture he confirms it is his. He did not name the movement or organise it. He finds its existence mildly interesting. He does not find it flattering or alarming.

05 The arrest ▼

Whether it is the civic guard or the party themselves, Marek is taken into custody for causing a public disturbance. He does not resist. He walks where he is directed with the mild patience of someone who has done this before and expects to do it again.

If it is the party who arrest him he looks at them with the expression of someone noting an interesting development. He does not comment on the fact that they are the same people who escorted him to the magistrate in Brindle Hollow. He simply files it away.

He is taken to the Aldermen's Hall cells. He will be there when the party arrives after the festival collapse. That is the next time they will see him.

Outcomes and Consequences
Party engages Marek — Crossroads mentioned

The party heard Marek describe Crossroads. They carry it. When the Act 1 finale collapses and they find themselves in the same gaol as him, the conversation picks up where it left off and the direction of Act 2 is established.

Party watches but does not engage

They saw the exchange. They saw the gesture. They saw Marek arrested. When they find him in the gaol after the finale the connection is there even without prior conversation. Marek will make the Crossroads reference in the gaol scene instead.

Party arrests Marek themselves

Marek ends up in the same place either way. The party carrying him there themselves adds a layer to the gaol scene — he has now been arrested by the same people twice. He finds this mildly interesting and will say so in precisely one sentence before moving on.

Party engages Pen

Pen is honest about finding Marek's argument harder to dismiss than most. They have seen the broken wing gesture before. They do not know where it comes from but they know it is spreading. The conversation with Pen gives the party a different angle on the same ideas — warmer, more grounded, more human.

DM Notes
The broken wing gesture in publicThis is the first time the party sees the gesture performed deliberately and publicly by Marek himself. They have seen it on the road, on Harwick's jacket, possibly in passing elsewhere. Here it has context and origin. Do not explain it. Let the party make the connection at whatever speed they make it.
CrossroadsMarek mentions it once, briefly, and moves on. Do not repeat it or emphasise it. Plant it and leave it. The gaol scene at the end of Act 1 is where it develops into something the party can act on.
Pen and MarekNeither of them is wrong. Pen's position is hard-won warmth from someone who has seen difficulty and still finds the balance positive. Marek's position is a structural diagnosis of a world whose conditions for life are quietly degrading. Both are true simultaneously. The party should feel the weight of that without anyone telling them what to conclude.
Morra's readShe thinks Marek is probably making a fair point. Play this as a quiet aside to the party rather than a public declaration. It is a small piece of characterisation — Morra is perceptive and honest and not easily impressed — and it lands differently coming from her than from anyone else in the scene.
The civic guardThey are not antagonists. They are people doing a job. The disturbance is minor. The arrest is routine. They have probably seen Marek before or heard the name. They handle it with the efficient boredom of people who manage public disruption for a living.
Pen after the sceneThey step down from Sylas's Step once Marek is taken. They do not perform again immediately. They stand for a moment looking at the space where he was standing, then tuck their flute away and walk on. If a party member follows them the conversation is available. If not, Pen disappears into the city and the square returns to its ordinary afternoon.
