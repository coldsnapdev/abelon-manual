Ad-Hoc Encounter Reference — Act 1 / After the Real Barricade
The Devrin Pursuit
Devrin killed Arjay overnight and fled with Tobin -- the party catches up at the Halloway checkpoint
Combat encounter — two real threatsAd-hoc — not in the original planPersonal stakes: highDevrin and Tobin both retconned

Devrin Kass silenced the party's tent, killed Arjay, and fled with Tobin overnight. The trail leads back to the Halloway checkpoint, where Yselle -- who knows nothing of what happened -- tries to physically stop Devrin from leaving in such a hurry. Devrin doesn't hesitate. She's a far more capable caster than her day job ever suggested, and when the party closes in, Tobin turns out to be exactly as dangerous in his own way -- a tough, bruising fighter standing between them and her escape.

At a glance
The retcon: Devrin is a genuine spellcaster -- Command and Silence, spell save DC 16 -- not the purely martial Ops lead the checkpoint scene implied. She's been holding that back the entire campaign.
Yselle intercepts: unaware of the murder, she tries to stop Devrin from bolting -- physically, verbally, whatever it takes. She's disciplined enough that Devrin actually has to work to get past her, not just wave a hand.
Silence first: Devrin opens with it on arrival or the instant she's confronted -- drops any caster in the area to somatic-only, herself included. If the party arrives mid-scene, this is the first thing that goes wrong for them.
Command, then movement: used on Yselle specifically to clear the way -- Devrin isn't trying to win a fight with her own colleague, just get past her.
Devrin fights to escape, not to win: against the party proper, every choice she makes is about buying distance, not victory -- she knows what they are and isn't interested in finding out the hard way.
Tobin: not the bystander he looks like -- when it comes to a fight, he's a tough, bruising fighter, and fights like someone who's done this before in a life his craftsman persona never mentioned.
Location Descriptions
The trail

Overnight cold, hurried tracks, the particular quiet of a camp that emptied out fast. Not hard to follow if anyone's trying.

The Halloway checkpoint, before dawn

The same bridge and camp from the checkpoint encounter, thinner-staffed at this hour -- most of the crew asleep or on the far side. Yselle is awake, reviewing paperwork by lamplight, when Devrin comes through fast and wrong.

NPCs
Devrin Kass Halloway Ops lead — secretly Broken Wing, revealed as a real spellcaster
▼
Visual description

The same competent, weary bearing from the checkpoint -- nothing about her posture or dress suggested any of this. That's rather the point.

Goals for this encounter

Get clear. Not interested in a fight with the party and knows she probably loses one -- every action she takes is about creating distance, not winning.

What she knows

Exactly what she did and why -- her theology is sincere, not performed. Corrupted things return to the dirt; they aren't tended. She killed Arjay because she believed that, not out of malice.

What she keeps secret

The spellcasting, until this scene -- nobody at Halloway or in the party had reason to suspect it. Doesn't explain where it comes from unless pressed hard, and even then gives very little away.

Personality points

Calm under pressure in a way that reads as unsettling rather than reassuring. Doesn't raise her voice at Yselle -- says her name once, almost gently, before Command does the rest. If cornered by the party and talking is possible, she doesn't deny what she did or ask forgiveness for it -- she explains it, plainly, the way she'd explain anything else she believed was correct.

Combat

AC 15, HP 68. Spellcasting: WIS 20 (+5), proficiency +3, spell save DC 16, spell attack +8. Dagger +6 (1d4+4 piercing) as a backup strike. Spells (no slots tracked -- use narratively, at will within reason for a single set-piece fight): Silence (2nd, no save, 20ft radius centred on a point she can see, suppresses verbal components -- opens with this), Command (1st, WIS save DC 16, one word: “Flee,” “Drop,” or “Grovel”), Hold Person (2nd, WIS save DC 16), Inflict Wounds (1st, spell attack +8, 3d10 necrotic, touch). Prioritises Silence on arrival, Command on whoever's physically blocking her path, and disengages the moment a clear route opens rather than pressing an advantage.

Yselle Corrin Halloway site manager — intercepts Devrin, knows nothing of the murder
▼
Visual description

Same composed, business-first bearing as the checkpoint -- travelling clothes, a company pin, a ledger still open on the table when Devrin comes through.

Goals for this encounter

Stop a senior colleague from bolting in the middle of the night without an explanation. Not brave in a combat sense -- brave in the much more ordinary sense of standing her ground because it's the professional thing to do.

What she knows

Nothing about Arjay, the murder, or Broken Wing. Only that Devrin is leaving in a way that makes no sense and won't answer why.

Personality points

Genuinely resists -- physically steps into the doorway, raises her voice, asks the obvious questions. Her composure under Command's effect breaks in a way that's uncomfortable to watch rather than played for tension; she doesn't understand what's happening to her.

Combat

AC 11, HP 15. WIS 14, proficient in Wisdom saves (+4 total) -- resists Command roughly a third of the time, which is the entire point of writing her this way rather than as an automatic pushover. No attacks of her own; if physically engaged she can attempt to grapple or block a doorway (Athletics +1) but is not a combatant and shouldn't be played as one.

Tobin Fled with Devrin — a tough, bruising fighter nobody expected
▼
Visual description

The proud craftsman from the checkpoint is still visibly there, but there's a different set to his shoulders now -- the stance of someone who's had to fight before and isn't thinking about it as a choice.

Goals for this encounter

Buy Devrin time to get clear, whatever that costs. Doesn't announce this -- just plants himself between her and whoever's closing fastest.

What he knows

Was there when Devrin killed Arjay, or close enough to know exactly what happened. Hasn't processed it, and fighting is easier than sitting with that right now.

What he keeps secret

Wherever this comes from isn't the checkpoint or the workshop -- he doesn't explain it during the fight, and probably wouldn't explain it cleanly even given the chance.

Personality points

Genuinely torn about Devrin and what she did -- that doesn't go away, it just isn't what's driving his hands right now. Fights hard, fights smart, and doesn't talk while he's doing it. If Devrin goes down or gets clear, his own fight changes shape immediately -- he's not committed to a lost cause for its own sake.

Combat

AC 15, HP 75. Smith's Maul (two-handed) +7 to hit, 2d6+4 bludgeoning. Rage-Forged (Trait): while below half HP, resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical attacks. Relentless (Trait, once per fight): if reduced to 0 HP, drops to 1 HP instead. Fights to protect Devrin's escape, not to win outright -- disengages or breaks off the moment she's clearly away.

Encounter Beats
1 Picking up the trail ▼

The camp Devrin and Tobin left behind is easy to read -- hurried packing, tracks heading back toward the checkpoint rather than away from Halloway's people entirely. A DC 12 Survival or Investigation check confirms the direction with confidence; without it, the party can still follow but loses some time.

2 Arrival -- Yselle already has her stopped ▼

If the party moves quickly, they arrive to find Yselle physically blocking Devrin's path at the checkpoint, demanding an explanation Devrin isn't giving. Tobin is nearby, visibly not okay. This is the moment to let land before anything else happens -- two colleagues, one of whom just became someone else entirely overnight.

3 Silence, then Command ▼

Devrin opens with Silence, centred to cover herself and Yselle (and any party spellcaster who's arrived in time to be caught in it -- don't pull this punch if a player's in range). Says Yselle's name, almost gently, then Command. If it lands, Yselle complies against everything she'd choose for herself, and the image of that should sit with the table for a moment before the scene moves on. If it fails, Devrin doesn't waste a second attempt -- she moves past Yselle physically instead, and the party's arrival becomes the more urgent problem.

4 The fight ▼

Devrin fights to create distance, not to win -- Hold Person on whoever's fastest, Inflict Wounds if cornered, retreating toward terrain that breaks line of sight the moment an opening exists. She disengages the instant a clear route opens rather than pressing any advantage she gets. Tobin is the other half of this fight and a genuine threat in his own right -- he plants himself between the party and Devrin and swings hard, fighting to buy her the seconds she needs rather than to win outright. He doesn't retreat when Devrin does; he breaks off only once she's actually clear.

5 Resolution ▼
Devrin capturedShe doesn't resist further once genuinely cornered, and explains herself plainly if asked -- no theatrics, no remorse performance either way. What happens to her next is a question for the table, not a scripted outcome.
Devrin killedEnds a thread rather than developing it further -- Broken Wing loses a committed believer, and whatever she knew about the wider network goes with her.
Devrin escapesThe manhunt stays open. She's now confirmed as a real spellcasting threat, which changes how the party -- and Halloway -- have to think about finding her again.
Tobin's fateTied to how the fight actually goes now, not a separate track -- he's fighting for real, and what happens to him happens in combat like anyone else here. Where he ends up (down, captured, fled once Devrin's clear) is a genuine outcome of the fight, not a formality decided afterward.

However it ends, Yselle is left with an incident she didn't sign up for and no easy way to process it -- if she survived Command's effect, she remembers exactly what she was made to do and by whom.

DM Notes
The retcon, stated plainlyDevrin was written and played as a purely competent, non-magical Ops lead through the checkpoint encounter. This scene is where that changes -- she's been a real spellcaster the whole time, simply never in a position where it needed to show. Don't foreshadow this before the scene; let it land as a genuine reveal.
Why DC 16 against Yselle specificallyYselle's Wisdom save is +4 -- proficient, not exceptional. At DC 16, Devrin succeeds on Command roughly two times in three. That's deliberate: 'sufficient power to overcome her resistance' means reliably capable, not automatic. Let the roll happen; don't fudge it either direction.
Silence catches everyone in the radiusIf a party spellcaster is close enough when Devrin opens with it, they're affected too -- verbal components stop working for them exactly the same as for Yselle. This is the scene's biggest tactical wrinkle and shouldn't be softened for the party's convenience.
Devrin's theology is sincereShe is not a mustache-twirling villain and shouldn't be played as one. She believes what she did to Arjay was correct, the same way she'd believe anything else she'd reasoned through. That sincerity is what makes her dangerous and what makes the confrontation land.
Tobin is a real fight, not a maybeHe's built as a genuine bruiser -- 2d6+4 a swing, resistance kicking in once he's hurt, and Relentless to survive a killing blow once. Play him as someone who's fought before in a life his craftsman persona never mentioned, not as a reluctant tagalong who happens to throw a punch. His emotional conflict about Devrin is still real; it's just not what's on display while he's swinging a maul.
