Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Trademasters
Honeywell Office & The Basement Warehouse
Oskar's brief, and the family underground he wants cleared out
Social encounter Combat possible Moral weight: high Sent by Morra Four days until delivery

The party travels to Honeywell to find Oskar Sternov, who explains that squatters have moved into one of his warehouses and need clearing before festival stock arrives in four days. He pays 30 gold up front. At the warehouse, the party finds not squatters but a family living underground with quiet dignity, led by a pragmatic elder and a young man running out of patience — and has to work out what "cleared" actually means before reporting back to Oskar for the second half.

At a glance
Part 1 — Honeywell Office
Arrival: a loud steelworks too busy to notice the party. Anyone asked will point them to Oskar.
Oskar's brief: squatters in his warehouse, needs them out before the festival, four days out. Pays 60 gold — 30 before, 30 after.
Oskar's personality: straight to the point, deadline and price both firm, no interest in chat.
Part 2 — The Basement Warehouse
Not squatters: a family living underground with quiet dignity. Dask leads and won't be rushed; Tomas is angry and close to the edge.
The real problem: they won't leave without somewhere to go — Oskar said "clear it," not how.
A child is present. Sem. Nobody comments on it. It just sits there.
Fight trigger: a dismissive tone, an implied threat, or reaching for a weapon too early tips Tomas into a fight he won't back down from.
Reporting back: what actually happened — and who saw it — shapes Oskar's response and the second half of payment.
Location Descriptions
Honeywell — the floor

The ground floor is a working steelworks — heat, hammering, the smell of hot metal. Nobody looks up as you pass through. If you ask, a worker points to a staircase at the back without breaking stride.

Honeywell — the office

Small and functional. Ledgers, supply maps, a desk worn from years of use, and a window looking down over the floor below.

The Basement Warehouse

A staircase from street level leads down. You hear low voices and movement before you see anything — the talking stops the moment your footsteps become audible. The space below is larger than it sounds: bedrolls arranged with care, food stored neatly along one wall, tools organised in an alcove. A separate room in the lower right is shut. The only light is what you've brought with you. It doesn't look squatted. It looks lived in.

NPCs
Oskar Sternov Trademasters — direct, practical, no ceremony
▼
Visual description

Worked his way up rather than inherited the position. Practical clothing, broad hands, the look of twenty years in this business and twenty more ahead of him.

Goals for this encounter

Get the warehouse cleared before the festival deadline. Not interested in how, only that it's done and doesn't come back to him.

What he knows

The deadline is real — first delivery locked in, materials already on a cart. Doesn't know who's occupying the warehouse or how long they've been there.

What he keeps secret

Hasn't considered what happens to whoever's down there afterward — genuinely hasn't occurred to him, not guilt he's hiding.

Personality points

No small talk. Trusts Morra's name to open the door. Proportional and direct in response to however the job actually goes — doesn't moralise, doesn't celebrate.

Dask The elder — pragmatic, clear-eyed
▼
Visual description

Weathered, unhurried, watchful — someone who has navigated precarity long enough that it no longer surprises him.

Goals for this encounter

Protect his people, especially Marta, and find any way out of this that doesn't end badly. Reads the party's intent the moment they arrive.

What he knows

Exactly why the party is there before anyone says a word. Knows the group has nowhere else to go if they leave without a plan.

What he keeps secret

Nothing calculated — he's simply not going to argue his case. He asks harder questions instead: "Where do you suggest we go?" Genuinely wants an answer.

Personality points

Cooperative in tone, immovable in practice. Doesn't escalate — observes, and decides what's still possible. If Tomas tips into a fight, says his name once, quietly, then moves to get Marta clear. Afterward, reappears in the doorway and looks at what happened without a word. The look is enough.

Tomas The young one — angry, committed, dangerous
▼
Visual description

Young, lean, wound tight — the kind of energy that hasn't yet learned to tell conviction from combustion.

Goals for this encounter

Use the fact that the warehouse is suddenly valuable as leverage. Frames it as principle — it's partly principle.

What he knows

That the space has become valuable to Oskar, and that this is the only leverage his people have.

What he keeps secret

Nothing hidden — he's been failed by enough things that he's stopped looking for a reason not to be angry, and it shows.

Personality points

Pushes back harder and less productively than Dask. Trigger point: a dismissive tone, an implied threat, someone reaching for a weapon early, being spoken over. Any of these can tip him. Once committed, he doesn't stop — the party has to choose to pull their blows, because he won't give them that choice.

Combat

Commoner, elevated HP. Unarmed or improvised weapon, no armour. Disadvantage on checks requiring calm; advantage on attack rolls while above half HP. Fights to kill, does not yield. If subdued: goes down hard, doesn't thank anyone.

Marta The old woman — Dask's priority
▼
Visual description

Small, unhurried, with the particular stillness of someone who has survived enough difficulty that this one doesn't frighten her.

Goals for this encounter

None of her own beyond staying with Dask. Not passive out of weakness — she's made her peace with how things go.

What she knows

Probably the most honest voice in the room about what the situation actually is, if the party asks her directly.

What she keeps secret

Nothing — answers plainly, without sentimentality, and doesn't ask for sympathy.

Personality points

When things escalate, Dask moves to her first and she goes without protest, without looking back.

Supporting cast Fetch, Pol, Anya & Sem, Vorik, Liss
▼
Fetch

Middle-aged, practical — has followed Tomas's anger before and paid for it. Puts a hand on Tomas's arm, gets shrugged off, then follows Dask instead. Not a coward; he's done the arithmetic.

Pol

Young, unfinished, stays because Tomas stays. Frightened, won't show it. If Tomas goes down, uses Dodge rather than continuing to fight. Standard commoner, improvised weapon, breaks if Tomas falls.

Anya & Sem

Mother and child, peripheral witnesses. Anya doesn't engage unless spoken to; if things escalate she pulls Sem behind her or covers his eyes. Sem's presence is never commented on by anyone in the room — it simply sits there.

Vorik & Liss

An old man who hasn't moved since the party arrived, and someone who was asleep and is now watching everything with their knees drawn up. Neither escalates nor de-escalates. Just present, which is its own kind of weight.

Encounter Beats
— Before you begin ▼

Oskar paid half upfront. The job is to clear the space without a fuss and without bodies. Those two requirements are already in tension, and the party will feel that before anyone says a word about it.

01 Through the floor, into the office ▼

The party enters through the noise and heat of the steelworks floor. Nobody stops what they're doing to greet them; a worker points to the stairs if asked. Let this beat breathe a little — the sensory contrast with the rest of Tallowhaven does more to establish the Trademasters than exposition would.

Oskar's at his desk when they arrive, and wastes no time once he hears Morra sent them. He lays out the job in two sentences: squatters in the south warehouse, needs them gone before festival stock arrives in four days. Thirty gold now, thirty on completion. He asks if there are questions, more from habit than real interest — and pays the first half before they leave.

02 The approach ▼

The party finds the warehouse without difficulty and descends the staircase. Before they reach the bottom they hear voices — low conversation, the sound of movement — which stop the moment footsteps become audible.

The space opens up below: bedrolls, food, tools, a home made underground with quiet determination. Sem's bedroll is smaller than the rest — the party will notice before they can decide whether to. Dask is already watching them from across the room. He knows exactly why they're here.

03 The conversation, and the real problem ▼

Dask is cooperative in tone and immovable in practice. He doesn't argue — he asks one question: "Where do you suggest we go?" Tomas is less measured, pushing the leverage he thinks the warehouse's sudden value gives him. The crack between the two men is immediate and visible, and the peripheral characters are present throughout — Anya keeping Sem close, Vorik watching, Liss drawn up against the wall.

The workers won't leave without somewhere to go or something in return, and that's arithmetic, not stubbornness — festival prices have doubled and every room in the city is taken. Oskar said clear the space. He didn't say how. That gap is where the moral weight of the scene lives.

04 The decision point ▼

The party's options, roughly: go back to Oskar and negotiate day labour on the workers' behalf (may work, solves everyone's problem); find somewhere else in the city for them (needs legwork and local knowledge they may not have yet); apply pressure (works on Tomas faster than Dask, and feels worse for it); or tell them the deadline and leave, technically not the party's problem but guaranteeing the same standoff in four days. Reward any angle a player finds that isn't listed here.

05 If it tips — Tomas escalates ▼

Only happens if the party's handling tips Tomas from frustrated to committed — a dismissive tone, an implied threat, reaching for a weapon too early, being spoken over. Watch for the trigger and let it land naturally rather than forcing it.

Fetch tries Tomas's arm, gets shrugged off, then follows Dask instead. Dask says Tomas's name once, quietly, knowing it won't work, then crosses to move Marta toward the locked room. Anya pulls Sem close; Vorik and Liss stay put and watch. The fight is Tomas and Pol against the party, in a low-ceilinged room with no space to spread out — Tomas knows every corner, the party doesn't. He won't yield; the party has to decide for themselves whether they're pulling their blows. Pol breaks if Tomas goes down. Dask reappears in the doorway at some point during or after, looks at what happened without expression, and says nothing. The look is enough.

06 Outcomes, and reporting back to Oskar ▼
Best — workers placed, space clearedThe party brokered something: day labour through Oskar, a different location, an angle he didn't suggest. Everyone leaves with something; Oskar is quietly impressed. He agrees to the arrangement without ceremony if the numbers work, and pays the second half. Mentions more work is available, slightly more warmly than he expected to.
Clean — workers leave under pressureThe group disperses without a fight. Job done technically. Dask's expression on the way out costs the party something they can't quantify. Oskar pays without ceremony; the job is done, more work available if they want it. He doesn't ask what happened.
Messy — fight, Tomas and Pol subduedBoth go down but survive; Dask takes the injured with him. Oskar pays, expression careful — he notes that the south district has eyes during festival preparation and this draws exactly the attention he asked them to avoid. He doesn't end the relationship, just notes the problem once and moves on.
Bad — Tomas or Pol killedOskar pays because a deal is a deal, then goes quiet. He needed the space cleared, not a body to explain — he has people to talk to, and his tone suggests whether there's more work is no longer certain. A magistrate may become involved depending on witnesses; Caen Rhys may hear about it too.
Failure — party refuses the jobOskar extends the deadline once. A second failure ends the Trademaster relationship cleanly. The party keeps the advance.
Unexpected — party sides with the workers against OskarThe Trademaster path closes. The Courtesan path becomes significantly more interesting — Seva will hear about it.

If the party returns asking Oskar to negotiate on the workers' behalf directly: he listens without interrupting, does the arithmetic out loud — festival labour is short, a few people who can move materials and don't require guild rates isn't the worst solution — and may agree, making clear it's a one-time arrangement tied to the festival, not a permanent fix.

DM Notes
Oskar vs AlricThe contrast is deliberate. Alric charms over breakfast; Oskar transacts in two sentences. Same goal, different worlds — let the party notice without it being pointed out to them.
Sem's bedrollNever draw attention to it. Let the party find it, and when they do, don't narrate their characters' reactions — let the players sit with it themselves.
The festival created the conflictThe workers didn't know the warehouse would be needed when they moved in; Oskar didn't know it was occupied until recently. Nobody made a bad decision to get here — the ground shifted under everyone. Worth internalising before you run this.
Tomas is not a villainHe's a young man who's run out of road and found the party standing in his way at the wrong moment. His anger is legitimate even if his target isn't. The fight, if it happens, should feel like something that didn't have to happen — not something that was always going to.
Oskar doesn't know about the witnessesAnya, Sem, Vorik, and Liss aren't known to him. If a fight happens and the party doesn't mention them, Oskar never learns a child was present underground for it — that's the party's information to carry or not.
