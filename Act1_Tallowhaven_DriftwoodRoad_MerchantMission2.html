Mission Reference — Act 1 / Merchants / Second Mission
The Driftwood Point Supply Problem
Investigate the disruption, find the bottleneck, clear the road
Investigation then combatLevel 3 partyCombat all but certainHidden Trademaster connection

Alric asks the party to find out why merchants trading with Driftwood Point haven't received their goods. Three merchant interviews point to a checkpoint on the road that's been blocking traffic for ten days under a thin legal cover, run by a crew who feel oddly confident about being challenged. The party investigates, then fights their way through the checkpoint -- and comes away with one loose thread (the operation was hired "through channels") that won't resolve until much later in the campaign.

At a glance
Part 1 — Investigation
The brief: Alric names three affected merchants and pays half upfront. He doesn't tell the party where to look beyond that.
What the merchants add up to: a checkpoint on the road, running ten days, blocking both directions, using a landowner's name (Cadog Melyn) as cover, run by someone who feels sanctioned from somewhere
Anwen: a courier who paid the toll rather than turn back -- the best source of tactical detail if the party finds her
The hidden layer: Trademaster-connected. Not discoverable this mission, no matter what the party does -- surfaces much later.
Part 2 — The Road
The mirror: this toll crew are capable people who took a job that pays well and asks few questions -- exactly what the party has been doing
Radek: the leader, genuinely enjoys the power more than the legal argument -- his tell is landing one extra hit he didn't need to
Escalation: starts as a transaction (Peck handles it), becomes an argument (Radek steps in), becomes a fight with backstops closing from both ends of the bottleneck
The thread: if questioned, Thessaly may mention the job came "through channels" -- the party can't follow it yet, but they carry it
Location Descriptions
The road to Driftwood Point

A natural bottleneck -- bridge, ford, forest track, or rocky defile depending on the map. A cart waits, paperwork changes hands, and it looks like a checkpoint rather than an ambush until it isn't.

The Establishment (referenced)

Not visited this mission, but the checkpoint's operation and paperwork make clear it's run with real organisation, not opportunism.

NPCs
Mared Ffion Silvermark Jeweller — composed, precise, gives the Anwen lead
▼
Personality points

Polite because the party comes from Alric, but the politeness has an edge -- precise rather than warm. States facts without embellishment; they're damning enough on their own.

What she knows

Rare coastal gems, expected ten days ago, not arrived. A Driftwood Point courier (Anwen) got through last week with someone else's goods and mentioned a checkpoint -- Mared didn't follow up herself, but tells the party now.

Goals for this encounter

Wants results, and is watching to see if the party is worth her time.

Osip Baran Butcher — direct, frustrated, seeds doubt about Alric
▼
Personality points

Not angry at the party, angry at the situation -- blunt and loud, warms up quickly if taken seriously.

What he knows

Deliveries stopped about ten days ago -- he's been counting, which pins the timeline. Recalls a previous northern supply route Alric championed that went nowhere.

What he keeps secret

Not accusing Alric of dishonesty -- just optimism that costs other people money. Line: “Not dishonest. Just optimistic in ways that cost other people money.”

Lande & Caeryn Fabled Furnitures — both directions blocked
▼
Lande

An elf furniture maker, quietly furious rather than panicking -- more unsettling than Osip's bluntness. Assesses the party before deciding how much to share. Both her outgoing shipment and an incoming one are blocked; she produces a ledger with Cadog Melyn's name written down from something overheard.

Caeryn

Lande's husband, the courier who was turned back. More openly annoyed, less guarded than Lande. Describes the checkpoint's location, people, and paperwork -- the man running it engaged with his arguments, then made clear the arguments weren't the point.

Anwen Driftwood Point courier — paid the toll, killing time before heading back
▼
Visual description

Found at lunch or dinner at a specific establishment if the party follows the lead and asks the right vendor or innkeeper.

Personality points

Guarded at first, warms up once she understands the party wants to fix the problem, not assign blame. Dark humour of someone who's had a genuinely bad week. Line, mid-vent: “That, plus the fact that every tradey in this town is looking at me like Sekarth himself, I'd say this has been a trip to quickly forget.”

What she knows

Checkpoint location, roughly how many people, the paperwork, Cadog Melyn's name (independently corroborating Lande's ledger). Almost as an afterthought: the leader seemed unworried about being challenged -- confidence from somewhere that makes things hard to contest. She can't say more; the party carries it.

Radek & Peck The checkpoint — leader and negotiator
▼
Radek (leader)

Genuinely enjoys having power over people at a bottleneck more than he cares about the legal argument, though he uses it. Engages seriously with counter-arguments, which is unsettling. Tell: lands one extra hit he didn't need to, early, before the fight even starts.

Combat -- Radek

AC 16, HP 52. Greataxe +6, 1d12+4 slashing (extra attack on a crit). Action Surge (1/short rest), Second Wind (1d10+4 as bonus action, 1/short rest). Intimidation +5, used actively -- failed save gives the target disadvantage on their next attack against him. Fights with enjoyment, not efficiency; last to go down, not gracefully.

Peck (negotiator)

Quick-witted, faintly absurdist gnome bard who delivers the toll demand with practiced comic timing, then steps back the moment combat starts -- not cowardice, professionalism. Exit line: something theatrical about preferring not to get blood on the ledger. If Radek falls and Peck's still standing, he puts his hands up with professional detachment -- no loyalty beyond the job.

Combat -- Peck

AC 13, HP 22. Vicious Mockery (DC 13), Dissonant Whispers (DC 13), Healing Word on self/allies, Bardic Inspiration (1d6, 1/short rest). Steps back from melee, uses Vicious Mockery from range if directly threatened -- priority is survival, not victory.

Morda, Brek & Gort The checkpoint — backstops and muscle
▼
Morda (Tallowhaven-side backstop)

Half-orc ranger, appears behind the party once they're committed. AC 14, HP 32. Shortsword +5 (1d6+3) / Longbow +5 (1d8+3, 150/600ft). Hunter's Mark, Colossus Slayer (+1d8 vs a target below max HP).

Brek (Driftwood-side backstop)

Older dwarf ranger holding the coast-facing end, clear professional shorthand with Morda. AC 15, HP 38. Handaxe +4 (1d6+2) / Longbow +4 (1d8+2, 150/600ft). Hunter's Mark, Dwarven Resilience.

Gort

Orc fighter standing with Radek, entirely reliable, no interest in the argument -- just the work. AC 15, HP 38. Longsword +5 (1d8+3). Relentless Endurance (drops to 1 HP instead of 0, 1/long rest), Second Wind.

Thessaly The checkpoint — magic user, held back, complicated feelings
▼
Personality points

Set back or concealed, may go unnoticed until a spell lands. Not cruel like Radek -- just here, doing a job, trying not to think too hard about it.

What she keeps secret

If questioned after the fight, most likely of the group to mention the job coming through channels rather than directly -- not the Trademaster connection itself, just that thread. The party can't follow it yet.

Combat

AC 12, HP 26. Thunderwave (DC 13, 2d8, pushes 10ft), Hold Person (DC 13), Misty Step (bonus action, 30ft), Magic Missile (3 darts, 1d4+1 each, auto-hit).

Encounter Beats
I1 Alric's brief ▼

Alric describes it as a matter of guild reputation -- merchants who trusted him are losing money in the most visible trading week of the year. He names three merchants (Mared, Osip, Lande), mentions as an aside that Lande had issues going the other way too, pays half upfront, and leaves the legwork to the party.

I2 The merchant conversations ▼

Visitable in any order. Mared gives the Anwen lead; Osip gives the ten-day timeline and doubt about Alric; Lande and Caeryn give both-directions confirmation, Cadog Melyn's name, and a ground-level account of the checkpoint. By the end, the party knows there's a bottleneck, running ten days, blocking both ways, with grey legal cover and a leader who feels protected.

I3 Finding Anwen ▼

A successful conversation with a vendor or innkeeper who deals with Driftwood Point traders locates her at a meal, killing time before heading back. She fills in tactical detail -- headcount, paperwork, the leader's manner -- and independently corroborates Cadog Melyn's name. The detail about the leader's confidence comes almost as an afterthought; let it sit without drawing attention to it.

C1 Arrival at the checkpoint ▼

It looks like a checkpoint, not an ambush -- a cart waiting, Peck visible and official-looking. Morda is behind the party before they realise it (DC 14 Perception to spot early); Brek is ahead past the bottleneck; Thessaly isn't immediately obvious (DC 16). A prepared party gets the advantage of noticing.

C2 The transaction, then the argument ▼

Peck explains the toll with practiced ease -- Cadog Melyn's name, a maintenance fee, entirely standard-sounding. If the party challenges the legal basis, he handles it pleasantly; if they push harder, Radek enters and engages with the argument genuinely, which is unsettling. At some point -- ideally sparked by an impatient waiting carter -- he does something one beat more than necessary. Let that land before anything else happens.

C3 The fight ▼
Full investigation, then road clearedAlric receives the report with measured satisfaction -- notes they found the full picture without saying he already knew part of it. Pays the second half. The party carries Thessaly's comment about channels.
Partial investigation, then road clearedHarder fight, same clear road. Alric pays the second half and notes they were efficient -- without saying what he noticed about what they missed.
Party pays the toll and turns backRoad stays blocked, supply line not restored. Alric is disappointed in a specific, legible way, finds another solution, and won't use the party for anything requiring nerve for a while.

Morda and Brek close from both ends. Thessaly's first spell lands from an unexpected angle, especially if the Perception checks failed. Gort moves with Radek; Peck steps aside and finds somewhere to be. Radek uses Action Surge when challenged or losing, talks throughout, and is the last down -- not gracefully.

DM Notes
The hidden Trademaster layerThe toll operation was commissioned through Trademaster-connected channels. Not discoverable this mission -- no documents, names, or conversations point that way. Thessaly's “channels” comment is the only thread, and it leads nowhere yet. It surfaces in Act 1's third round, once the factions are already in motion.
Osip's line about Alric“Not dishonest. Just optimistic in ways that cost other people money.” Doesn't need resolving -- it's a legitimate perspective from someone with a real grievance. Let it sit.
Radek's extra punchPlay it once, early, ideally before combat starts -- one beat more than necessary directed at a bystander or waiting carter. It establishes who he is before the fight, so the combat feels like a continuation rather than a surprise.
Peck's exitMake it funny and human -- a professional stepping out of a situation that's moved past his job description. One good line, well timed, then out of the way.
The seeded detailA waiter mentioned a delivery issue from the coast before this mission was even offered. A party that connected the dots at dinner earns the satisfaction of having noticed early.
