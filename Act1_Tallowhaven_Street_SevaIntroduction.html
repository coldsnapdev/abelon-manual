Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Courtesans Network
A Street in Tallowhaven
Seva finds the party — a chance encounter that is not a chance encounter
Social encounter No combat Brief and unhurried Leads to Crost household
How This Encounter Happens

The previous evening at the Amber Cup, Seva may or may not have had a pleasant conversation with the party. If she did, she ended it warmly and without making any move. She said she would find them tomorrow. If the party never engaged her, she simply left at some point unremarked. Either way she has already decided they are worth the effort.

The following morning she finds them in the street. It looks like coincidence. A familiar face from the road, a warm greeting, a natural pause in the flow of a busy Tallowhaven morning. She has a small favour to ask. She is unhurried about it.

This is not a recruitment. It is an evaluation dressed as a favour. She does not tell them who she is or what she represents. She is simply a well-connected woman who needs something retrieved and thinks they might be the right people to do it.

Environment

A street in Tallowhaven, mid-morning. Festival preparation visible in the background — workers moving materials, temporary structures going up, the city adjusting itself around an approaching event. The encounter is brief and conducted on the move or standing to one side. Nothing about it looks like a job interview.

Appearance

Travel clothes, slightly finer than the occasion requires. Composed and unhurried. She greets the party as if running into them is a pleasant surprise, which it may even be, partly. She is good at this.

Her manner
The greetingWarm, natural. She remembers details from any previous conversation. If they never spoke she references the road with the easy familiarity of someone who noticed them without making a thing of it.
The favourShe gets to it without preamble. A necklace left at a private engagement she attended some days ago. Silver, distinctive. She would retrieve it herself but the timing is awkward. She would rather not revisit the household directly. She gives them the Crost address and pays half upfront.
What she does not sayThe nature of the engagement. Why the household is complicated. What she is actually assessing. None of this is hidden exactly. It simply is not offered.
How she leavesShe thanks them, says she will find them when they are done, and continues on her way. The whole encounter takes perhaps five minutes. It does not look like anything from the outside.
Encounter Beats
01 The encounter ▼

Seva appears on the street. A familiar face. She greets them warmly and falls into step or pauses to talk. The morning continues around them — festival workers, traders, the ordinary business of a busy city. Nothing about this looks significant.

She mentions the necklace briefly and practically. A private engagement, a household she would rather not revisit, would they be able to collect it on her behalf. She gives them the address. She pays half upfront, drawn from somewhere without ceremony.

02 Questions ▼

If the party asks about the engagement she is economical. A private matter. She attended as a guest. She left the necklace behind and would prefer not to make more of retrieving it than it needs to be.

If they ask who she is or what she does she describes herself simply as someone who finds it useful to know the right people. She says it without mystery, as if it is the most ordinary thing in the world, which to her it is.

She does not volunteer more than is asked. She is not evasive. She simply answers the question that was asked and no other.

03 The departure ▼

She thanks them, says she will find them when they are done, and continues on her way. She does not look back. The encounter is over in the time it takes to have a brief conversation on a busy street.

The party is left standing with half a fee and an address. Nothing about what just happened feels like the beginning of something. That is exactly how she intended it.

DM Notes
Keep it briefThis encounter should feel small. A familiar face, a quick favour, a few coins, an address. The weight of it only becomes apparent later when the Crost household turns out to be more complicated than a simple retrieval. Do not play it as a significant moment. Let the players discover that it was one.
She will find themShe says this and she means it. She does not give them a time or a place. She simply knows where they will be. When they have completed the Crost job she finds them again — possibly at the Amber Cup, possibly in the street, possibly somewhere that makes them wonder how she knew. That is the second encounter. This is just the beginning.
The name SableIf the party asks her surname this is the first time they hear it. Sable. If they later encounter other members of the network and hear the same surname, the penny will drop without anyone explaining it. Let that happen at its own pace.
If they recognised her from the roadShe receives the recognition warmly. Of course she remembers them. She was there. She does not address the question of what she was doing there unless directly pressed, and even then she is economical. She was travelling with someone. Things became complicated. She found her own way to Tallowhaven. All of this is technically true.
