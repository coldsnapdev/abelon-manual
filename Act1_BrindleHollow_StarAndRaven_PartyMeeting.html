Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Brindle Hollow
The Star and Raven
Session one opening — the party meets and receives the job
Social encounter No combat Session Zero / One opening Job offered by Morfudd Pryce
Environment

The Star and Raven is the kind of tavern that has been keeping people warm for a long time without making a point of it. Low ceilings, heavy beams darkened by years of smoke, stone walls and worn wooden floors. Candles and a single fireplace provide most of the light. It smells of woodsmoke and something cooking in the back.

It is a rainy evening. The windows are small and the rain streaks them. The room is not full but it is occupied — a handful of regulars, a traveller, two dwarves having a drink after work, and an elf in the corner who appears to be doing well at cards.

This is where the party finds itself at the start of the campaign. Nobody is heroic yet. The world is simply in front of them.

The bar Where Nesta works. The natural place for newcomers to orient themselves. She will have sized the party up before they reach it.
The fire Warmth and light. A natural gathering point on a wet evening. Coll is nearby.
Seren's corner The elf with the cards. A table slightly apart from the others. A game of Crowns and Crows in progress. Players who investigate will find someone who has been here longer than most.
The dwarves Borek and Yael, after a shift. They are not plot relevant. They are texture. The world has always had people like them in it.
Rain outside Nobody is going anywhere in a hurry. The weather gives the scene permission to breathe.
Crowns and Crows The card game Seren is playing. A bluffing game — players declare the value of their hand and others decide whether to believe them. If a player joins, run it as Insight vs Seren's Deception. Seren has been playing for longer than anyone at the table has been alive.

Mid-forties, hair pulled back practically, an apron that has seen better days. The face of someone who has seen everything a small village can produce and remained fond of it anyway. She notices the party before they notice her.

Role in the scene
First contactThe party will likely approach her first. She is welcoming without being effusive. She can answer questions about Brindle Hollow, about the other patrons, and about the general state of things.
The jobShe knows Morfudd Pryce is looking for capable people. She will mention it if the party seems at a loose end, or if directly asked about work. She does not oversell it.
What she knowsEverything that has happened in Brindle Hollow for the last twenty years. She will not volunteer it. She will answer questions honestly and without embellishment.

portrait
Morfudd Pryce Aeronist priest — the employer
▼
Appearance

Middle aged, thin, slightly officious. Well kept dark robes with subtle green and blue embroidery. Receding hair, a neatly trimmed beard, and the expression of a man whose seeding ceremony was ruined by a one-handed paladin making a point about a goat. He introduces himself with his full name.

The situation
What happenedMorfudd organised a seeding ceremony at the Stillwater Shrine — a ritual sacrifice of an animal in the pool in preparation for new growth. Marek Talan disrupted it non-violently but sufficiently to ruin the day. Morfudd made a citizen's arrest.
The jobEscort Marek to Tallowhaven to face the magistrate. Morfudd cannot do it himself — he has the shrine to manage and Marek needs capable hands to make sure he arrives. He will pay on commission of the delivery.
His mannerPompous rather than rude. He considers himself a person of standing in Brindle Hollow and is accustomed to being taken seriously. He is slightly embarrassed to be seeking help in a tavern but has run out of better options.
On MarekHe describes Marek as a troublemaker and a disruptor. His account is accurate in its facts and uncharitable in its framing. He genuinely believes Marek was wrong.

Seren Elf — Crowns and Crows, corner table

Appearance

Quietly androgynous, ageless in the way that makes it impossible to guess whether they are two hundred or five hundred. Fine features, unhurried expression, a hand of cards visible at the edge of the frame. The faint suggestion of someone who has been winning for longer than anyone at the table has been alive.

Role in the scene
OptionalSeren does not approach the party. They are available if the party investigates. A player who sits down at the card table gets a brief encounter with someone ancient, perceptive, and entirely untroubled by strangers.
If a player winsSeren is delighted rather than annoyed. Someone interesting finally sat down. The conversation that follows can go anywhere you want it to.
If Seren winsThey accept the outcome with quiet grace and offer to buy the loser a drink. Still opens the same conversation.
What they knowSeren has been in Brindle Hollow for a long time. They know about the shrine, about Morfudd, and about the general rhythm of the place. They will not volunteer much but will answer questions thoughtfully.

Encounter Beats
Purpose of this sceneThe party meets. The world is established. A job is offered. This scene should breathe. Do not rush toward the job. Let the players find their feet, talk to people, and make the room feel real before the plot starts moving.
01 Arrival ▼

The party arrives at the Star and Raven on a wet evening. The room is warm. Nesta is behind the bar. The fire is going. The scene is set.

Let players introduce their characters to each other. Do not force it. Give them things to interact with — the bar, the fire, the dwarves, Seren's card game — and let the introductions happen naturally.

02 The world in miniature ▼

This beat is purely optional engagement. Players who talk to Nesta get a warm, practical read on Brindle Hollow. Players who talk to Coll get a local's perspective. Players who talk to Reed get a glimpse of Tallowhaven before they arrive there. Players who approach Seren get something older and harder to read.

None of this is required. All of it rewards curiosity. The world is here if they want it.

03 Morfudd arrives ▼

Morfudd Pryce enters and introduces himself with his full name. He finds the party — either through Nesta's recommendation or by identifying them as capable-looking strangers — and explains the situation.

He describes Marek's disruption of the seeding ceremony, his citizen's arrest, and the need to escort Marek to Tallowhaven to face the magistrate. He is slightly embarrassed to be doing this in a tavern but keeps his dignity intact.

He pays on completion of the delivery. He cannot accompany them himself. He needs capable hands.

04 Questions and negotiation ▼

The party will have questions. Morfudd answers them honestly and with his characteristic slight pomposity. He describes Marek as a troublemaker. His account is accurate in its facts.

If the party negotiates payment upward they will find him resistant but not immovable. He is spending his own money and feels the pinch of it. A persuasive case gets a small concession.

If the party asks what Marek actually did, Morfudd's account is telling. He describes a man who made arguments rather than threats, who disrupted rather than damaged, and who left when asked. The party may form their own conclusions about whether this required an arrest.

05 The job is accepted ▼

Morfudd tells them Marek is being held at the Stillwater Shrine, which is where they will begin in the morning. He leaves with the manner of a man who has solved a problem and would like not to think about it further.

The rest of the evening is the party's. The room is still warm. The rain is still outside. Seren is still at the card table.

DM Notes
Session one toneThis scene sets the register for everything that follows. Keep it grounded, unhurried, and human. Nobody is special yet. The world is simply in front of them and they are deciding what to do with it.
Crowns and CrowsIf players want to actually play the card game with Seren, run it as a series of Insight vs Deception contested rolls. Seren has a high Deception modifier reflecting centuries of practice. Winning is genuinely satisfying. Losing is graceful. Either way Seren buys a drink and conversation opens.
Morfudd's pomposityPlay it straight. He is not a villain and he is not comic relief. He is a man of modest local standing whose ceremony was disrupted and who believes he is entirely in the right. He probably is, technically. That is part of what makes Marek interesting.
Seeding awayNothing in this scene needs to be seeded toward Act 2 or Act 3. The world is established simply by being itself. Trust that.
