Ad-Hoc Encounter Reference — Act 1 / After the Real Barricade
The Devrin Pursuit
Devrin killed Arjay overnight and fled with Tobin -- the party catches up at the Halloway checkpoint
Combat encounter — two real threatsAd-hoc — not in the original planPersonal stakes: highDevrin and Tobin both retconned

Devrin Kass silenced the party's tent, then used a single Command spell on Yselle to order the entire Halloway crew to pack up and move out through the night -- clearing the camp so she and Tobin could kill Arjay unseen. They fled after, leaving a Broken Wing sigil and a marker pointing the way, an open invitation to be followed. The party picks up that trail. It leads away from the checkpoint entirely, to wherever Devrin's actually gone to ground -- and when the party catches up, she's a far more capable caster than her day job ever suggested, with Tobin standing beside her as exactly the kind of fighter nobody expected either.

At a glance
The retcon: Devrin is a genuine spellcaster -- Command and Silence, spell save DC 16 -- not the purely martial Ops lead the checkpoint scene implied. She's been holding that back the entire campaign.
What already happened: the Command-on-Yselle moment is resolved backstory, not something to restage -- one spell, the night of the murder, ordering the whole crew out so Devrin and Tobin could work unseen. State it plainly; don't re-run it as a live scene.
The trail: a Broken Wing sigil and a marker, left deliberately -- Devrin wants to be found, or doesn't care if she is. It leads away from the checkpoint entirely.
Silence first: Devrin opens with it the instant she's confronted -- drops any caster in the area to somatic-only, herself included.
Devrin fights to escape, not to win: every choice she makes is about buying distance, not victory -- she knows what the party is and isn't interested in finding out the hard way.
Tobin: not the bystander he looks like -- when it comes to a fight, he's a tough, bruising fighter, and fights like someone who's done this before in a life his craftsman persona never mentioned.
Location Descriptions
The trail

A Broken Wing sigil scratched into the dirt and a rough marker pointing a direction -- deliberate, not an accident. The tracks beyond it are easy enough to read: two people moving fast, no longer trying to hide it.

Where the trail ends

Off the road, away from the checkpoint and Halloway's people entirely -- a clearing, a ruined structure, or a stretch of wilderness that gives Devrin defensible ground and a line of retreat. She chose it deliberately.

NPCs
Devrin Kass Halloway Ops lead — secretly Broken Wing, revealed as a real spellcaster
▼
Visual description

The same competent, weary bearing from the checkpoint -- nothing about her posture or dress suggested any of this. That's rather the point.

Goals for this encounter

Get clear. Not interested in a fight with the party and knows she probably loses one -- every action she takes is about creating distance, not winning.

What she knows

Exactly what she did and why -- her theology is sincere, not performed. Corrupted things return to the dirt; they aren't tended. She killed Arjay because she believed that, not out of malice.

What she keeps secret

The spellcasting, until this scene -- nobody at Halloway or in the party had reason to suspect it. Doesn't explain where it comes from unless pressed hard, and even then gives very little away.

Personality points

Calm under pressure in a way that reads as unsettling rather than reassuring -- the same quality that let her give a single quiet order and empty a camp without anyone questioning it. If cornered by the party and talking is possible, she doesn't deny what she did or ask forgiveness for it -- she explains it, plainly, the way she'd explain anything else she believed was correct.

Combat

AC 15, HP 68. Spellcasting: WIS 20 (+5), proficiency +3, spell save DC 16, spell attack +8. Dagger +6 (1d4+4 piercing) as a backup strike. Spells (no slots tracked -- use narratively, at will within reason for a single set-piece fight): Silence (2nd, no save, 20ft radius centred on a point she can see, suppresses verbal components -- opens with this), Command (1st, WIS save DC 16, one word: “Flee,” “Drop,” or “Grovel”), Hold Person (2nd, WIS save DC 16), Inflict Wounds (1st, spell attack +8, 3d10 necrotic, touch). Prioritises Silence on arrival, Command on whoever's physically blocking her path, and disengages the moment a clear route opens rather than pressing an advantage.

Tobin Fled with Devrin — a tough, bruising fighter nobody expected
▼
Visual description

The proud craftsman from the checkpoint is still visibly there, but there's a different set to his shoulders now -- the stance of someone who's had to fight before and isn't thinking about it as a choice.

Goals for this encounter

Buy Devrin time to get clear, whatever that costs. Doesn't announce this -- just plants himself between her and whoever's closing fastest.

What he knows

Was there when Devrin killed Arjay, or close enough to know exactly what happened. Hasn't processed it, and fighting is easier than sitting with that right now.

What he keeps secret

Wherever this comes from isn't the checkpoint or the workshop -- he doesn't explain it during the fight, and probably wouldn't explain it cleanly even given the chance.

Personality points

Genuinely torn about Devrin and what she did -- that doesn't go away, it just isn't what's driving his hands right now. Fights hard, fights smart, and doesn't talk while he's doing it. If Devrin goes down or gets clear, his own fight changes shape immediately -- he's not committed to a lost cause for its own sake.

Combat

AC 15, HP 75. Smith's Maul (two-handed) +7 to hit, 2d6+4 bludgeoning. Rage-Forged (Trait): while below half HP, resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical attacks. Relentless (Trait, once per fight): if reduced to 0 HP, drops to 1 HP instead. Fights to protect Devrin's escape, not to win outright -- disengages or breaks off the moment she's clearly away.

Encounter Beats
1 What actually happened, before the party ever wakes up ▼

This is backstory to state plainly, not a scene to run: Devrin cast Silence on the party's tent, then used a single Command spell on Yselle to order the entire Halloway crew to pack up and move out through the night -- clearing the camp so she and Tobin could kill Arjay unseen. It worked. By the time the party wakes, everyone but their own tent is gone, and Arjay is dead. Don't restage the Command moment as something the party witnesses; it's already happened.

2 Picking up the trail ▼

The sigil and marker are deliberate -- Devrin isn't hiding that direction is the point. A DC 12 Survival or Investigation check confirms the trail with confidence and cuts travel time; without it, the party can still follow, just slower. The tracks lead away from the checkpoint and Halloway's people entirely, toward wherever Devrin's actually chosen to make her stand.

3 Arrival ▼

Devrin and Tobin are waiting, not hiding -- she chose this ground deliberately, with a line of retreat in mind. There's no confrontation to talk through here; she doesn't try to explain herself or negotiate. The moment the party's presence is clear, she opens the fight on her own terms.

4 The fight ▼

Devrin opens with Silence, centred to cover herself and any party spellcaster close enough to be caught in it -- don't pull this punch. From there she fights to create distance, not to win: Hold Person on whoever's fastest, Inflict Wounds if cornered, retreating toward terrain that breaks line of sight the moment an opening exists. She disengages the instant a clear route opens rather than pressing any advantage she gets. Tobin is the other half of this fight and a genuine threat in his own right -- he plants himself between the party and Devrin and swings hard, fighting to buy her the seconds she needs rather than to win outright. He doesn't retreat when Devrin does; he breaks off only once she's actually clear.

5 Resolution ▼
Devrin capturedShe doesn't resist further once genuinely cornered, and explains herself plainly if asked -- no theatrics, no remorse performance either way. What happens to her next is a question for the table, not a scripted outcome.
Devrin killedEnds a thread rather than developing it further -- Broken Wing loses a committed believer, and whatever she knew about the wider network goes with her.
Devrin escapesThe manhunt stays open. She's now confirmed as a real spellcasting threat, which changes how the party -- and Halloway -- have to think about finding her again.
Tobin's fateTied to how the fight actually goes now, not a separate track -- he's fighting for real, and what happens to him happens in combat like anyone else here. Where he ends up (down, captured, fled once Devrin's clear) is a genuine outcome of the fight, not a formality decided afterward.

However it ends, this is the confirmed end of the immediate manhunt thread -- what's left is what the party does with the outcome, not a further chase.

DM Notes
The retcon, stated plainlyDevrin was written and played as a purely competent, non-magical Ops lead through the checkpoint encounter. This scene is where that changes -- she's been a real spellcaster the whole time, simply never in a position where it needed to show. Don't foreshadow this before the fight; let it land as a genuine reveal.
The Command-on-Yselle moment is backstory, not a sceneIt already happened, the night of the murder -- one spell, ordering the crew out, done. Don't restage it as something the party watches happen live. If the party asks how the camp emptied out, that's the answer, delivered as established fact, not as a fight to referee.
Why DC 16Spell save DC 16 is what a Command powerful enough to move an entire disciplined crew's site manager actually requires -- reliably capable, not a guaranteed success, but not a coin flip either. That's the standard this whole retcon is built to hit.
Silence catches everyone in the radiusIf a party spellcaster is close enough when Devrin opens with it, they're affected too -- verbal components stop working for them exactly the same as it did for Yselle that first night. This is the scene's biggest tactical wrinkle and shouldn't be softened for the party's convenience.
Devrin's theology is sincereShe is not a mustache-twirling villain and shouldn't be played as one. She believes what she did to Arjay was correct, the same way she'd believe anything else she'd reasoned through. That sincerity is what makes her dangerous and what makes the confrontation land.
Tobin is a real fight, not a maybeHe's built as a genuine bruiser -- 2d6+4 a swing, resistance kicking in once he's hurt, and Relentless to survive a killing blow once. Play him as someone who's fought before in a life his craftsman persona never mentioned, not as a reluctant tagalong who happens to throw a punch. His emotional conflict about Devrin is still real; it's just not what's on display while he's swinging a maul.
