Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Trademasters' Guild
The Basement Warehouse
Clear the space — four days, no fuss, no bodies — underground
Social encounter Combat possible Moral weight: high Four days until delivery Claustrophobic setting
⚔ Combat Guide
Environment

A warehouse on the south side of Tallowhaven's working district, accessed via a staircase from street level. The basement below is where the family have made their home. The party descends to find them. They can hear voices before they arrive — low conversation, the sound of movement in a confined space. They choose whether to announce themselves or observe first.

The basement is larger than it sounds. Someone has arranged it with quiet determination — bedrolls placed thoughtfully, food stored neatly along the top wall, tools organised in the alcove to the left. A separated room in the lower right is locked or pulled shut. The space does not look squatted. It looks lived in, underground, by people who have made the best of a difficult situation.

The darkness is not total but it is present. The only light comes from what they have brought with them. Everything feels closer than it would above ground.

The descent The staircase from street level. The party hears the family before they see them. A natural moment to pause and choose how to enter.
The bedrolls Several, arranged tidily in the main space. A smaller one among them. The party will notice it. Do not draw attention to it.
The food area Along the top wall. Organised and carefully maintained. Evidence of a community managing scarcity with dignity.
The tool alcove Left side. Tools that belong to working people. The sum total of their professional lives stacked in a corner underground.
The locked room Lower right, separated from the main space. Where Marta sleeps, or where the family keeps what little they consider valuable. Dask will move Marta here when things escalate.
The claustrophobia If the fight happens it happens in a low-ceilinged underground room with no room to spread out. The party cannot move freely. Tomas knows the space. They do not.

Dask The elder — pragmatic, clear-eyed
▼
Appearance

A man who has been navigating precarity long enough that it no longer surprises him. Weathered, unhurried, watchful. He reads the party the moment they walk in and knows exactly why they are there before they speak.

Personality
Default modeCooperative in tone, immovable in practice. He does not argue. He asks questions that are harder to answer than arguments would be.
Under pressureHe does not escalate. He observes. He is deciding what is possible and what is not, and acting accordingly.
When Tomas escalatesHe says Tomas's name once, quietly, knowing it will not work. Then he moves toward Marta. He has chosen which battle he can still affect.
AfterwardsHe reappears in the doorway after the fight. He looks at what happened without expression. He does not say anything accusatory. The look is enough.
His one question
"Where do you suggest we go?"

Not rhetorical. He genuinely wants to know if the party has an answer. If they do not, that is the problem in the room.

Tomas The young one — angry, committed, dangerous
▼
Appearance

Young, lean, the kind of tightly wound energy that has not yet learned to distinguish between conviction and combustion. He has been failed by enough things that he has stopped looking for a reason not to be angry.

Personality
Default modePushes back harder and less productively than Dask. His anger is closer to the surface and less controlled.
The leverage playHe has worked out that the warehouses are suddenly valuable. He wants to use that. He frames it as principle. It is partly principle.
Trigger pointA dismissive tone. An implied threat. Someone reaching for a weapon before the conversation is finished. Being spoken over or ignored. Any of these can tip him.
When committedHe does not stop. He will fight until he cannot. The party must choose to pull their blows. He will not give them that choice himself.
Combat — if it comes to that
Stat blockCommoner with elevated HP. Unarmed or improvised weapon. No armour. Disadvantage on checks requiring calm. Advantage on attack rolls while above half HP.
BehaviourFights to kill. Does not yield. The party must decide what that means for them.
If subduedGoes down hard. Does not thank anyone. Dask's expression when he sees the outcome is the moral weight of the scene landing.

Encounter Beats
Before you beginOskar paid them half upfront. The job is to clear the space without a fuss and without bodies. Those two requirements are already in tension. The party will feel that before anyone says a word about it.
01 The approach ▼

The party locates the warehouse without difficulty. From street level they find the staircase down. Before they reach the bottom they can hear voices — low conversation, the sound of movement. The talking stops when footsteps on the stairs become audible.

They descend into the basement. The space opens up. Bedrolls, food, tools — a home made underground with quiet determination. Sem's bedroll is smaller than the rest. The party will notice before they can decide whether to.

Dask is already watching them from across the room when they arrive at the bottom of the stairs. He knows why they are here.

02 The conversation begins ▼

Dask is cooperative in tone and immovable in practice. He does not argue. He asks one question.

"Where do you suggest we go?"

Tomas is less measured. He pushes back harder and less productively. He has already worked out that the warehouses are suddenly needed and he wants to use that leverage. Dask thinks it will make things worse. The crack between them is immediate and visible.

The peripheral characters are present throughout. Anya keeps Sem close. Vorik watches. Liss has drawn their knees up.

03 The real problem surfaces ▼

The workers will not leave without somewhere to go or something in return. That is not stubbornness. It is arithmetic. Leaving means sleeping outside in a town where every room is taken and festival prices have doubled.

This is when the party must decide what completing the job actually means. Oskar said clear the space. He did not say how. The gap between those two things is where the moral weight lives.

04 The decision point ▼

The party's options:

Go back to Oskar and negotiate on the workers' behalf. He may have day labour available for festival logistics. That solves everyone's problem at once. He may not.

Find somewhere else in the city for the workers to go. Requires legwork and local knowledge they may not yet have.

Apply pressure. Works on Tomas faster than Dask and feels worse because of it.

Tell them the deadline and leave. Technically not the party's problem. Means coming back to the same situation in four days.

Reward the unexpected. If a player finds an angle not listed here, make it work.

05 Tomas escalates — the fight path ▼
Fight path — only if triggered This beat only happens if the party handles the encounter in a way that tips Tomas from frustrated to committed. A dismissive tone, an implied threat, reaching for a weapon too early, being spoken over. Watch for the trigger and let it land naturally rather than forcing it.

Fetch puts a hand on Tomas's arm. Says his name. Gets shrugged off. Then follows Dask.

Dask says Tomas's name once, quietly, knowing it will not work. Then he crosses to Marta and moves her toward the locked room. Anya pulls Sem close or covers his eyes. Vorik watches. Liss stays against the wall.

Tomas is committed now. Pol stays because Tomas stays. The fight is Tomas and Pol against the party, in a low-ceilinged underground room with no room to spread out. Tomas knows every corner. The party does not.

Tomas does not yield. The party must decide — are they pulling their blows? He will not give them that choice himself. Pol breaks if Tomas goes down.

Dask reappears in the doorway at some point during or after. He looks at what happened without expression. He does not say anything accusatory. The look is enough.

Likely Outcomes
Best outcome — workers placed, space cleared

The party brokered something. Day labour through Oskar, a different location, a solution Oskar did not suggest. Everyone leaves with something. Oskar is quietly impressed by the angle they found.

Clean outcome — workers leave under pressure

The party applied enough pressure that the group disperses without a fight. Job done technically. Dask's expression on the way out costs the party something they cannot quantify. Oskar pays without ceremony.

Messy outcome — fight, Tomas and Pol subdued

The party pulled their blows. Both go down but survive. Dask takes the injured with him. Oskar is unhappy about the mess but pays. Warns them once about visibility. Caen Rhys may hear about it depending on witnesses.

Bad outcome — Tomas or Pol killed

Oskar's tone changes entirely. He pays because a deal is a deal but the nature of the relationship is different now. The magistrate may become involved depending on witnesses. A child was in the room. That will not leave certain players quickly.

Failure — party refuses the job

Oskar extends the deadline once. Second failure ends the Trademaster relationship cleanly. The party keeps the advance.

Unexpected — party sides with the workers against Oskar

The Trademaster path closes. The Courtesan path becomes significantly more interesting. Seva will hear about it.

Oskar — Responses
Key principleOskar is a practical man. He is not interested in how they feel about what happened. He is interested in whether the space is clear and whether any of it will come back to him. His responses are proportional and direct. He does not moralisise and he does not celebrate.
Space cleared, workers placed — creative solution ▼

He listens. If the numbers work he agrees to the day labour arrangement without making a production of it. Pays the second half. May mention there is more work if they want it, slightly more warmly than he expected to say it.

Space cleared, workers dispersed ▼

Pays the second half without ceremony. The job is done. More work is available if they want it. He does not ask what happened.

Space cleared, fight happened, no deaths ▼

He pays. His expression is careful. He tells them the south district has eyes during festival preparation and that this kind of thing draws exactly the attention he asked them to avoid. He does not end the relationship. He notes the problem once and moves on.

Someone died ▼

He pays because a deal is a deal. Then he is quiet for a moment. He tells them he needed the space cleared, not a body to explain. He has people to talk to. He will be in touch about whether there is more work. His tone suggests the answer to that question is not certain.

Party returns asking to negotiate on workers' behalf ▼

He listens without interrupting. He does the arithmetic visibly — festival labour is short, the warehouses need to be clear, a few people who can move materials and do not require guild rates is not the worst solution. He may agree. If he does, he makes clear this is a one-time arrangement tied to the festival, not a permanent fix. He is not their benefactor. He is a practical man who found a workable number.

Empty handed — first visit ▼

He does not raise his voice. He extends the deadline once. He tells them what four days actually means for his operation — the first delivery is locked in, the materials are already on a cart somewhere, the festival schedule does not move. He makes clear there will not be a third conversation.

DM Notes
Sem's bedrollNever draw attention to it. Let the party find it. The moment they do, do not narrate their characters' reactions. Let the players sit with it.
The festival created the conflictThe workers did not know the warehouses would be needed when they moved in. Oskar did not know they were occupied until recently. Nobody made a bad decision to get here. The ground shifted under everyone. This is worth understanding before you run the scene.
Dask's lookIf the fight happens, Dask reappears in the doorway at some point and looks at what occurred without expression. Do not describe what his look means. Do not have him say anything that names it. Just describe the look and move on. The players will do the rest.
Tomas is not a villainHe is a young man who has run out of road and found the party standing in his way at the wrong moment. His anger is legitimate even if his target is not. The fight, if it happens, should feel like something that did not have to happen rather than something that was always going to.
Oskar does not know about the witnessesThe peripheral characters — Anya, Sem, Vorik, Liss — are not known to Oskar. If the fight happens and the party does not mention them, Oskar does not know a child was present underground in a room where the party fought two men. That is the party's information to carry or not.
