Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Merchants
The Shallow Tuber
Breakfast with Alric Venn — the Merchant thread opens
Social encounter No combat Job offered and paid upfront Leads to Copper Vine
Environment

The Shallow Tuber is a respectable Tallowhaven eating establishment — the kind of place where guild business gets done over food without anyone calling it a meeting. Clean tablecloths, unhurried service, a menu that changes with the season. It is not grand but it is the right kind of comfortable. The sort of place Alric uses for exactly this purpose.

Morning light through the windows. A handful of other diners conducting their own quiet business. The room has a low murmur that makes private conversation possible without effort. Alric is already seated when the party arrives. He has ordered food. There is enough for everyone.

Alric Venn Merchants' Guildmaster — systems thinker, fair payer
▼
Appearance

Fifties, well dressed without ostentation. The kind of wealth that does not need to announce itself. Calm, considered face. The bearing of someone who thinks in systems rather than people. He is always noting something, though he does not always show it.

His situation
The paymentHe owes a levy to the Aldermen's Hall from the festival fundraiser. He has negotiated grace from Caen Rhys pending collection from a debtor. The money flows through him rather than to him — he is a conduit for a civic obligation, not a man enriching himself.
The debtorCaspar Veld at the Copper Vine. Known for requiring chasing. Alric is not angry about it. It is simply how Caspar operates and someone has to deal with it.
Why the partyHe noticed them at the Aldermen's Hall. Capable strangers delivering a prisoner without drama. He is a good judge of people and he is provisionally interested in what he saw. This breakfast is the rest of his assessment.
His manner at breakfast
Questions firstHe does not pitch immediately. He asks about where they are from, what brought them to Tallowhaven, what they made of the road. He listens. He is building a picture.
The offerWhen he does make it he frames it practically. A collection that has been outstanding too long. A known debtor who requires a personal visit. He describes it as routine. He is not lying about this — it is routine. The moral texture is not his concern to flag.
Payment upfrontHe pays before they leave the table. He considers prompt fair payment a point of personal honour. A deal is a deal. He expects the same in return.
PomposityPresent but not aggressive. He considers himself a person of standing and he is not wrong. He is slightly posh in the way of someone who is richer than most people around him and has stopped noticing it. The party will probably find him slightly irritating. That is appropriate.
What he does not say
The moral weightHe does not mention that Caspar may resist, that the party will have no real leverage beyond implied threat, or that he is watching how they handle it. He describes the job as it appears on the surface. The texture is for the party to find.

Huw Alric's aide — present, says little

Role

Sits slightly back from the table. Has documents. Does not speak unless Alric addresses him directly. He is watching the party with the careful attention of someone who has been told that watching carefully is part of his job. If the party speaks to him directly he is polite, slightly stiff, and refers anything substantive back to Alric.

Encounter Beats
01 Arrival — Alric is already seated ▼

Alric rises briefly when the party arrives, gestures to seats, invites them to eat. The food is already there. He has ordered for everyone without asking what they wanted, which is either considerate or presumptuous depending on your disposition. The food is good.

Huw sits slightly back. He has a cup of something and a stack of papers he is pretending to read.

02 The questions ▼

Alric asks questions that feel like small talk. Where are they from. How they found the escort work. What they thought of Tallowhaven on the way in. He listens to the answers with the attentiveness of someone building a file rather than making conversation.

He is not cold about this. The interest is genuine. He simply has a purpose behind it that he does not announce.

03 The offer ▼

When he is satisfied with what he has heard he makes the offer. Practical, unhurried, no drama. The Merchants' Guild collects levies from traders who operate under its banner at sanctioned events. One trader — Caspar Veld at the Copper Vine — has an outstanding balance from the recent market. He has been chased by letter. He requires a personal visit.

He explains that the money flows through him to the city. He is not enriching himself. He is managing a civic obligation that is overdue because one man keeps finding reasons to delay.

He describes Caspar as a known rascal who often requires chasing. He says this without heat, as a fact. He is not wrong.

04 Payment upfront ▼

He pays before anyone leaves the table. He takes a small purse from Huw and sets it down without ceremony. He does not make a production of it. A deal is a deal and he pays his side of it first.

This is the moment the moral texture shifts slightly. The party now has money they have not yet earned. Walking away from the job means keeping it or returning it to a man who will not take it back.

Alric does not frame it this way. He simply pays and moves on.

05 The end of breakfast ▼

Alric finishes his food, thanks them for coming, and leaves with Huw. He does not linger. He has other business and he assumes they do too now.

He expects to hear from them when the matter is resolved. He does not specify how. He will find out.

DM Notes
Alric's pomposityPlay it as a texture rather than a character flaw. He is slightly posh, slightly presumptuous about ordering for the table, slightly accustomed to being the most important person in the room. None of this makes him a villain. It makes him slightly irritating in a way that is entirely human.
The upfront paymentThis is the most important mechanical moment in the scene. The party now has a commitment with consequences. Do not draw attention to it beyond Alric setting the purse down. The weight of it lands later at the Copper Vine when Caspar asks what they are going to do about it.
What Alric is assessingHe spent the first half of breakfast deciding whether they were worth the job. He spent the second half watching how they received it. Did they ask the right questions. Did they push back on anything. Did they take the money without blinking. All of it is information. He will have an opinion about them before the Copper Vine encounter is over, whether he hears from them or not.
The chain of obligationAlric owes the Aldermen's Hall. Caspar owes Alric. The party is the mechanism by which that chain completes. Nobody in that chain is simply wrong. The party will feel the weight of being a link in it when they are standing in front of Caspar's bottles.
