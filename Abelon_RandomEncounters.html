Random Encounters — Act 1 Tallowhaven

Context-specific encounter tables for idle moments, travel, and overnight rests
How to useRoll when the party camps, rests, idles, or moves without purpose. Match the circumstance to the correct table. Roll a d10. Even a result of nothing is worth the theatre of the dice. The suspense is part of it.
NotesThe Vaelseer appears once, in the tavern table. The dream appears once, in the inn table. Neither should be repeated if they have already occurred. The dog that adopts the party during city movement can persist across subsequent scenes if the moment lands well.
Sleeping at an Inn Overnight, indoors — the Star and Raven or Amber Cup
d10 ▼
1A rat is somewhere in the room. It never shows itself but can be heard periodically moving behind the wainscoting. It is gone by morning.
2The room has a slow leak. Not dramatic — a single drip, hitting the same spot on the floor every few seconds. It does this all night. The floor is fine. The sound is not.
3Someone in an adjacent room is snoring at a volume that is genuinely impressive. It stops at some point. Nobody can say when.
4A cat has gotten into the room somehow and is sitting on one character's chest when they wake. It leaves when the door is opened, unhurried and unashamed.
5The fire goes out in the night. The room is noticeably cold by morning. Nobody is harmed. Everyone is slightly irritable.
6A drunk festival-goer tries the wrong door in the small hours, rattles it, apologises loudly to nobody, and moves on down the corridor.
7One character wakes briefly to voices in the street below — two people, unhurried, speaking in a language nobody in the party recognises. It resolves itself. They go back to sleep.
8Something small moves under one of the beds. Investigation finds nothing. The sound does not return.
9One character dreams vividly — not a vision, just a strange one. A door they have seen before, somewhere it should not be. They wake with the image already fading.
10Nothing happens. Everyone sleeps well. One character wakes in an inexplicably good mood and cannot explain why.

Camping Outdoors Night, exposed — the road to Tallowhaven or the road into exile
d10 ▼
1Rain begins around the second watch. Not violent — persistent, cold, the kind that finds its way into everything. It stops before dawn but the damage is done.
2An animal investigates the camp during the night. Something medium-sized, moving carefully at the edge of the firelight. It retreats when noticed and does not return.
3One character on watch sees a light in the distance — a single point, moving slowly, then stopping, then gone. No explanation presents itself.
4A fox sits just outside the firelight for an extended period, watching the camp with complete calm. When it eventually leaves it does so without urgency.
5Someone's rations have been got at. Not a rat — the damage suggests something larger and more determined. The food is still edible, mostly.
6The fire dies earlier than it should. By the time anyone notices the coals are barely warm. The wood was dry. There is no obvious reason.
7Insects. Not dangerously, not dramatically, but persistently and specifically around one character for the entire night. No bites. Just the sound.
8Any animals with the party become briefly agitated in the small hours — not panicked, just alert, ears up, watching the same direction. They settle after a few minutes.
9One character on watch smells something they cannot identify. Not threatening — just present. Faintly sweet, slightly wrong. Gone within the hour.
10A clear night, unexpected warmth for the time of year, no disturbances. Whoever is on watch sees more stars than usual. They are not sure that is possible. It is a good night.

Idle in a Tavern Inside, drinks in hand, between things
d10 ▼
1A Vaelseer is sitting alone at a corner table. They are looking at nothing in particular. If any player makes eye contact, they hold it for a moment too long before looking away. They leave without speaking to anyone.
2Two patrons at a nearby table are playing cards and the game has clearly been going for a long time. One of them is winning by an amount that should have ended the evening an hour ago. The loser keeps reaching into their coat.
3A waiter catches a glass before it falls — an almost impossible catch, fluid and unhurried — then continues as if nothing happened. Nobody else in the room reacts.
4A musician in the corner finishes a song, pauses, then begins the same song again. And again. Nobody complains. Nobody seems to notice except the party.
5The fire in the hearth produces an unusual colour for several minutes — not dramatically, just slightly off. Greenish. It stops. The fire is normal again.
6A patron at a nearby table falls asleep mid-conversation with their companion. The companion finishes their thought anyway, drains their cup, and quietly leaves.
7A child who should not be in this establishment at this hour appears briefly in a doorway, clocks the room, and disappears before any member of staff notices.
8The innkeeper quietly cuts off a patron who does not appear to be drunk. The patron accepts without argument, pays, and leaves. The innkeeper watches them go for a moment longer than necessary.
9Someone leaves a folded piece of paper on the table next to the party's and walks out before anyone can get a good look at them. It is not addressed to anyone. It is not clear whether it was left deliberately or forgotten.
10Nothing of note happens. The drinks are good. The fire is warm. For an hour, Tallowhaven asks nothing of anyone.

Moving Through the City Streets, squares, travelling between locations in Tallowhaven
d10 ▼
1A festival performer's trained crow lands on one character's shoulder and refuses to leave for several minutes. Its owner eventually retrieves it, apologetic but privately impressed.
2Two merchants are having a heated argument directly in the party's path and show no signs of moving. The argument is about a debt. Or possibly a cheese. It is hard to tell.
3A child runs directly into one character at full speed, bounces off, looks up, and runs away without a word. The character notices their coin purse has been moved — not taken, just moved to the wrong pocket.
4A dog adopts the party. It follows them from this point with quiet determination. It belongs to no one visible. It will not leave.
5It begins to rain heavily and without warning. Anyone not under cover is immediately soaked. It stops ten minutes later as if it never started.
6A wizard's spell misfires somewhere nearby — nothing dangerous, but a flock of pigeons is briefly and completely bright orange before returning to normal. Nobody else seems to find this remarkable.
7An elderly woman stops one specific character, studies their face for a moment, says something short and declarative — "I thought so" or "there it is" — and walks away without elaborating.
8A guild notice pinned to a board catches the eye of one character. It contains a name they recognise in a context that does not quite make sense. By the time they look for it again it is covered by another notice.
9A street vendor is selling something the party cannot identify. It smells extraordinary. The vendor cannot or will not explain what it is. It costs one copper. It tastes like nothing they have encountered before.
10The street the party is on goes briefly and inexplicably quiet — all background noise dropping away for perhaps ten seconds before returning to normal. Nobody else reacts.

At the Market or a Shop Commercial spaces — browsing or buying
d10 ▼
1The shopkeeper gives the wrong change and insists, politely but firmly, that they are correct. They are not correct. They are also not going to change their position.
2Another customer is examining the same item as one party member and reaches for it at exactly the same moment. They are perfectly civil about it. It is nonetheless a standoff.
3A street child is loitering just outside the shop entrance with the focused non-expression of someone who is definitely not planning to steal anything.
4Something in the back of the shop makes a sound that the shopkeeper ignores completely. It is not a small sound. They do not acknowledge it was made.
5A delivery arrives mid-transaction — several crates, carried by a single person who is smaller than the crates and moving with the calm of someone who has done this many times. The shopkeeper waves the party aside without apology.
6One item on the shelf is priced significantly lower than everything around it. There is no obvious reason. When asked, the shopkeeper looks at it as if seeing it for the first time and changes the price.
7A regular customer enters, exchanges a long and apparently meaningful look with the shopkeeper, and leaves without buying anything or saying a word. The shopkeeper's manner is slightly different afterwards.
8The shopkeeper's assistant knocks something over in a back room. There is a pause. Then the assistant appears in the doorway, makes eye contact with the shopkeeper, and retreats without speaking.
9One character notices an item that is almost certainly not legal to sell openly. It is on the shelf between two completely mundane objects. The shopkeeper seems unaware of it, or is performing unawareness extremely well.
10A cat is asleep on the counter and is clearly considered the highest-ranking presence in the establishment. The shopkeeper works around it without comment.
