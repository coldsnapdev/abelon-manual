Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / The Finale
The Festival Meeting & Finale
The signing — the collapse — Cooper's Square — the arrest
Act 1 climaxCombat — finaleLevel 3 partyThree faction pathsLeads to exile

The trade signing collapses the moment someone in the room accuses the party of exactly what they did the night before -- and whichever faction they stood with turns the argument into open conflict. The Driftwood delegation walks out for good. The party fights their way through their chosen faction's crew in Cooper's Square, the same square as the Bardic Interlude, until Caen Rhys arrives with no army behind him except thirty frightened witnesses -- and ends Act 1 by naming what happened and deciding what comes next.

At a glance
Structure: three parts -- the Meeting (accusation), Cooper's Square (the faction fight), the Arrest (Caen Rhys ends it). Don't roleplay the meeting itself: set the scene, then skip to the accusation.
Which accusation fires: depends on who the party sided with at the Evening Invitations, and whether that faction's rival was killed or warned beforehand at the Assassination Paths -- six possible combinations, each with its own accuser and wording
The pattern: whichever accusation lands, it's specific and true -- the party really did do the thing being described. There's no clean defence, only a response.
The fight: three escalating waves per faction (mooks, then a lieutenant, then the leader), the leader hanging back until losses force their hand, and attempting to flee if left standing alone
The end: Caen Rhys arrives with three unarmed civic staff and a frightened crowd -- no army, just legitimacy -- and offers exile with provisions rather than a trial
Location Descriptions
Aldermen's Hall, the signing room

The formal back chamber, long table, blue-floored and well appointed. Alric at one end, Aldous Crane at the other, Caen Rhys presiding from the side. Adjoining: an empty waiting room (an exit route during the collapse), a kitchen annex where Seva's Sables have been operating as staff, an anteroom with the emergency exit Caen Rhys uses, and a food-prep area staging the rest of each faction's people.

Cooper's Square, after dark

The same square as the Bardic Interlude -- Sylas's Step at the north end, open at both ends so it channels rather than contains. The faction that hired the party knows exactly where they'll be.

NPCs
Caen Rhys Magistrate — highest civil authority in Tallowhaven
▼
Visual description

Arrives from the civic quarter on foot with Pren and Tev a step behind, Oswin further back as the only armed person present, hand nowhere near his weapon.

Goals for this encounter

Name what happened and remove the party from Tallowhaven before three damaged factions and a dead trade deal need somewhere to put their anger.

What he knows

Has tracked the shape of the party's involvement all week from behind his own courtroom desk. Not surprised by any of this.

Personality points

Completely, actually calm -- the decision was made before he walked into the square. Three lines, no pause between them: “Are you going to kill all of them too?” ... “I think you've made your point.” ... “Come with me. Before this gets worse.” Starts walking after the third line and expects them to follow. His authority is legitimacy and thirty witnesses, not force.

If Seva was killed beforehand (Path 1): Alric is present as normal, and Elbeth watches from the far side of the square without fighting. If Alric was warned (Path 4): standard fight, no change.

If Oskar was killed beforehand (Path 5): Elbeth leads the Courtesans as acting head and arrives as the wave-3 leader instead of Seva; Cors does not step sideways here, grieving in his own way. If Seva was warned (Path 2): standard fight, Elbeth leads wave 1 as normal.

Encounter Beats
1 The meeting, then the accusation ▼

Don't roleplay the negotiation. Set the scene -- Alric and Aldous Crane at the table, Caen Rhys presiding, the party visibly standing with their chosen faction -- then skip straight to the moment it turns. Every other faction representative has been recalculating since the party walked in; the accusation comes when the accuser finds the perfect opening, and should feel inevitable rather than sudden.

If the party sided with Alric, Oskar accuses them of dismantling the Driftwood Road on Alric's coin -- “Who are you here for?” If they sided with Oskar, Seva accuses them of silencing a true story at The Establishment -- “Was it worth it?” If they sided with Seva, Alric accuses them of engineering the crisis they then resolved for the Courtesans -- “What exactly is your connection to Seva Sable?”

If a leader was killed or warned beforehand at the Assassination Paths, a replacement accuser fires instead: Elbeth (suspicion, no evidence, if Seva was killed), Huw (an overheard whisper to Caen, if Alric was killed), or Cors (quiet and personal, if Oskar was killed) -- each messier and less clean than the standard three, which is the point.

2 The room reacts ▼

It doesn't matter what the party says in response -- the damage is done. Aldous Crane watches just long enough to confirm his assessment of Tallowhaven, then stands and leaves with his delegation, permanently. The Sables filter out quietly through the kitchen annex before the argument fully escalates. By the time Caen Rhys acts, they're gone.

As he moves to intervene, the faction that hired the party turns on them, quietly, in a single line each: Alric -- “You have cost me everything I have spent a year building. I will remember that.” Oskar -- “I knew what you were. I gave you the benefit of the doubt. That was my mistake.” Seva, barely audible -- “Leave Tallowhaven tonight. If you are still here tomorrow, that becomes my problem to solve.” Caen Rhys leads the party out through the emergency exit: “Keep moving. I will find you before the night is out.”

3 Cooper's Square ▼

The door closes on an ordinary evening -- for a moment, nothing happens. Then the faction the party sided with arrives, in three escalating waves: mooks first (several break or flee under pressure), then a lieutenant with real teeth (Huw, Cors and Drest, or Mira), then the faction leader themselves once their people have taken real losses. All three leaders hang back until forced to commit, and all three attempt to flee if left standing alone -- whether the party lets them go carries weight into Act 2.

4 The arrest ▼

Caen Rhys arrives on foot with Pren and Tev a step behind and Oswin further back -- the only armed person present, hand nowhere near his weapon. He doesn't need more than that; the fight is already over, and a frightened crowd of thirty or forty ordinary Tallowhaven residents watches from the edges. He walks to the centre, looks at what's happened, and says only what's necessary: “I am not going to ask what happened here. I am going to tell you what happens next. You will come with me. You will spend the night in the cells. And in the morning you will leave Tallowhaven. Whatever you were doing in this city is finished.” He doesn't wait for a response.

DM Notes
Aldous Crane's exitHe never reacts to the accusation directly -- he watches, and when he's seen enough, stands and leaves without a word. His departure should feel heavier than anything actually said in the room; the deal is dead the moment he moves toward the door.
Cors Holdworn's dual loyaltyIn the teal path he fights for Oskar; in the pink path he steps toward the Sables in wave 2, a repositioning most parties will miss entirely. Don't draw attention to it -- if a player notices and asks later, it's a thread for Act 2.
Replacement leaders, if a target was killedElbeth leading the Courtesans is angrier and less composed than Seva would have been. Caen Rhys holding the Merchant seat (if Alric died) is not a guild member, and the Driftwood delegation notices. Anneth Holdworn leading the Trademasters (if Oskar died) is patient and controlled -- quieter than Oskar, which is almost worse.
The escape attemptIf a faction leader flees and the party lets them go, leave it deliberately loose. They're still out there, and they know exactly what was done and by whom -- that's Act 2's problem, not this scene's.
Caen Rhys has no armyThree unarmed civic staff and a frightened crowd. His authority is entirely legitimacy -- the city's collective agreement that his word means something. The party can see exactly who's actually behind him, and that's the point.
