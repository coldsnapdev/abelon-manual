Catacombs, Floor 2: The First Sarcophagi
The cycle still working, the keepers' traps, and the first touch of the Rot
ExplorationTrapsFirst Rot exposureLevel 3–4 party

Floor 2 is where the shrinekeepers' craft is still visible and still working. Three sarcophagi hold the successful output of the cycle: a lore slate, a jar of preserved leaves that enlarge whoever eats them, and modest grave goods. Spike traps installed later by the keepers guard the way. A pool of Rot sits beneath a footbridge in the south-east, and it is the party's first chance to touch the Rot, deliberately or by accident. Two of Adelina's pages are lying where a careful or curious party will find them. The floor ends in two staircases side by side at the top right, which are the party's second blind choice.

At a glance
Arrivals and Departures
Arrivals: Two arrival points, one for each staircase on floor 1. They converge into the same floor.
Departures: Two staircases side by side at the top right, the next blind choice. Each lands at a different arrival point on floor 3.
The Floor
Sarcophagi: Three. A lore slate in the larger one in the low-central room, a jar of leaves, and grave goods worth 120 to 150gp. Floor 2 shows the cycle working as intended.
Pages: Two of Adelina's pages. One on the crates near the main sarcophagus and one on the ground near the sarcophagus in the top-left. Both are easy to find.
Spike traps: The keepers' later anti-looting carpentry. Spot with passive Perception 13, trigger on a Dexterity save DC 13 for 2d10 piercing. The same DCs apply to every spike trap on the floor.
The Rot pool: Under the bridge in the south-east. Touching it gives Rot. Rot Dregs lie in or beside it as scenery.
Crates: Keepers' storage. The first page near the main sarcophagus sits on top of them.
Location Descriptions
The arrival stairs

Two stairways emerge into different parts of the floor. The air is cool and faintly sweet. The stonework here is older and better kept than the antechamber, with leaves and vines carved along the lintels.

The low-central room

The largest chamber on the floor, with the main sarcophagus at its centre. Crates stand along one wall. The room is quiet and reverent, and it is the place where the keepers' belief in the cycle is easiest to read.

Loot

The larger sarcophagus holds a slate tablet sealed in with the body. It carries a keeper's own words: "May the gods build with me as I have built for others. By their service, I am renewed." A small sun-and-crescent motif sits beside a diamond at the top. It is purely emotional, with no mechanical content.

Adelina's page lies on the crates near this sarcophagus. No check is needed to find it.

The other two sarcophagi

Two further tombs sit in side rooms, one of them in the top-left corner of the map. Place the jar and the grave goods in whichever two the map shows. The top-left sarcophagus has a second page on the ground beside it.

Loot

A sealed clay jar holding preserved leaves. Eating them enlarges the eater for 1 hour, as the Enlarge effect without concentration. A character who examines the leaves and makes a Medicine check at DC 12 can tell what they do before eating. A Large creature has to squeeze through the narrow stairs and corridors, which is a small cost the players discover themselves.

Grave goods: jewellery and worked gemstone left as tribute, worth 120 to 150gp. Nothing magical.

Adelina's page lies on the ground near the top-left sarcophagus. No check is needed to find it.

The spike traps

The keepers' own later work, installed to discourage looters. Clean carpentry that still does its job. A strip of flagstones in a narrow corridor looks normal until someone steps on a plate that drives spikes up from below.

The Rot pool and bridge

A sunken pool in the south-east, crossed by a narrow stone footbridge. The liquid is thick, black and glossy and moves in slow swirls with faint steam rising from it. The bridge itself is safe to cross. Anything that touches the pool is touching Rot.

The bone pile

Remains in the bottom-left corner, already old when the keepers built their traps. Evidence of an earlier casualty with no mechanical content.

Encounter Beats
1 Arrival ▼

Open the scene at the arrival point that matches the staircase the party took. Let them take in the carved leaves and vines and the better-kept stonework. This is the first place where the keepers' work still looks like work.

Do not tell them the floor is safe or dangerous. The spike traps are the only active hazard, and a party that moves carefully and notices the first set of holes learns the pattern the rest of the dungeon uses.

2 The spike traps ▼

Use passive Perception 13 against the tell. Do not call for a roll, because asking for one tells the table something is there. If anyone meets the DC, describe small regular holes in the stones or a flagstone sitting proud.

A character who notices can make an Investigation check at DC 13 to learn how it works. Once the trap is known, stepping over it needs no roll. If someone wants to jam it for the others, that is a Dexterity check with thieves' tools at DC 13. Failing by 5 or more triggers it on them. A character who walks onto an undetected plate makes a Dexterity save at DC 13, taking 2d10 piercing on a failure.

Why this mattersFloor 3 repeats the same carpentry, rotted. A party that has learned what a working trap looks like here will read the rotted ones as decay without being told.
3 The sarcophagi ▼

Let the party open them at their own pace. Each one lands in a different category: lore, a useful item and treasure. The slate is the most important of the three. It tells the party that the shrinekeepers believed in the cycle personally and that their burial was meant to feed it.

The jar of leaves is the most interesting decision. Players who eat first and ask later will become Large for an hour and may save the jar for later. Both are fine. Floor 3 has a twin of this jar with the opposite effect.

Hand out Adelina's pages as they are found. The party should leave this floor holding "What it is," "Resisting it" and "Removing it."

4 The Rot pool ▼

A player will poke the pool. Touching Rot gives Rot, so let the Constitution save run at DC 10 for the edge, plus 1 per stack. If a stack lands, narrate it in the body's terms: the character feels physically weaker, their body aches and they move more slowly. Tell the player the mechanical effect every time: Strength reduced by 2 and speed reduced by 10 ft.

Pages found on this floor explain the cure. The cure is magical healing, which clears every stack at once. Rest clears nothing, and the party can learn that here without a death at stake.

5 The two departures ▼

Two staircases sit side by side at the top right. Ask which the party takes and open floor 3 at the matching arrival point. Do not describe floor 3 before they commit.

DM Notes
The Rot pool is infectious.The first stack of Rotten may land on this floor. That is intended. Every stack is -2 to the next ability score in the order Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, and -10 ft speed from the first stack. The cap is six stacks. Magical healing clears everything and the expelled Rot leaves as a single dead Rot Dreg.
Rot Dregs are scenery here.A dying Rot Dreg or two near the pool is background. They have no actions and harm only by contact. Do not treat them as a fight. Their stat block is in the Rot reference.
The corrupted twin comes next.Everything on this floor has a corrupted counterpart on floor 3: the slate, the jar and the treasure. Keep note of the exact wording of the slate and the Medicine DC on the jar, because floor 3 mirrors both.
Sarcophagus placement.The slate is in the larger sarcophagus in the low-central room. The jar and the grave goods go in the other two. The map decides which is which, so confirm against your annotated copy.
Pages on the floor.Place two pages: one on the crates near the main sarcophagus and one on the ground near the top-left sarcophagus. Once both are found, nothing else on this floor yields a page.
