Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Courtesans Network
A Street in Tallowhaven & The Crost Household
Seva asks a favour, and the party learns what she's really testing for
Social encounterNo combatMoral weight: highCourtesans Network introduction

Seva finds the party on the street the morning after the Amber Cup and asks a small favour: retrieve a silver necklace she left behind at a private engagement. What looks like a simple errand turns out to be a test -- the necklace is now around the host's wife's neck, given to her as a gift by a husband trying to cover his own indiscretion, and the party has to find a way to get it back without burning the household down. Seva was never really testing whether they could find the necklace. She was testing how many doors they'd leave open behind them.

At a glance
Part 1 — A Street in Tallowhaven
How it happens: looks like coincidence -- a familiar face, a warm greeting, a natural pause in a busy morning. It isn't coincidence.
The ask: a necklace left at a private engagement, a household she'd rather not revisit herself. Half payment upfront, the Crost address, five minutes total.
What she doesn't say: the nature of the engagement, why the household is complicated, or what she's actually assessing. Not hidden -- just not offered.
Part 2 — The Crost Household
The real situation: Edvar had a private guest (Seva, professionally) while his wife was away, found the necklace after, and gave it to his wife Lena as a gift rather than dealing with it honestly
What Seva is testing: not whether the party can find the necklace -- whether they can retrieve it without burning the household. Relational arithmetic, not a fetch quest.
The real choice: cover story to Lena, wait for an opportunity, pressure Edvar, theft, or tell Lena the truth -- each closes or opens different doors
Location Descriptions
A street in Tallowhaven

Mid-morning, festival preparation visible in the background -- workers moving materials, temporary structures going up. Brief and conducted on the move or standing to one side. Nothing about it looks like a job interview.

The Crost household

A respectable but not wealthy home. The front room is where the encounter plays out if the party is invited in; the necklace is visible on Lena there. Edvar will try to keep the conversation on the doorstep instead, away from her.

NPCs
Seva Sable Courtesans Network — finds them, asks a favour, evaluates
▼
Visual description

Travel clothes, slightly finer than the occasion requires. Composed and unhurried.

Goals for this encounter

This is an evaluation dressed as a favour. She's not primarily interested in the necklace -- she wants to know how many doors would open to the party afterward, and whether the people behind them would be smiling.

What she knows

Left the necklace deliberately. Knew it would be useful. Does not believe any story about it not being there.

What she keeps secret

The nature of the engagement (she was there professionally), why the household is complicated, and what she's actually assessing -- none hidden exactly, just not offered unless asked.

Personality points

Warm and natural, remembers details from any prior conversation. Answers exactly the question asked and no other -- not evasive, just precise. Says she'll find them again and means it, with no time or place given.

Edvar Crost The husband — frightened and cooperative
▼
Visual description

Around fifty-five, portly, well dressed -- the bearing of a man who considers himself well regarded in Tallowhaven and would be devastated to lose that.

Goals for this encounter

Get the necklace out of his house without Lena ever knowing why. Will cooperate entirely with the party toward that end, and may offer additional payment for discretion.

What he knows

Used a few days alone at home to entertain a private guest -- Seva, professionally. Found the necklace after and gave it to Lena as a gift rather than dealing with it honestly. Recognises exactly why the party has come the moment they mention it.

What he keeps secret

Won't tell Lena himself, won't create a scene, and can't retrieve the necklace himself without raising questions he can't answer.

Personality points

Warm on first impression, calculating fast once he understands why the party's there. At the door, glances back toward Lena and starts shepherding the party outside before anything's said indoors.

Lena Crost The wife — warm, unsuspecting, wearing the necklace
▼
Visual description

Similar age to Edvar, grounded and warm -- the kind of woman the party will immediately feel guilty about deceiving.

Goals for this encounter

None -- she doesn't know anything is wrong unless the party or Edvar tells her.

Personality points

Friendly and hospitable, will answer questions freely. If told the truth outright, the mission fails for Seva's purposes -- but Lena herself comes out of it feeling genuinely protected.

Encounter Beats
1 The street encounter ▼

Seva appears, greets the party warmly, and falls into step or pauses to talk while the ordinary business of the city continues around them. She mentions the necklace briefly and practically -- a private engagement, a household she'd rather not revisit, would they collect it on her behalf. She gives the address and pays half upfront, drawn from somewhere without ceremony.

If asked about the engagement, she's economical: a private matter, she attended as a guest, she'd prefer not to make more of it than it needs to be. If asked who she is, she describes herself simply as someone who finds it useful to know the right people -- said without mystery, as if it's the most ordinary thing in the world, which to her it is.

2 Arrival at the door ▼

Edvar opens the door, well dressed, slightly surprised by capable-looking strangers on his step. Behind him, briefly visible before he fills the doorway, Lena in a well-made dress -- wearing the necklace. Let the party look; let them make the connection themselves.

If they mention the necklace or Seva's name immediately, Edvar's manner changes -- he glances back into the house, lowers his voice, and starts moving them back from the door before they've agreed to move. If they don't mention it, he invites them in with practiced hospitality and the scene plays out with Lena present.

3 Edvar's private conversation ▼

Alone with the party, he drops the pretense quickly -- frightened, managing it, and willing to cooperate entirely if they can retrieve the necklace without Lena knowing why. He may offer additional payment for their discretion; this should feel slightly uncomfortable to accept, since the party is now being paid by both sides of a situation only one side knows exists (Seva always finds out).

4 The social navigation ▼

The party needs a way to retrieve the necklace that leaves the household intact. Roughly: a cover story to Lena (best social roll, leaves everyone intact, she feels good about the visit); waiting for an opportunity (slow, avoids deceiving her directly); pressuring Edvar into finding his own clumsy solution; outright theft if a moment presents itself (Seva would find this amusing if it works, a failed attempt is a scene nobody wanted); or telling Lena the truth, which fails the mission for Seva's purposes even if the necklace comes back.

5 The exit ▼
Best -- necklace retrieved, Lena charmed, Edvar gratefulThree people who'd answer the door smiling. Seva is genuinely, visibly pleased -- pays the second half, asks one specific question about the household that reveals she knew more than she let on, and mentions she may have further use for people who can navigate a room like that.
Good -- necklace retrieved, Edvar cooperative, Lena unawareOne warm door, one neutral. Acceptable. Seva pays the second half and notes that Edvar will be careful around the party now -- useful information in itself.
Partial -- retrieved via theftNecklace back, but the household has a vague unease about the visit. Not closed, but suspicious. Seva files it as adequate but inelegant.
Interesting -- party sides with Lena, no necklaceOne closed door, one genuinely warm one. Seva sits with it: “You made a friend. I just need to decide if she's the right one.” The path opens, differently.
Party took Edvar's extra paymentSeva pays without comment, then makes clear -- not threatening -- that her information reaches further than the party assumed. She's more interested in them now, not less.
Party told Lena the truthNecklace returned if it was. Seva is quiet, then: “That wasn't yours to do.” Not angry, precise. Doesn't close the door entirely -- the instinct is legible to her -- but the shape of any future relationship changes.
Failure -- scene created, magistrate involvedShe does her arithmetic out loud, quietly, naming each door that's closed. No necklace, Edvar as an enemy, Lena's life damaged. The path is closed for now.

However it's retrieved, the exit needs to be clean -- Lena neutral to warm about the visit, Edvar feeling the specific relief of a man handed a second chance he probably doesn't deserve. He watches them leave with the expression of someone filing something away. Not a friend. Might answer the door to them again.

DM Notes
Keep the street scene briefIt should feel small -- a familiar face, a quick favour, a few coins, an address. The weight only becomes apparent later, at the Crost household. Don't play it as significant; let the players discover that it was.
The surname SableIf the party asks, this is the first time they hear it. If they later meet other Network members with the same surname, let the connection land on its own -- don't explain it.
Seva's deliberate placementShe left the necklace there on purpose and knew it would be useful. If the party claims it was never there, she'll know, and will say so pleasantly.
Edvar is not a villainHe made a poor decision and is managing the consequences badly. His fear and his cooperation are both genuine -- the party can feel that without needing to approve of him.
Lena deserves betterTrue, and it doesn't change what the mission requires. The party's awareness of that tension is the moral texture of the scene -- don't resolve it for them by making Lena suspicious or Edvar confess.
Seva's one questionWhen she receives the necklace, decide in advance what specific question she asks about the household -- something only prior familiarity with Edvar could reveal. It's a signal, not a threat.
