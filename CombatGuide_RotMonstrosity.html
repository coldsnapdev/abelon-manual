Combat Reference — The Rot Monstrosity

Everbloom Catacombs, Floor 4 — the lake and the circle — Level 3–4 party — Deadly boss
Encounter behaviour

The Monstrosity is a literal chunk of Zareth given form, living in the lake of Rot at the bottom of the catacombs. It has no mind, so no fear, command or other cognitive effect ever works on it. It fights to remove whatever is in its reach.

It stays in the lake. The keepers' torches along the semicircular platform are why it has not left, and they do not stop it once the fight begins. If the party withdraws up the narrow stair, it does not follow out of the lake.

Its Rot DC is 17, plus 1 for every stack a creature already has. Two Surge attacks a turn plus Corroding Presence can stack Rot quickly on a level 3 or 4 party. The Officiant's Stole gives advantage on Rot saves, so a party that rested and attuned at the altar is much better placed. The cap is six stacks, and any further infestation drops the character to 0 HP. Magical healing clears every stack.

Terrain — the arena below the altar chamber
The lakeThick, glossy black Rot on every side of the circular platform, moving in slow swirls with steam rising. Anyone knocked or shoved in makes a Constitution save on entry and at the start of each turn in it: DC 12 below the waist, DC 14 waist to head, DC 17 fully under, each +1 per stack. Climbing out is Athletics DC 12.
The circular platformThe party's ground for the fight, with one narrow stair on and off. Everything else is lake.
The torchesTorches line the semicircular platform before the circle. A torch pulled from its bracket is an improvised weapon that deals fire damage. The Monstrosity is vulnerable to fire, so fire damage is doubled.
The darkBeyond the circle, the far walls are swallowed by darkness. The Monstrosity's blindsight reaches 120 ft.

Multiattack — two Surge attacks.

Harden (Recharge 5–6) — loses up to 20 HP, gains that many temp HP melee hits on it take 2d8 necrotic until the temp HP are gone
Legendary actions (3, at the end of another creature's turn)
Pulse (1) — Moves up to half its speed.
Expel (2) — Loses 6 HP and expels a Rot Dreg with 6 HP, which enters initiative immediately.
Surge (2) — Makes one Surge attack.
Traits
Survival Instinct — Targets creatures that have dealt it fire or radiant damage this encounter first.
Corroding Presence — A creature that ends its turn within 5 ft takes 2d6 necrotic and makes a DC 17 Con save or gains a stack.
Effluent (Acid) — When it takes acid damage, each creature within 5 ft makes a DC 17 Dex save or takes 3d6 necrotic and makes a DC 17 Con save against the Rot.
Effluent (Lightning) — When it takes lightning damage, each creature within 10 ft makes a DC 19 Dex save or takes 3d6 necrotic and makes a DC 17 Con save against the Rot.
Thunder Fission — Takes thunder damage as lost HP and expels a fragment with half that HP: up to 6 HP is a Dreg, up to 18 a Skulker, up to 45 a Beast. No fragment if the thunder damage drops it to 0.
Cold Suspension — Reduced to 0 by cold, it freezes and recovers after 1 hour unless destroyed by fire, radiant damage or salt. Cold also Slows it until the end of its next turn.
Living Rot, Seep — No mind, so no cognitive effects. Moves through gaps as small as one inch.
Tactics
Starts submerged and rises when someone steps onto the circular platform or the party has had a moment to take in the lake. Its first target is whoever dealt it fire or radiant damage first, then whoever is nearest. Surge twice each turn on the same target where possible, since two failed saves can drop a character several stages in one round. Use Harden as soon as it recharges while a melee fighter is engaged, so every hit costs the attacker 2d8 necrotic. Spend legendary actions after the party's turns: Pulse to close on someone who stepped back, Expel when a caster is standing off, Surge when anyone is within 10 ft. Expelled Dregs spawn next to the weakest-looking target.
Running the damage types
Necrotic heals it, so anyone using necrotic damage feeds it. Acid and lightning splash back onto anyone close, so ranged users are safer. Thunder splits it, so a loud party fills the platform with fragments. Fire and radiant are the clean answers. Cold slows it and may freeze it, which buys an hour and gives the party time to finish it with fire or salt.

Tactics
Each Dreg is a small hazard on the platform, and an expelled one enters initiative immediately. It drifts toward whoever is closest. A character who kills one takes nothing for it. The danger is standing in contact at the end of a turn with the Monstrosity's aura already running.
