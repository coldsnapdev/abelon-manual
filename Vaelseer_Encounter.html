Vaelseer NPC Reference
14
What is a Vaelseer
A Vaelseer is one who draws from the wellspring of Vael, the minor god of perception and foresight. They are not priests and have no doctrine. They do not announce themselves. A Vaelseer knows what they are and what they can do. They do not advertise either. In Abelon they are rare — a folk awareness exists of people with unsettling perception, but most people would not have a word for what they are looking at.
Known Vaelseers in this campaign Seren — encountered at the Star and Raven in Tallowhaven. Sits alone at a corner table most evenings, nursing a drink, watching the room. Uses this encounter as written.
Tone — applies to all Vaelseers A Vaelseer is unhurried and entirely without performance. They do not lean forward dramatically or lower their voice to a whisper. They speak the way someone speaks when they already know the answer and are simply waiting for you to catch up. If the party tries to rattle them, they are mildly interested. They cannot be provoked.
On readings A Vaelseer does not offer readings for coin. They offer them as a consequence of play. If a player wins the game proposed, they will read them. If the player loses, the Vaelseer thanks them for the game, buys a drink, and that is the end of it. They will not play the same game twice with the same person. They are not running a racket — they are simply particular about how they spend their perception.
What they actually know Vael's domain is not prophecy in the grand sense. It is the next step. A Vaelseer reads the shape of a person — who they are, what they are carrying, what they are about to walk toward — and speaks it back to them in terms that could be dismissed as coincidence. The reading is always true. How true depends on the dice.

The Games
How it works The Vaelseer proposes a game. The player chooses to accept or decline. Each game involves a single contested roll — usually Dexterity or Wisdom — against the Vaelseer's check. As a baseline, Vaelseers run at +5 Dexterity and +4 Wisdom — adjust for specific NPCs as needed. On a tie, the player wins. No game is offered twice to the same person in one encounter. Keep track as you go.
1
The Cut
The Vaelseer places a deck face-down between you. You each cut to a card simultaneously. High card wins. Aces low.
Contested Dexterity check. The cut itself is intuition, not speed.
2
The Spread
The Vaelseer fans seven cards face-down across the table. You pick one. He picks one without looking. High card wins.
Contested Wisdom check. He claims the cards prefer certain hands.
3
The River
The Vaelseer deals three cards face-up between you — the river. One card is dealt to each of you face-down. You add your hidden card to the highest river card in your head. High total wins. No peeking until the reveal.
Contested Wisdom check. A memory and instinct game dressed as arithmetic.
4
The Tower
The Vaelseer shuffles the deck and holds it face-down. You both call a suit before the top card is dealt. If it matches your call, you win. If it matches theirs, they win. If neither, deal again.
Contested Wisdom check. He has been reading decks for thirty years.
5
The Mirror
The Vaelseer places one card face-down. You place a coin heads or tails on the table. They flip the card. Face card — heads wins. Number card — tails wins. Simple.
Contested Dexterity check. He watches your hands when you place the coin.
6
The Long Draw
The Vaelseer deals the deck slowly, face-up, one card at a time. You call stop whenever you like. The card showing when you stop is yours. They take the next card. High card wins. You can wait — but so can they.
Contested Wisdom check. Patience is the game, not the cards.

The Reading — Roll a d20
How to run it Ask the player to roll a d20 openly. Do not explain what the roll determines before they roll — only that the Vaelseer is about to speak. The result determines the accuracy of the reading, from vague warmth to something that should not be possible to know. Read the appropriate band and then the character-specific text if they rolled 16+.
1 – 5
Barnum — warm, vague, could apply to anyone
►
"You carry something that was given to you a long time ago. Not an object, exactly. More like a way of moving through the world. It has served you. It will cost you something before you are done with it."
"There are two paths ahead. You already know which one you will take. You have probably known for some time. What you are still deciding is whether you deserve it."
"Someone close to you does not say what they mean. You have noticed. You have chosen, so far, not to ask. That choice will come back around."
"You are better at this than you give yourself credit for. Not at everything. At the particular thing that matters right now. Trust that."
DM note: Deliver these slowly, with pauses. They should feel considered even if they are general. The Vaelseer is not performing. They are just speaking a truth that happens to be available to everyone.
6 – 10
Horoscope — specific enough to feel like more than coincidence
►
"You left something behind. Not by accident — on purpose, even if you told yourself otherwise. You have not decided whether that makes you brave or a coward. The answer is probably both."
"There is a version of this that ends with you exactly where you hoped. There is another version. You have been trying not to think about the second one." A pause. "It is worth thinking about."
"Someone is looking for you. Not in the way that should worry you — not yet. But they have a question only you can answer, and they do not know they are looking for you yet. They will."
"You are carrying more than you need to. Not weight. Responsibility. Some of it is not yours. You took it on anyway. That is either your greatest strength or the thing that breaks you. Probably both, at different times."
DM note: These can land for almost any character but should feel pointed. If you know something specific about the character that one of these fits especially well, steer toward that one.
11 – 15
Prescient — specific details that suggest more than guesswork
►
"You came from somewhere quieter than this. You chose to leave. The quiet is still there — it travels with you. Most people cannot hear it. One or two of the people you will meet soon will be able to. Pay attention to which ones."
"There is something ahead of you that you have been told does not exist. The person who told you that was not lying. They simply did not know. You will know. Probably before you want to."
"You are looking for someone. Or something. The thing you are looking for is looking for you too, in its own way. It will find you before you find it. That is not necessarily bad news."
"You have a gift you are afraid of. Not afraid it will fail — afraid of what it means if it works. That fear is reasonable. It will not protect you from the moment when you have to use it anyway."
DM note: At this level the Vaelseer should pause slightly longer before speaking, as if assembling something he can see more clearly. The player should feel that he is reading them specifically, not reciting.
16 – 19
True — character-specific, impossible to dismiss as coincidence
►
At this level, use the character-specific readings below. The Vaelseer goes quiet for a moment before speaking — longer than feels comfortable. They do not break eye contact.
DM note: These readings contain real character information. Deliver them calmly and without emphasis — the content does the work. If the player asks how they know, the Vaelseer says nothing for a moment and then changes the subject. They do not explain themselves.
Nat 20
Something else — the Vaelseer goes pale. They did not expect this.
►
"The Vaelseer begins to speak. Then stops. They look at you — not the way they have been looking at you, with that studied calm — but the way someone looks at something they did not expect to see. They set the deck down. They pick up their drink. They take a long sip before saying anything at all."
"What they say next is shorter than you expected. One sentence. Maybe two. But something about the way they say it — the care they take with each word, as if handling something fragile — makes it land like a stone dropped in still water."
DM note: Use the character-specific reading but precede it with the description above. Then add one additional line that references something no one at the table has said aloud — something from the character's private backstory that the Vaelseer should have no possible way of knowing. Keep it short. Do not explain it. Let it sit.

Character Readings — 16 and above
Usage These readings are written for high rolls (16–19) and Natural 20s. Each draws on real backstory details the player has established. The Vaelseer speaks in second person, present tense, without preamble. They do not ask questions. They state.
Arjay (Rowland Jiggleboots)
Mini  ·  Gnome Druid
►
"You have been the tallest thing in the room your entire life. That is not a comment on your height. The woods made you into something your family would not recognise. You are not sure they would be frightened. That uncertainty is its own answer.

Something changed in the woods. Not recently — a few years ago. You followed something into a part of the forest you had known for a century and came out different. You describe it as a scar. That is not quite right. Scars are where things ended. This is where something started."
DM note (16–19): Do not reference the Rot by name. The Vaelseer cannot name what they see in Arjay — only that something is different about him in a way that is old and deep and not entirely finished. The phrase "this is where something started" should land as ambiguous. Arjay does not know what it means yet either.

DM note (Nat 20): Add — "The wolf found you at the same time. You think you saved him. I am not sure the accounting is that simple." The Vaelseer says nothing further about Scar. They do not ask what a dire wolf has to do with any of it.
Jenok Neoheim
Kerry  ·  Goliath Barbarian
►
"You have been looking for someone since before you left home. You have not told everyone why. The reason is simpler than you have made it — you are afraid of what you will find. Not that he is dead. That he is changed in a way you cannot follow him into.

The thunder in you is not anger. People assume it is anger. It is the part of your father that lives in your chest. You have not decided yet whether that is a gift or a debt. It will ask you to decide soon."
DM note (16–19): "He" is Dohyuk. Do not say the name. Jenok knows who is meant immediately. The detail about the father lands differently depending on how Kerry has been playing Jenok's relationship to Taeyok — follow whatever has emerged at the table.

DM note (Nat 20): Add — "He is alive. He has found something he did not go looking for. He is not sure whether to be grateful." Pause. "Neither are you, I think." The Vaelseer does not elaborate.
Tenok Neoheim
Jord  ·  Goliath Fighter
►
"You noticed before your brother did. You said nothing. You are still carrying that — the gap between what you saw and when you spoke. You tell yourself it would not have changed anything. That may even be true.

You watch more than you move. People mistake that for caution. It is not caution. It is the understanding that once you commit, you commit entirely. You are waiting to be certain. You will not always have the luxury of certainty. That is the lesson coming."
DM note (16–19): "What you noticed" refers to Dohyuk's departure and the wrongness that preceded it — whatever Tenok's backstory establishes he observed first. The reading reinforces the character distinction between the twins: Tenok watches, Jenok acts. Press on whatever guilt thread Jord has established.

DM note (Nat 20): Add — "There will be a moment when your brother goes first and you are not there to see what happens. What you do in the seconds after that is what you are, down to the bone."
Wren Holloway
Kaia  ·  Elf Wizard
►
"You have spent most of your life in a room full of better things than the room you are standing in. That is not a complaint about where you are. It is simply a fact about where you came from. You are discovering that better things are not the same as useful ones.

You write to someone. They write back. You have never met them and you know more about them than you know about most people you have spent years beside. That connection is real. Do not let your instinct for categorisation convince you otherwise."
DM note (16–19): The person Wren writes to is his penpal from Pearlstrand — the Kessavari correspondent established in Kaia's backstory conversations. The Vaelseer has no way of knowing this person exists, let alone the nature of the correspondence. The reading should land as both accurate and slightly inexplicable.

DM note (Nat 20): Add — "Your brother visited you in the labrary more often than he needed to. He knew you noticed. That was the point." The Vaelseer does not explain how they know about the labrary. They do not appear to find it worth explaining.
An Kanglan
Taz  ·  Aasimar Monk
►
"You have been told you were ready when you were afraid you were not. You trusted that. You should not trust it quite so completely. The person who told you was right about what you are capable of. They were less right about why it mattered.

You carry something that belonged to your father. Not the locket. Something older than that, that has no object to hold it. You have been using it your whole life without knowing its name."
DM note (16–19): "The person who told you" is Divine Master. The Vaelseer cannot name him. The implication that Divine Master's reasons were not what they appeared should land as disquieting without being specific enough to explain. "Something older" is the Aeronian celestial heritage — The Vaelseer can perceive it without knowing what it is or where it came from. Do not clarify.

DM note (Nat 20): Add — "The man in the dream is not warning you. He is directing you. There is a difference. I would think carefully about who benefits from you being here." The Vaelseer finishes their drink. They do not look at An again for the rest of the evening.

After the Reading
If the player asks how he knows The Vaelseer looks at them for a moment. "I play cards," they say. Then they pick up the deck and shuffle it. That is the end of that conversation.
If the player tries to engage further The Vaelseer is not unfriendly. They will talk about the weather, the city, the quality of the ale. They will not talk about what was said or what it means. They find questions about the reading mildly boring, the way a craftsman finds questions about how long something took.
If another player immediately wants a reading The Vaelseer will play again. They propose a different game — they always propose a different game. They do not offer the same game twice to the same table in one evening. If all six games have been exhausted, they pack the deck away and buy a round.
What a Vaelseer does not do They do not invite readings. They do not suggest the party might be interested. They do not hint that they are anything other than someone who likes cards. The encounter begins and ends entirely on the party's initiative. If they leave the Vaelseer alone, they sit with their drink and bother no one.
