Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven
The Amber Cup
First evening in Tallowhaven — three threads available, none of them pressed
Social sceneNo combatThreads: Merchants, Trademasters, CourtesansLet it breathe

The party settles into Tallowhaven's best working-quarter inn for the night. Morra gets them fed and housed; in the room around them, all three guild threads are quietly available — Dask and Tomas audibly frustrated at their table, Seva watching from near the card game, and Oskar's name ready to surface if anyone asks Morra about work. None of it needs to be activated tonight, and the scene works fine if nothing happens beyond rest.

At a glance
Purpose: the party rests and the world opens around them. Nothing here should feel like a mission briefing — it's the end of a long day in a warm room.
Trademasters thread: Dask and Tomas are audible at their table before they're visible — workers with nowhere to go, a space suddenly needed. Morra can fill in context if asked, and will point to Oskar/Honeywell if asked about work.
Courtesans thread: Seva is present near the card table but will not approach first. If the party recognises her from the road and comes to her, she may offer the Crost Household favour tonight. If not, Mira delivers the invitation the next day instead.
Merchants thread: Already seeded from the Aldermen's Hall — nothing more needed tonight.
Location Descriptions
The Amber Cup

The best inn in Tallowhaven's working quarter -- warmer and slightly more professional than the Star and Raven, the kind of place that knows how to end a working day well. Smells of good food and candle wax. A harpist plays in the corner, a card game runs at a side table, a few couples dance near the back. Festival preparation has brought extra trade and the inn is making the most of it.

NPCs
Morra Halfling innkeeper — runs this room with quiet authority
▼
Visual description

Middle-aged halfling, small but commanding. Warm round face, sharp observant eyes that miss nothing. Practical clothing, hair pinned back efficiently.

Goals for this encounter

Get the party settled -- rooms, food, drink -- and read them accurately in about thirty seconds without making a performance of it.

What she knows

Context on Dask and Tomas if asked (frustrated, not dangerous); mentions Oskar Sternov at Honeywell if asked about work (“tell him Morra sent you”); knows Seva by face, confirms she's been around a couple of evenings and keeps to herself, but won't volunteer more about a guest.

Personality points

Simultaneously welcoming everyone in the room and tracking all of them. A slight smile that suggests she's seen everything this inn can produce and remains fond of it anyway.

Seva Sable Present — will not approach, available if sought
▼
Visual description

A drink in hand near the card table, present long enough to be part of the room rather than a new arrival. Doesn't look at the party when they enter, which is itself a kind of attention.

Goals for this encounter

Has already decided the party is worth the effort -- patient either way. If approached and the conversation goes well, offers a small favour: retrieving a necklace from a private engagement she'd rather not revisit herself.

What she keeps secret

Doesn't explain who she is or what she does unless directly asked, and is economical even then.

Personality points

If recognised from the road, receives it warmly and without defensiveness -- of course she remembers, she was there. Asks questions that feel like interest and are also assessment.

Dask and Tomas (Trademaster thread) Workers at a table, louder than the room requires
▼
Dask

Older, weary, practical -- nursing a drink, saying less than Tomas. Not hopeless, just tired in the specific way that comes from hoping and being wrong enough times. If engaged separately, more measured and honest: he's not looking for rescue, just somewhere dry to sleep that won't get them moved on in four days.

Tomas

Younger, angry, audible -- the reason the table is noticeable. Angry about the warehouses, the guild system, festival season making rich people richer. Not wrong about any of it, and not making it easier for Dask. Has worked out the warehouse is suddenly valuable and wants to use that leverage.

Mira Courtesans Network — warm, present, watching
▼
Visual description

In the room somewhere -- at the bar, near the dancing, at a table with people she seems to have just met. Good company in the way that makes people forget they only met her an hour ago.

Goals for this encounter

Aware of both the party and Seva. If Seva doesn't make contact tonight, finds whichever party member seems most receptive the following day.

What she keeps secret

Delivers Seva's message as a warm, slightly mysterious coincidence rather than a set-up: Seva sends her regards, and asks that they bring their friends to the private table upstairs.

Encounter Beats
1 Arrival and settling ▼

Morra gets the party settled -- rooms, food, drink -- with the efficient hospitality of someone who does this well, reading them without a performance of it. Let players take stock of the room: the harpist, the card table, the dancing near the back, Dask and Tomas audible at their table, a woman near the card game who was on the same road as them this afternoon.

2 The room finds its shape ▼

Players who listen to Dask and Tomas pick up the shape of the situation -- workers with nowhere to go, a space suddenly needed. Morra fills gaps if asked, without editorialising. Players who drift toward the card table find Crowns and Crows in progress and Seva nearby; recognising her from the road starts the conversation naturally. Players who ask Morra about work get Oskar's name and the Honeywell office.

3 Seva -- if approached ▼

Warm and interested without being eager. Her questions about the party's day and the road feel like interest and are also assessment. If the conversation goes well -- if the party seem capable of discretion -- she mentions the favour: a necklace left at a private engagement she'd rather not revisit herself. She pays half upfront, gives them the Crost address, and asks them to meet her back here when it's done. She doesn't say what happens after.

4 Seva -- if not approached ▼

She makes no move tonight, finishes her drink, and leaves at some point unremarked. The following day, Mira finds whichever party member seems most receptive and makes the encounter feel like a coincidence -- a shared table, a good conversation -- before delivering Seva's message. Upstairs: a comfortable private room, Seva already there, unhurried, wine on the table. She lets them settle before describing the favour.

5 The evening ends ▼

The party has a bed, a meal, and the beginning of whatever comes next -- Alric at the Shallow Tuber if accepted, Oskar at Honeywell if pointed there, or Seva's favour if contact was made. The Amber Cup continues without them: Morra's still at the bar, the harpist plays a little quieter, Dask and Tomas are still at their table. The room doesn't need the party to keep going.

DM Notes
Do not push the threadsAll three are available and none need activating tonight. If the party eats, drinks, and goes to bed, the threads are still there in the morning -- a party that rests and recovers shows up tomorrow in better shape.
Crowns and CrowsThe same game from the Star and Raven. Players who notice have found a piece of world consistency -- nobody comments on it, it simply exists. The game travels with the kind of people who move between towns.
Seva will not approachThis is firm. She doesn't make the first move publicly. A careful player might notice she's aware of them without looking at them -- that's its own signal for anyone paying attention.
Morra and OskarHer recommendation is casual and genuine -- she isn't his agent, she just knows he's usually hiring around festival time and that capable strangers passing through sometimes want work.
Dask and Tomas here vs. at the warehouseThe party meets them tonight as strangers with a problem, and again at the warehouse as the subject of a job. Whether they connect the two depends entirely on how carefully they listened tonight -- don't prompt the connection, let it land on its own.
