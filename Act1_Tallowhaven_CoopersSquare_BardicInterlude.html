The party comes across Marek interrupting a bard's performance in a public square, making the case that the world's small joys don't outweigh what's actually wrong with it. He's arrested for the disturbance, mentions Crossroads in passing if the party engages him, and is taken to the Aldermen's Hall cells -- where he'll still be when the party sees him next, after the Act 1 finale collapses.

At a glance
Setup: a small crowd gathered around Sylas's Step, where the bard Pen performs. Marek has interrupted, standing at its base rather than taking the stage.
The exchange: Pen argues the world's small joys are enough; Marek answers with the broken-wing gesture, in public, for the first time
The arrest: routine -- civic guard or the party, Marek doesn't resist either way, and ends up in the Aldermen's Hall cells regardless
If engaged: Marek mentions Crossroads once, briefly, as a place people are starting to ask the right questions without any answers yet -- the Act 2 seed
Location Descriptions
Cooper's Square

One of Tallowhaven's older open spaces -- wide enough for a market, paved with uneven stone, ringed by the backs of guild buildings and the fronts of modest shops. At the north end, a low unadorned stone plinth called Sylas's Step, which performers have used for as long as anyone remembers. A small but attentive crowd has gathered -- festival week has made people more disposed to gather. Some are amused, some uncomfortable, a few genuinely listening to both sides.

NPCs
Pen Bard — non-binary, old, battle-scarred, holds the crowd
▼
Visual description

Old in the way of someone who's been everywhere and kept little of what they found there except the experience. Battle-scarred, travel-worn clothing in several mismatched layers, a flute at the belt repaired so many times the original is mostly memory.

Goals for this encounter

Doesn't step down when Marek interrupts -- listens, considers, and answers from where they're standing.

What they know

Have seen the broken-wing gesture before, not often, enough to know what it means without seeking it out.

What they keep secret

Privately think Marek is probably right about more than they'd like -- will say this honestly if the party asks afterward, though it doesn't change what they do on the Step.

Personality points

Hard-won warmth rather than innocence -- have seen the worst the world offers and still concluded the balance is positive. Not easily rattled; find Marek genuinely interesting rather than threatening. Line: “You say this, sir, but look around you. The world still turns. People find life, love, and meaning all over this apparently god-forsaken world.”

Marek Talan Former Paladin — reappears, interrupts, makes his point, gets arrested
▼
Visual description

The missing fingers are visible as always -- the crowd may notice, nobody says anything.

Goals for this encounter

Not trying to win the crowd -- making an argument he believes is true, precise rather than angry, adding the part Pen's song didn't account for.

What he knows

Doesn't claim to know what's happened to the gods, only that he feels their absence, and has since before he lost his fingers.

What he keeps secret

If asked about the gesture, confirms it's his but says he didn't name it, didn't organise it, doesn't know most people who use it -- finds their existence mildly interesting and largely beside the point.

Personality points

Never angry, always precise. Line, raising both hands in the linked-thumb gesture: “A bird with a broken wing can still fly.” A beat, then: “But neither high enough nor far enough to survive for very long.” If given any opening, mentions Crossroads once and moves on immediately, as if it weren't significant. It is.

Morra Amber Cup — gives the party the précis, watches with mild pleasure
▼
Goals for this encounter

Spots the party at the edge of the crowd and gives them her précis unasked: “They've been at it for a good few minutes now. Honestly, it's more entertaining than the song was. Some guff about how grateful we should be for all the small things that make life great, or something.”

What she keeps secret

Privately thinks Marek is probably making a fair point -- says this quietly to the party if asked, not to the crowd.

Personality points

If a player identifies Marek by name, she registers it -- she's heard the story before, and looks at him with the slightly more attentive expression of someone updating their assessment.

Encounter Beats
1 Arrival -- Morra gives the précis ▼

The party finds a small crowd around Sylas's Step -- Pen on the plinth, Marek at its base, the argument already underway. Morra, at the edge of the crowd, spots the party and gives her précis unasked, staying put to see how it ends.

2 The exchange ▼

The party arrives in time to hear Pen's response: “You say this, sir, but look around you. The world still turns. People find life, love, and meaning all over this apparently god-forsaken world.” Marek listens, doesn't dismiss it, then raises both hands -- the linked-thumb gesture, visible to the whole crowd -- and answers: “A bird with a broken wing can still fly.” A beat, gesture held. “But neither high enough nor far enough to survive for very long.” The crowd goes quiet; some are recognising the gesture for the first time. Pen watches him with the expression of someone who finds the argument harder to dismiss than most.

3 The party decides ▼

The crowd starts to disperse or linger. A civic guard has noticed and is drifting toward the square without urgency -- not a crisis, just something that may need managing. The party can watch and do nothing, engage Marek, engage Pen, approach Morra, arrest Marek themselves, or leave. If nobody acts within a minute or so, the guard arrives and begins moving Marek on -- he doesn't resist.

4 If the party engages Marek ▼

Same patient detachment as before -- precise, brief answers, no performance. If given any opening, he mentions Crossroads: a place where people are beginning to ask the right questions without having arrived at any of the right answers. He moves on immediately after, as if it weren't particularly significant.

5 The arrest ▼
Party engages Marek, Crossroads mentionedThey carry it. When the finale collapses and they find themselves in the same gaol as him, the conversation picks up where it left off, and Act 2's direction is established.
Party watches but doesn't engageThey saw the exchange, the gesture, the arrest. Marek makes the Crossroads reference in the gaol scene instead.
Party arrests Marek themselvesHe ends up in the same place either way. Being arrested by the same people twice, he finds mildly interesting and says so in exactly one sentence before moving on.
Party engages PenPen is honest that they find Marek's argument harder to dismiss than most, and have seen the broken-wing gesture before without knowing where it comes from -- a warmer, more human angle on the same ideas.

Whether it's the civic guard or the party, Marek is taken into custody for causing a disturbance and doesn't resist -- if it's the party arresting him, he notes it as an interesting development and files it away without comment. He's taken to the Aldermen's Hall cells, where he'll still be when the party arrives after the Act 1 finale collapses.

DM Notes
The gesture in publicFirst time the party sees the broken-wing gesture performed deliberately and publicly by Marek himself, with context and origin. They may have seen it before, on the road or elsewhere. Don't explain it -- let the party connect it at whatever speed they connect it.
CrossroadsMarek mentions it once, briefly, and moves on. Don't repeat or emphasise it. Plant it and leave it -- the gaol scene at the end of Act 1 is where it develops into something actionable.
Pen and MarekNeither is wrong. Pen's hard-won warmth and Marek's structural diagnosis are both true simultaneously. Let the party feel the weight of that without being told what to conclude.
The civic guardNot antagonists -- people doing a routine job, handling a minor disturbance with the efficient boredom of people who've probably seen Marek before.
Pen after the sceneSteps down once Marek is taken, doesn't perform again immediately, looks for a moment at the space where he stood, then tucks their flute away and walks on. Available if followed; otherwise disappears into the city.
