Mission Overview

Alric presents this as a matter of guild reputation. Merchants who trusted him have been left without goods during the most visible trading period of the year. He needs the party to speak to the affected traders, find out what they know, trace the problem back, and solve it. He pays half upfront.

He mentions Lande at Fabled Furnitures as an aside — she had issues with a shipment going the other way too. He remembered what mattered to him. He did not retain the directional detail.

The party is expected to do the legwork. The investigation leads naturally to the road. The road leads naturally to a fight.

The hidden layer The toll operation is Trademaster-connected. The party cannot discover this during this mission regardless of what they do. No documents, no names, no overheard conversations point that way. The connection surfaces later through pattern recognition.
The seeded detail A waiter mentions the chef's special is unavailable due to a delivery issue from the coast. This happens before the mission is offered. A party that connected the dots at dinner will feel the satisfaction of having noticed early.

Mared mentions that a Driftwood Point courier got through last week. A vendor or innkeeper who deals with visiting traders knows where she tends to eat. The party finds her at lunch or dinner at a specific establishment if they follow the lead and interact well enough with the right person.

The dark humour of someone who has had a genuinely bad week and has reached the stage of finding it faintly funny.

What she knows
The checkpointLocation, roughly how many people, the paperwork, Cadog Melyn's name on the document. She describes the leader without naming him — physically imposing, engaged with her argument and then made clear it was not the point.
The Sekarth commentAfter describing the toll experience she adds something like: "That, plus the fact that every tradey in this town is looking at me like Sekarth himself, I'd say this has been a trip to quickly forget."
The key detailAlmost as an afterthought — the leader said something that suggested he was not worried about being moved on. The confidence of someone operating with backing from a direction that makes things difficult to challenge. She cannot say more because she does not know more. The party carries it.
Investigation Beats
Sequence is flexibleThe party can visit the merchants in any order. The picture assembles differently depending on the sequence. Lande is the most informative but most guarded. Osip is the most immediate but has the least tactical detail. Mared is the most composed and gives the Anwen lead. A thorough investigation gives the party tactical advantage at the road. Skipping straight to the road after the first lead means arriving less prepared.
I.1 Alric's brief — the Shallow Tuber ▼

Alric describes the problem as a matter of guild reputation. Merchants who trusted him are losing money during the most visible trading period of the year. He gives them three names — Mared Ffion at Silvermark, Osip Baran at the butchers, and Lande at Fabled Furnitures. He mentions Lande had issues going the other way too. He says this as an aside. He does not elaborate.

He pays half upfront. He expects them to find out what happened and fix it. He does not tell them where to look beyond the three merchants.

I.2 The merchant conversations ▼

The party visits the three merchants in whatever order they choose. Each conversation gives them a different piece of the picture.

From Mared: the Anwen lead and confirmation that someone got through by paying.

From Osip: the timeline — ten days — and doubt about Alric's reliability as an architect of grand plans.

From Lande and Caeryn: both directions are blocked, Cadog Melyn's name, and a ground level account of what the checkpoint looks like and how it operates.

By the end of the merchant visits the party knows there is a checkpoint, it has been running for ten days, it blocks both directions, it uses a landowner's name as legal cover, and its leader operates with the confidence of someone who feels protected.

I.3 Finding Anwen ▼

Mared's mention of a courier who got through gives the party a thread to pull. A successful conversation with a vendor or innkeeper who deals with visiting traders from Driftwood Point locates Anwen — she is having lunch or dinner at a specific establishment and is killing time before heading back tomorrow.

She fills in the tactical detail: rough number of people at the checkpoint, the paperwork operation, the leader's manner. She gives them Cadog Melyn's name a second time, independently corroborating Lande's ledger entry.

Her Sekarth comment lands naturally in the middle of venting. The key detail about the leader's confidence comes almost as an afterthought as she is wrapping up. Let it sit without drawing attention to it.

I.4 What the party knows before they go ▼

A bottleneck on the road between Tallowhaven and Driftwood Point. Running for ten days. Blocking both directions deliberately. Legal cover from Cadog Melyn's land ownership — genuinely grey rather than obviously false. A leader who is physically imposing and rhetorically confident. Multiple operatives. The operation feels sanctioned from somewhere.

A party that visited all three merchants and found Anwen has enough tactical detail to approach the checkpoint with a plan. A party that skipped straight to the road after the first lead arrives less prepared but gets there faster. Both are valid choices with different consequences.

Phase Two — The Road
The Toll Faction
The mirror dynamicThis group are capable people who took a job that pays well and asks few questions. The party has been doing exactly that for the last few sessions. There but for the grace of the gods. Nobody in this group is a villain by nature. They are professionals in a morally ambiguous situation, which is precisely what the party is.

Combat Beats
Map pendingThe exact tactical layout depends on the map selected. The encounter is designed around a bottleneck — a point where the road narrows and carts must slow. Whether that is a bridge, a ford, a forest track, or a rocky defile, the principles hold. Morda behind. Brek ahead. Radek and Gort at the centre. Thessaly elevated or set back. Peck visible at the front until combat starts.
C.1 Arrival — the checkpoint in view ▼

The party approaches the bottleneck. People are there. It looks like a checkpoint rather than an ambush — a cart waiting, Peck visible and official-looking, paperwork in hand. Radek is present but silent. Morda is behind the party before they realise it. Brek is ahead on the far side, visible only if someone looks past the bottleneck. Thessaly is not immediately obvious.

A Perception check against DC 14 spots Morda before she is in position. DC 16 spots Thessaly's position. Let prepared parties have this advantage.

C.2 The transaction — Peck works ▼

Peck greets them warmly. He explains the toll with practiced ease. Cadog Melyn's name on the document. Maintenance fee for use of the crossing. Entirely standard. He quotes a figure. His timing is good. He almost makes it sound reasonable.

Radek says nothing yet. He is letting Peck work.

C.3 The argument — Radek enters ▼

If the party challenges the legal basis Peck handles it pleasantly. If they push harder Radek enters the conversation. He engages with the argument genuinely, which is unsettling. He knows the position is grey. He also knows where he is standing and who is behind the party.

At some point he does something that makes clear the argument was never the point. A carter who has been waiting too long says something impatient and Radek's response is one beat more than it needed to be. Let that land before anything else happens.

C.4 The fight ▼

Morda and Brek close from both ends when combat starts. Thessaly's first spell lands from somewhere the party did not expect — prioritise this reveal if they failed the Perception check. Gort moves with Radek. Peck steps aside with a comment and finds somewhere to be that is not in the middle of it.

Radek fights with genuine enjoyment. He uses Action Surge when challenged or when the fight starts going against him. He says things during combat. He is the last to go down.

The sheep on the open ground, if present on the map, cause minor chaos if anyone runs through them. Disadvantage on the next attack roll from the distraction is reasonable.

C.5 Aftermath ▼

Peck, if still standing, puts his hands up with professional detachment. The job has changed shape. He has no particular loyalty to Radek beyond the contract.

Prisoners can be taken. Nobody names who hired the group even if subdued and questioned. The documentation naming Cadog Melyn exists but proves nothing about the actual employer. Thessaly, if questioned, may say something about the job coming through channels rather than directly. The party cannot follow that thread yet. They carry it.

The road is clear. The fish will arrive. The Driftwood Point relationship is back on track.

Outcomes
Full investigation then road cleared

The party visited all three merchants, found Anwen, and arrived at the bottleneck with tactical detail. They know roughly what to expect. Alric receives the report with measured satisfaction. He notes they found the full picture — both directions blocked — without commenting that he already knew part of it. He pays the second half. The party carries Thessaly's comment about the job coming through channels.

Partial investigation then road cleared

The party followed the first lead and went straight to the road. The fight was harder. The road is still clear. Alric pays the second half. He notes they were efficient. He does not say what he noticed about what they missed.

Party pays the toll and turns back

The road is not cleared. Alric's supply line is not restored. He receives the report without visible anger. He is disappointed in a specific way that the party will understand. He will find another solution. He will not be using them for anything that requires nerve for a while.

DM Notes
The hidden Trademaster layerThe toll operation was commissioned through Trademaster-connected channels. This is not discoverable during this mission. No documents, no names, no conversations point that way. Thessaly's comment about the job coming through channels is the only thread available and it leads nowhere yet. The connection surfaces in Act 1's third round when the factions are already in motion and the party can look back and understand what they were part of.
Osip's comment about AlricNot dishonest. Just optimistic in ways that cost other people money. The party carries this. It does not need to be resolved. It is a perspective on a man they are working for, offered by someone with a legitimate grievance. Let it sit.
Radek's extra punchPlay this once, early in the encounter, before combat starts if possible. A moment where he does something one beat more than necessary to a bystander or a waiting carter. It establishes who he is before the fight and makes the combat feel like the continuation of something rather than a surprise.
Peck's exitMake it funny and make it human. He is a professional stepping out of a situation that has moved beyond his job description. The comment about the ledger should land as dark comedy without undercutting the tension of what is happening around him. One good line, well timed, then he is out of the way.
