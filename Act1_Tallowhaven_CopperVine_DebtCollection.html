Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Merchants' Guild
The Copper Vine
Collecting Alric's debt from Caspar Veld
Social encounter No combat expected Moral weight: high Party paid in advance
Environment

The Copper Vine sits on a side street off Tallowhaven's main trading quarter. It is slightly more decorated than its neighbours — a professionally painted sign, window boxes, the faint smell of wine and oak that reaches the party before they open the door. Inside, it is dim and pleasantly cool. Bottles line every wall, some in racks, some on open shelves. A small tasting counter occupies one end with two stools.

Everything in here is fragile. The party will notice this. Caspar will notice that they have noticed.

Bottles Everywhere. On shelves, in racks, on the counter. The implicit threat writes itself. Nobody needs to say anything.
Tasting area Two stools at the counter. Caspar may offer the party a seat here early on. It is a disarming move.
Back room A door behind the counter. Caspar disappears through it occasionally. Private space if the party needs a word with him alone.
Street Visible through the window. A scene inside has witnesses outside. Caspar knows this and so should the party.

Caspar Veld Wine merchant — the debtor

Appearance

A man in his mid-forties, well dressed in a way that suggests he considers himself a cut above the average trader. Clean hands, good boots, the bearing of someone who finds his own company interesting. Warm on first impression. The warmth has a practiced quality.

Personality
Default modeCharming, slightly performative. He greets everyone as if they might be a customer. The smile fades when business turns awkward but does not disappear entirely.
Under pressureHe runs a script. Mild surprise, mild indignation, mild reassurance. He has done this before and has a routine. He will try to run it before the party cuts through it.
CorneredHe flips. The speech is his weapon and he uses it when he runs out of road. It is partly genuine and partly calculated. Both things can be true.
AfterwardsHe watches whoever leaves with the expression of a man filing something away. He is not their enemy. He might even respect them depending on how they handled it.
The speech — when he calls their bluff
"So what happens now then? You're going to smash up my shop? Rough me up a bit? All so you can hand the money directly to a man who'll use it to pay a bill he owes to a building? You came in here as errand runners and now you're deciding how far you'll go. That's worth knowing about yourselves, isn't it?"

He is not wrong. He is also not paying. The speech is a calculated exit route dressed as moral philosophy. The party must decide what to do with that.

What he knows
The debtHe owes it. He knows he owes it. He has no genuine dispute, only deflections.
AlricHe has been here before with Alric's people. He considers this a normal part of the arrangement.
The partyHe does not know who they are. He is learning as the scene progresses.
Encounter Beats
Before you beginThe party have been paid in advance by Alric. They cannot simply walk away without either completing the job or keeping money they did not earn. Caspar does not know this. The bottles are present throughout every beat. Do not draw attention to them. Let the players do that.
01 The entrance ▼

The party walks in. It smells good. Caspar emerges from the back with the manner of someone who expects to be pleased by whoever has just arrived. He is friendly, slightly performative about it, and gestures at a bottle he is particularly proud of this week.

The moment they mention Alric's name the warmth does not disappear. It becomes a different kind of warm. Practiced.

02 The routine ▼

Caspar has done this before. He expresses mild surprise that it has come to this, mild indignation that Alric did not simply send a message, and mild reassurance that of course it will be sorted — he has been meaning to get to it. He has been busy. The festival preparation has been chaotic. Surely they understand.

He is not panicking. He is running a script. Let him finish it before the party cuts through it.

03 The push ▼

The party pushes past the script. They are not here for reassurance. They are here for payment.

Caspar recalibrates. Becomes slightly more direct. Raises a mild dispute about the exact amount — which he knows will not land but buys him a moment. Then pivots to logistics. He does not have that sum liquid right now. Perhaps next week.

04 The argument lost ▼

The party does not accept next week. They have reasons: Alric's payment to the city, the chain of obligation, the fact that Caspar has been next-week-ing this for longer than is reasonable.

Caspar runs out of road. He knows it. There is a brief silence where he reassesses the situation and the people in front of him. This is the moment before the speech. Let it sit for a second.

05 The speech ▼

Caspar gestures at the shop. At the bottles. At them. Calm, almost reasonable, slightly theatrical.

"So what happens now then? You're going to smash up my shop? Rough me up a bit? All so you can hand the money directly to a man who'll use it to pay a bill he owes to a building? You came in here as errand runners and now you're deciding how far you'll go. That's worth knowing about yourselves, isn't it?"

He is not wrong. He is also not paying. He waits to see what they do with that.

06 The decision ▼

This moment belongs entirely to the party. The options are roughly:

Back down — leave empty handed. They still have Alric's advance in their pocket. That conversation will need to happen.

Implicit threat — pick up a bottle. Look at it. Put it down somewhere that isn't where they found it. Say nothing about it. This may work. It costs them something about themselves.

Explicit threat — words or actions that make the implication clear. Works with higher probability. Costs more.

Creative solution — take a case as collateral, negotiate a witnessed payment schedule on the spot, find a third angle. Caspar will find this harder to argue with than a threat.

Reward the unexpected. If a player does something you did not anticipate, find a way to make it work rather than refusing it.

07 The aftermath ▼

However it resolves, Caspar watches them leave with the expression of a man filing something away. He is not their enemy. He might even respect them slightly depending on how they handled it.

Alric receives their report with the same measured calm he brings to everything. He notes what they did and how. He does not say much about it. His tone is the mirror of their conduct — see the Alric responses section below.

The party carries whatever they did out of the room. Nobody awards or deducts anything explicitly. The moral weight lives in what they chose, not in what the game tells them about it.

Likely Outcomes
Success — money retrieved cleanly

The party found a way through, whether by persuasion, creative pressure, or implicit threat. Caspar paid or gave something of equivalent value. They leave with the money.

Alric pays the second half of their fee. He does not celebrate. He had a feeling about them and is confirmed in it.

Success — creative solution

The party negotiated something Alric did not suggest — a witnessed schedule, collateral, an angle he did not think to offer. Caspar cooperated because it gave him a workable exit.

Alric is quietly more interested in them than he was. He files it away without comment.

Partial — money retrieved, something broken

The party got the money but used explicit coercion, damaged property, or caused a scene. The job is technically done.

Alric pays without ceremony. He mentions carefully that Caspar is a known figure in the trading community and that visibility has costs. Not a reprimand. Just information delivered in a particular way.

Partial — Caspar dead or seriously hurt

The situation escalated past what anyone intended. The money may or may not have been retrieved. Alric is quiet for longer than usual. He asks precise questions. He tells them to say nothing to anyone and that he has things to handle. He will be in touch.

The Merchant thread is complicated but not necessarily closed. How complicated depends on what witnesses exist.

Failure — empty handed

The party came back without the money. Caspar called their bluff and they folded. They still have Alric's advance.

See the Alric failure response below. The door is not closed but it is noticeably less open than it was this morning.

Alric Venn — Responses
Key principleAlric's tone is a mirror, not a judgement. He does not moralise. He reflects back what the situation now is and what that means practically. He is always calm. The variation is in the temperature of that calm.
Smooth persuasion — no drama ▼

Alric counts the money. Thanks them. Pays the second half without delay. Says something like: "Good. I had a feeling about you." Simply. Meaning it. There will be more work if they want it.

Creative solution — angle he didn't suggest ▼

He listens to how they did it. He is slightly more interested than he expected to be. He does not quite compliment them but notes the approach. Pays promptly. He files it away as information about how they think.

Implicit threat — bottles survived ▼

Still satisfied. He does not ask questions. The slight cooling in his manner suggests he knows roughly what happened and considers it an acceptable tool used appropriately. Pays without ceremony.

Explicit threat — property damaged ▼

He pays. His tone is careful. He mentions that Caspar is a known figure in the trading community and that visibility has costs. Not a reprimand. Just information delivered in a particular way. The relationship continues but with a note attached.

Caspar seriously hurt ▼

The money is accepted in silence. He takes a moment. Then he explains quietly that the guild operates within Tallowhaven's civic structures and that certain outcomes create problems that cost more than an unpaid levy. He will handle what needs handling. He would prefer not to use them for something like this again.

Caspar dead ▼

The room changes. He does not raise his voice. He asks one or two precise questions. Then he tells them to stay available and say nothing to anyone. He has work to do. They are not dismissed warmly. The Merchant path is not closed but what it looks like from here is different.

Empty handed — Caspar called their bluff ▼

Alric listens to what happened with the same attentive calm he brings to everything. He is quiet for a moment that is worse than anger.

He tells them to keep the advance. Quietly, without ceremony. It is already gone in his accounting. He will handle Caspar through other means in his own time.

Then: "I didn't need you to frighten him. I needed to know whether you would when it mattered. Caspar asks that question of everyone eventually. Now I know your answer."

He remains polite. He does not make a scene. He wishes them well with the festival. The door is not closed permanently. It is noticeably less open than it was this morning.

DM Notes
The bottlesNever mention them unless a player does. Their presence is the implicit threat. If a player picks one up or looks at one deliberately, let the moment breathe. Caspar will notice. You do not need to narrate that he noticed.
The advance paymentThe party cannot easily walk away. They were paid before the job. If they return empty handed they keep money they did not earn. If they return the money, Alric will not take it — a deal is a deal. This is not coercion. It is a commitment with consequences, which is different.
Alric does not ask howWhen the party reports back with the money, Alric does not ask what happened. If they did something they are not proud of, that stays with them rather than being acknowledged or judged. The moral weight belongs to the party, not to Alric's response.
Caspar is not a villainHe is a man who has been pushing his luck long enough that it has become his default mode. His speech is partly genuine. He is not wrong about the system. He is using the moral argument cynically to avoid a legitimate debt, and both of those things are simultaneously true.
Reward the unexpectedIf a player does something you did not plan for, find a way to make it land rather than refusing it. The best moments in this encounter will be things neither you nor the players anticipated.
