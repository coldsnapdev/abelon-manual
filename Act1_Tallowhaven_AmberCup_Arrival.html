Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven
The Amber Cup
First evening in Tallowhaven — three threads available, none of them pressed
Social scene No combat Threads: Merchants, Trademasters, Courtesans Let it breathe
Environment

The Amber Cup is the best inn in Tallowhaven's working quarter — not the grandest in the city, but the one that has earned its reputation. Warmer and slightly more professional than the Star and Raven. The kind of place that knows how to end a working day well. It smells of good food and candle wax.

The room is active without being heaving. A harpist plays in the corner. A card game is running at a side table. A few couples are dancing near the back. The festival preparation has brought extra trade and the Amber Cup is making the most of it.

The party arrives tired at the end of a long day — an escort, an ambush, a magistrate, a city. They need food, drink, and a bed. Morra will see them settled before anything else happens.

The bar Where Morra operates. She has the room mapped before the party finishes their first sentence.
The card table Crowns and Crows. The same game Seren was playing in Brindle Hollow. Seva is nearby — not playing, watching. Players who recognise the game may drift toward it.
The harpist Background warmth. The Amber Cup curates its atmosphere deliberately. Morra did not let this happen by accident.
The dancing Near the back. Festival energy bleeding into the evening. Tallowhaven is gearing up for something.
Dask and Tomas At a table, having a drink. Louder than they should be. The Trademaster thread is audible before it is visible.
Seva Present. Near the card table. Not playing. She arrived here by the same road as the party and has been here long enough to have a drink in hand. She does not approach them. She is available if they come to her.
The Three Threads
Merchants

Already seeded. Alric suggested breakfast at the Shallow Tuber tomorrow morning. The thread is open. Nothing more needed tonight.

Trademasters

Dask and Tomas are at a table. Their situation is audible if the party listens. Morra can fill in the picture if asked. Oskar's name and the Honeywell office can surface naturally here.

Courtesans

Seva is present but will not approach. If the party comes to her — recognising her from the road, or simply drawn to the card table — the conversation can begin. She may give them the Crost household job tonight or arrange to find them tomorrow.

Appearance

A halfling woman, middle-aged, small but commanding. Warm round face with sharp observant eyes that miss nothing. Practical inn-keeper's clothing, hair pinned back efficiently. The expression of someone who is simultaneously welcoming every person in the room and keeping track of all of them. A slight smile that suggests she has seen everything this inn can produce and remains fond of it anyway.

Role in the scene
First contactShe greets the party, gets them settled, reads them accurately in about thirty seconds. She knows they have had a long day without being told.
On Dask and TomasIf the party notices the disturbance at their table she will provide context — they have been here a few nights, they are not dangerous, they are frustrated and taking it out on the evening. She handles it with the calm efficiency of someone who has done this before.
On OskarIf the party asks about work she mentions that Oskar Sternov at the Honeywell office is usually looking for capable hands around festival time. Tell him Morra sent you. She does not explain the relationship further. It is simply how things work in Tallowhaven.
On SevaShe knows Seva by face if not by name. She does not volunteer information about her guests. If asked she confirms the woman has been here a couple of evenings and keeps to herself.

Dask, Tomas and the Trademaster Thread
Load
Dask Older worker — weary, practical
▼

Nursing a drink and saying less than Tomas. He has been here before in the sense that he has been in situations like this before. He is not hopeless. He is tired in a specific way that comes from hoping and being wrong enough times.

If a player engages him separately from Tomas he is more measured and more honest about the situation. He is not looking for rescue. He is looking for somewhere dry to sleep that will not get them moved on in four days.

Load
Tomas Younger worker — angry, audible
▼

He is the reason the table is noticeable. Louder than the room requires. Angry about the warehouses, about the guild system, about the fact that festival season makes rich people richer and does nothing for people like him. He is not wrong about any of this. He is also not making it easier for Dask.

He has already worked out that the warehouses they are staying in are suddenly valuable. He wants to use that. He is telling anyone who will listen, which is nobody at this point except Dask who has heard it already.

Encounter Beats
Purpose of this sceneThe party rests. The world opens up around them. Three threads are available and none of them push. This scene should not feel like a mission briefing. It should feel like the end of a long day in a warm room where interesting things happen if you look for them.
01 Arrival and settling ▼

The party arrives. The room is warm and active. Morra gets them settled — rooms, food, drink — with the efficient hospitality of someone who does this well. She reads them without making a performance of it.

Let the players take stock of the room. The harpist. The card table. The dancing near the back. Dask and Tomas audible at their table. A woman near the card game who was on the same road as them this afternoon.

02 The room finds its shape ▼

Players who listen to Dask and Tomas pick up the shape of the situation — workers with nowhere to go, a space that is suddenly needed, festival season making everything harder. Morra can fill in the gaps if asked. She is not editorial about it. She just knows what she knows.

Players who drift toward the card table find Crowns and Crows in progress and Seva nearby. If they recognise her from the road the conversation begins naturally. If they simply sit down near the game she will make conversation at her own pace.

Players who talk to Morra about work get Oskar's name and the Honeywell office. Tell him I sent you.

03 Seva — if approached ▼

She is warm and interested without being eager. She asks questions about the party's day, about how they found the road, about what they thought of Tallowhaven on arrival. These feel like interest. They are also assessment.

If the conversation goes well — if the party seem capable of discretion and handling a situation with some nuance — she mentions the favour. A necklace left at a private engagement. She would prefer not to revisit the household herself. She pays half upfront and gives them the Crost address.

She asks them to meet her back at the Amber Cup when they are done. She does not say what happens after that.

04 Seva — if not approached ▼

She makes no move tonight. She finishes her drink and leaves at some point, unremarked. The party may or may not notice her go.

The following day Mira finds whichever party member seems most receptive. She makes the encounter feel like a coincidence — a shared table, a good conversation, a natural moment. Then the message: Seva sends her regards and asks that you bring your friends to the private table upstairs.

Upstairs: a comfortable private room. Seva already there. Unhurried. Wine on the table. She lets them settle before saying anything of substance. Then she describes the favour.

05 The evening ends ▼

The party has what they came for — a bed, a meal, and the beginning of whatever comes next. Tomorrow: Alric at the Shallow Tuber if they accepted, Oskar at the Honeywell if Morra pointed them there, or Seva's favour if contact was made tonight.

The Amber Cup continues after they go upstairs. Morra is still at the bar. The harpist plays a little quieter. Dask and Tomas are still at their table. The room does not need the party to keep going.

DM Notes
Do not push the threadsAll three are available. None of them need to be activated tonight. If the party sits quietly, eats their food, and goes to bed, the threads are still there in the morning. The scene is not a failure if nothing plot-relevant happens. A party that rests and recovers is a party that shows up tomorrow in better shape.
Crowns and CrowsThe same game from the Star and Raven. Players who notice this have found a piece of world consistency. Nobody comments on it. It simply exists. The game travels with the kind of people who move between towns.
Seva will not approachThis is firm. She does not make the first move publicly. If a player is watching her carefully they may notice she is aware of them without looking at them. That awareness is its own signal for anyone paying attention.
Morra and OskarHer recommendation of Oskar is casual and genuine. She is not his agent. She simply knows that he is usually hiring around festival time and that capable strangers passing through Tallowhaven sometimes want work. It is the kind of thing she knows because she runs a good inn and pays attention.
Dask and Tomas here vs at the warehouseThe party meets them tonight as strangers with a problem. They meet them again at the warehouse as the subject of a job. Whether they connect the two depends entirely on how carefully they listened tonight. Do not prompt the connection. Let it land on its own.
