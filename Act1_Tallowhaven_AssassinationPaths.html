Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Third Missions
The Assassination Paths
Seven o'clock. Three private rooms. The party arrives too late for a briefing — but in time for a choice.
Combat — conditionalMoral weight: extremeFollows Evening InvitationsFeeds Festival Finale

If the party goes after a target instead of attending their own faction's briefing -- or arrives having already decided to act -- they walk into a private room where the target reads the situation almost instantly. There's no briefing left to give. Whichever room the party enters, they choose: kill, or warn. Both choices lead to the Festival Finale, but through very different doors.

At a glance
The realisation: each target reads the room in seconds, without panic -- none of the three try to flee, and none of them raise their voice first
The choice: kill or warn, once, per room. Warning always means the target arrives at the signing already prepared and already owed something.
The rooms are small on purpose: one exit each, a companion covering the door or the stairs -- these are not fair fights to run from, only fights to finish
Six paths forward: each target's kill or warn outcome feeds a specific Festival Finale accusation and faction-collapse combination -- see Outcomes on each
Location Descriptions
Private room, the Amber Cup (Seva)

Small and enclosed -- a round table, three chairs, candles, an untouched decanter. One narrow window, too small to exit through quickly. Mira controls the door from behind the party. The tavern is loud below; a fight here wouldn't be heard immediately.

Private room, the Shallow Tuber (Alric)

A rectangular dining room, one door, no meaningful window. Huw stands between the party and the exit. Soundproofed enough that raised voices are needed to be heard outside.

The Honeywell Office (Oskar)

Cramped and functional, the desk dominating. The only exit is the stairs, where Tav stands. The window looks down fifteen feet onto the dark forge floor -- nobody's leaving that way.

NPCs
Seva Sable Courtesans Network — CR 2, the most dangerous person in the room
▼
Personality points

Reads the room in about two seconds and shows nothing. Line, quietly: “Close the door, Mira.” Then: “You are not here for a briefing. So. What are you here for?” Never raises her voice, even losing -- the quieter she gets, the more dangerous she is.

Combat

AC 14, HP 32, Atk +5, Spd 30. Dagger +5 (1d4+3) melee or thrown (range 20/60). Read the Room (action, DC 12 Insight -- learns a target's emotional state and concealed weapons). Deflect (reaction, reduces one seen melee hit by 1d6+3). Uncanny Poise (no opportunity attacks moving away). Composed (advantage vs frightened, can't be charmed by CR 2 or lower). Opens with Read the Room to identify the biggest threat, keeps the table between herself and the party, uses Deflect on every melee hit she sees coming. Does not flee.

Mira Sable Sable operative — CR 1/2, controls the door
▼
Personality points

Warm until she isn't -- the transition is fast enough to surprise anyone who only knew her as the friendly face at the door. Stands her ground alongside Seva.

Combat

AC 14, HP 18, Atk +5, Spd 30. Dagger +5 (1d4+3) melee or thrown (20/60). Sneak Attack (+1d6 with advantage or an adjacent ally). Uncanny Poise. Starts behind the party at the door, draws the moment things are clear, and moves toward whoever's furthest from Seva to create a two-front problem.

Alric Venn Merchants' Guildmaster — CR 1, desperate energy, has never done this before
▼
Personality points

Has never been in a fight -- frightened and won't show it, commits with desperate, unclever energy. Line, carefully: “You are not here to discuss what I asked you to discuss.” Then: “I want to understand what has brought you to this room instead of the other one.” Angrier than he's ever been -- at the situation as much as the party -- which makes him more dangerous than his stats suggest. Won't run.

Combat

AC 12, HP 22, Atk +4, Spd 30. Rapier +4 (1d8+2). Suggestion (1 slot, DC 13 Wis save, last resort only). Fights with feeling, not skill.

Huw Alric's assistant — CR 1/2, more capable than anyone realised
▼
Personality points

Cold in a way that surprises anyone who's only seen him taking notes. Positions himself to protect Alric rather than engage independently -- doesn't break, fights until he can't.

Combat

AC 13, HP 18, Atk +4, Spd 30. Rapier +4 (1d8+2). Charm Person (1 slot, DC 13 Wis save, 1 hour), used immediately on whoever looks most dangerous. Composed.

Oskar Sternov Trademaster — CR 1, cold anger, fights close and dirty
▼
Personality points

Doesn't need a weapon -- forty years of working with his hands. Cold anger, not hot: doesn't shout, doesn't telegraph. Line, without inflection: “You are not here about the meeting.” Then: “Then say what you have come to say.” Says nothing at all during the fight -- the silence is the most unsettling part of fighting him in a small room at night.

Combat

AC 12, HP 24, Atk +5, Spd 30. Unarmed strike +5 (1d6+3). Grapple (Strength contest +5, removes a target from the fight). Powerful Build. Goes immediately for the most dangerous party member and tries to pin them first, using the cramped office to push people into the desk, walls, and window.

Tav Trademaster apprentice — CR 1/4, angry, controls the stairs
▼
Personality points

Loyalty to Oskar has become identity. Angry enough to stay longer than wise -- holds the only exit until he genuinely can't anymore.

Combat

AC 12, HP 11, Atk +4, Spd 30. Handaxe +4 (1d6+2) melee, thrown round one (range 20/60).

Encounter Beats
A Seva's room ▼
KillTwo against a full party in a confined room, neither willing to flee -- it's over quickly. Getting out means passing through the main floor below; Morra will know something happened upstairs and won't stop them, but she'll remember.
WarnShe listens without expression, already having anticipated something like this, and asks exactly what Alric said and what the party wants in return. She tells them she'll need them standing with the Courtesans at tomorrow's meeting -- a statement, not a request.

The party enters the private room above the Amber Cup expecting a briefing that isn't coming. Seva looks up, reads the situation in about two seconds, and gives nothing away. She has Mira close the door behind the party, then asks directly what they're here for -- partly to hear them say it, partly to buy Mira the two seconds she needs to position.

B Alric's room ▼
KillHuw is the more capable fighter but is protecting rather than attacking; Alric fights with anger, not skill. It doesn't last long. Other diners noticed the party go in and will notice them come out -- nobody stops them tonight.
WarnHe listens without surprise, asks exactly what Oskar said, and thanks them with a warmth that may feel unsettling given what he'll do with it. He tells them, as settled fact, to be at the signing tomorrow -- he'll need capable people in the room.

The party is admitted by Huw, who positions himself between them and the exit before Alric has even looked up. Alric reads the room slower than Seva would -- he's a systems thinker, not an instinct reader -- but gets there, sets down his papers, and asks carefully what's brought them to this room instead of the other one.

C Oskar's room ▼
KillOskar is the more dangerous of the two; Tav is committed but outmatched. When it's done, getting out means back down the stairs and through a quiet but not empty forge district. Nobody stops them -- the body is found in the morning.
WarnHe listens, concludes rather than processes, and says: “I know what she wants from the signing. Now I know what she is willing to do to get it.” He asks exactly what Seva said, pays without discussion if asked, and tells them, simply, to be at the signing tomorrow.

Tav lets the party up without being asked and takes the top of the stairs behind them -- the only exit, now blocked. Oskar doesn't look up immediately, then reads them quickly, sets down his pen, and states flatly that they're not here about the meeting.

DM Notes
Six paths into the FinaleKill Seva: Elbeth steps in as acting Courtesan leader, suspects the party, and accuses them -- Alric (who hired them) is livid the accusation landed on his guests; fight is the Merchant faction path. Warn Seva: she arrives already knowing, turns the accusation on the party for standing with Alric instead -- fight is the Courtesan faction path. Kill Alric: Caen Rhys steps in as civic anchor, Huw accuses the party to him -- Oskar (who hired them) is livid; fight is the Trademaster faction path. Warn Alric: he turns the accusation on the party for standing with Oskar -- fight is the Merchant faction path. Kill Oskar: Anneth Holdworn leads the Trademaster delegation, Cors (secretly loyal to the Courtesans) accuses the party -- Seva (who hired them) is livid; fight is the Courtesan faction path. Warn Oskar: he turns the accusation on the party for standing with Seva -- fight is the Trademaster faction path.
None of the three panicEach target reads the room and responds with control, not fear -- that consistency is deliberate. These are people who have all, in their own ways, been in difficult rooms before.
The kill paths are quickTwo against a full party in a confined space -- don't drag these out mechanically. The weight of the scene is in what just happened, not in a long combat.
The warn paths cost the party something tooEvery faction leader who's warned expects the party at the signing, explicitly, as a settled fact rather than a request. That's a debt as much as a reward.
