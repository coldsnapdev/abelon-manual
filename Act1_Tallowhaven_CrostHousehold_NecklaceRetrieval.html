Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Courtesans Network
The Crost Household
Retrieving Seva's necklace — a test of discretion, charm, and judgement
Social encounter No combat Courtesans Network introduction Moral weight: high
How This Mission Begins

Seva may have made contact with the party at the Amber Cup the previous evening, or Mira may have delivered her message the following morning. Either way the mission brief is the same — a necklace left at a private engagement, a household she would rather not revisit herself, half payment upfront.

When the job is done, however it resolves, Seva asks the party to meet her back at the Amber Cup. She does not say what happens after that. What happens after that is the beginning of the Courtesans Network relationship.

The Setup

Seva approached the party at the Amber Cup and asked a favour — a necklace left at a private engagement she attended some days ago. She would retrieve it herself but the timing is awkward and she would rather not revisit the household directly. She gave them Edvar Crost's address and paid half upfront. She described it simply as a silver piece, distinctive, left behind at a private gathering.

What she did not say: she was there as a sole guest, professionally. Edvar's wife does not know. The necklace is now around Lena Crost's neck, presented to her as a gift. Edvar knows exactly who the party is and why they have come the moment they mention the necklace. He will want to talk privately. Immediately.

Seva left the necklace deliberately. She knew it would be useful. She does not believe any story about it not being there.

Environment
The door Edvar opens it himself or has it opened. The moment he sees the party he is calculating. If Lena is visible behind him wearing the necklace, the calculation accelerates.
The front room Where the encounter plays out if the party is invited in. A respectable but not wealthy home. The necklace is visible on Lena.
The doorstep Edvar will try to keep the conversation here — outside, away from Lena. He moves to shepherd the party back before anything is said indoors.
The necklace Silver, distinctive. Around Lena's neck. The party may or may not notice it immediately depending on their position and Perception. If they do not notice it on arrival, they will inside.

Edvar Crost The husband — frightened and cooperative

Appearance

Around fifty-five, portly, well dressed. The bearing of a man who considers himself well regarded in Tallowhaven and would be devastated to lose that. Warm on first impression. The warmth calculates very quickly when he realises why the party is here.

What happened

He used a few days alone at home to entertain a private guest. Seva was there professionally. The necklace was left behind. He found it, recognised it, and rather than return it quietly presented it to Lena as a gift. It seemed like the simpler solution at the time. It was not.

His behaviour
At the doorHe sees the party and immediately looks back over his shoulder toward Lena. He begins to shepherd them back from the doorway before anyone says anything. He wants to be outside with the door closed before words are exchanged indoors.
When he understandsHe drops the pretense quickly when alone with the party. He is frightened and trying not to show it. He will cooperate entirely if they can retrieve the necklace without Lena knowing anything. He may offer them additional payment for their discretion.
His limitsHe will not tell Lena himself. He will not create a scene. He needs the necklace to leave his house and he needs Lena to remain unaware of why.
If the party takes his additional payment

They are now being paid by both sides of a situation only one side knows exists. Seva will know about this payment. She always does.

Appearance

Similar age to Edvar, grounded and warm. The kind of woman the party will immediately feel guilty about deceiving. She is wearing the necklace. She received it as a gift from her husband and has no reason to think anything of it.

Her role
UnawareShe does not know why the party is here. If Edvar manages the doorstep she may not encounter them at all. If the party comes inside she is present and the necklace is visible.
If approached directlyShe is friendly and hospitable. She will answer questions. She does not know anything is wrong unless the party tells her.
If told the truthThe encounter fails for Seva's purposes. The necklace may or may not be returned. The household is damaged. Seva receives the necklace and something else she did not expect — a clear picture of where the party's loyalties sit when pressed.
Encounter Beats
What Seva is testingShe is not primarily interested in the necklace. She wants to know how many people would answer the door to the party afterwards and whether they would be smiling when they did. The necklace is the minimum bar. Above it, her evaluation is entirely relational.
01 Arrival — the door ▼

A portly gentleman of around fifty-five opens the door. Well dressed, slightly surprised by the sight of capable-looking strangers on his step. Behind him, visible briefly before he shifts to fill the doorway, a woman of similar age in a well made dress. She is wearing a necklace.

Stop there. Let the party look. Let them make the connection themselves if they are going to. Do not tell them what Edvar is thinking.

If they mention the necklace or Seva's name immediately, Edvar's manner changes. He glances back into the house, lowers his voice, and begins moving them back from the door before they have agreed to move.

If they do not mention it, he invites them in with practiced hospitality and the scene plays out with Lena present.

02 Edvar's private conversation ▼

If the party gets Edvar alone he drops the pretense quickly. He is frightened and managing it. He will cooperate entirely if they can retrieve the necklace without Lena knowing anything about why.

He may offer additional payment. This should feel slightly uncomfortable to accept.

He cannot retrieve it himself — he cannot manufacture a reason to take the necklace back from his wife without raising questions he cannot answer. He needs the party to find a way.

03 The social navigation ▼

The party must find a way to retrieve the necklace that leaves the household intact. Their options:

A cover story to Lena — a lost heirloom, a mistaken purchase, something that gives her a reason to hand it over without implicating anyone. Requires the best social roll but leaves everyone intact. Lena feels good about the visit.

Wait for an opportunity — find a reason to stay long enough that the necklace is removed or unattended. Awkward and slow but avoids direct deception of Lena.

Edvar retrieves it under pressure — the party makes clear they are not leaving without it and Edvar finds his own solution. He will. It will be clumsy and Lena will be curious about it.

Theft — if the party finds a moment when the necklace is unattended. Seva would find this amusing if successful. A failed attempt is a scene nobody wanted.

Tell Lena the truth — the encounter fails for Seva's purposes. The necklace may still be returned. See outcomes.

04 The exit ▼

However they retrieve it, the exit needs to be clean. Lena should feel neutral to warm about the visit. Edvar should feel the specific relief of a man who has been handed a second chance he probably does not deserve.

Edvar watches them leave with the expression of someone filing something away. He is not their friend. He might answer the door to them again if they knocked.

Likely Outcomes
Best — necklace retrieved, Lena charmed, Edvar grateful

Three people who would answer the door smiling. Seva is genuinely pleased and shows it more than usual. The Courtesan path opens warmly.

Good — necklace retrieved, Edvar cooperative, Lena unaware

Necklace back. One warm door in Edvar, one neutral in Lena. Acceptable. Seva pays the second half and asks her one revealing question.

Partial — necklace retrieved via theft

Necklace back but the household has a vague unease about the visit. Not a closed door. A suspicious one. Seva files it under adequate but inelegant.

Interesting — party sides with Lena, no necklace

No necklace. One closed door in Edvar. One genuinely warm door in Lena who feels protected. Seva receives this information and sits with it. She says something like: "You made a friend. I just need to decide if she's the right one." The Courtesan path opens but differently.

Failure — necklace not retrieved, scene created

No necklace and damage done. Seva does her arithmetic out loud, quietly, so the party understands what happened and why it matters. The Courtesan path is difficult from here.

Suspicious — party claims necklace was never there

Seva does not believe them. She says so directly and pleasantly. Then she waits to see what they do with that. How they respond to being caught in a small lie tells her more than the lie itself.

Seva's Responses
Seva's principleShe is not interested in whether the party feels good about what they did. She is interested in how many doors are open to them afterwards and whether the people behind those doors would be smiling. No item is worth more than the person who has it. The necklace is the minimum bar. Everything above it is relational arithmetic.
Necklace retrieved, Lena charmed, Edvar relieved ▼

She receives it without ceremony. Pays the second half. Is more openly pleased than usual — this is a harder outcome to achieve than most people manage. She asks one question, something specific about the household that reveals she already knew more than she let on. Then she mentions that she may have further use for people who can navigate a room like that.

Necklace retrieved, Edvar pressured, Lena neutral ▼

Pays the second half. Notes that Edvar will be careful around them now, which is useful information in itself. She files it. More work is available.

Party took Edvar's additional payment ▼

She pays them without comment. Then she says something that makes clear she knows about the additional payment. Not threatening. Just letting them know her information reaches further than they assumed. She is more interested in them now, not less.

Party told Lena the truth ▼

She receives the necklace if it was returned. She is quiet for a moment. Then: "That wasn't yours to do." Not angry. Precise. She does not close the door entirely — the instinct behind the decision is legible to her. But the nature of any future relationship is different. They are people with principles. That is useful to know.

Empty handed — they claim it was never there ▼

She does not believe them. She says so directly and pleasantly. She asks a few precise questions and works out what actually happened. She decides whether their failure reflects poor observational skills or poor effort. If she concludes the former she offers a second attempt. If the latter the door narrows.

Scene created — Aldermen's Hall involved ▼

She does her arithmetic out loud. Quietly. So the party understands exactly what happened and what it cost. No necklace. Edvar as an enemy. Lena's life damaged. Possibly a magistrate who now has their names attached to a household incident. She names each door that has closed. The Courtesan path is closed for now.

DM Notes
Seva's deliberate placementShe left the necklace there on purpose. She knew it would be useful. She does not believe any story about it simply not being found. If the party tries this she will know and will say so pleasantly. The question she is asking them is whether they can retrieve it without burning the household — not whether they can find it.
Edvar is not a villainHe did something wrong and is managing the consequences badly. His fear is genuine. His cooperation is genuine. He is a man who made a poor decision and is now dependent on strangers to contain it. The party can feel that without needing to approve of him.
Lena deserves betterThis is true. It does not change what the mission requires. The party's awareness of it is the moral texture of the encounter. Do not resolve it for them by making Lena suspicious or by making Edvar confess. Let the party sit with it.
Seva's one questionWhen she receives the necklace she asks one question about the household — something specific that reveals she knew more than she told them. Decide what this question is before you run the scene. It should be something that could only be known through prior familiarity with Edvar. It is a signal, not a threat.
