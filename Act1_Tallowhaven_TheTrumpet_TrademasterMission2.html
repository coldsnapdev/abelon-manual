The Trumpet
Find the source of the article, deal with it quietly — before it grows legs

⚔ Combat Guide
Mission Overview

Oskar finds the party. He does not do this lightly — he is a man who expects people to come to him and the fact that he has left the Honeywell and tracked them down tells the party the situation is serious before he opens his mouth. He is livid in the specific way of someone whose life's work has been publicly insulted in a paper that half of Tallowhaven reads over breakfast.

He puts The Trumpet on the table. Front page. He lets them read it. Then he points to the research body cited in the article — The Western Reach Institute of Commerce and Craft — and tells them flatly that it does not exist. No address, no charter, no record with the city, no history anywhere in the region. Someone invented it, dressed up a set of selectively presented numbers, and fed it to a journalist.

He does not want a retraction. Retractions are buried on page four and nobody reads them. He wants the source found and the problem removed before the festival meeting. He pays half upfront.

The hidden layer The article was commissioned by the Courtesans Network to stall the levy proposal before it gained public momentum. The party cannot discover this during this mission. The chain ends at Perran Carew, who was fed the idea and the data through an intermediary who has since become unreachable. That intermediary was acting for the network.
The dead end trap The party may think suppressing the article and leaning on Calla is sufficient. It is not. Oskar will make this clear if they report back without Perran's name. The source still exists. The problem still exists. The Trumpet is not the problem. The source is the problem.
Phase One — Investigation
The Article

Headline: "Tallowhaven Trademasters' Reputation in Tatters!"

Byline: Calla, The Tallowhaven Trumpet

Cited source: The Western Reach Institute of Commerce and Craft — a fabricated research body with no address, no charter, and no civic record anywhere in the region. Oskar spotted it immediately.

Claims: Independent research shows that on average, Trademaster guild works are overpriced and of a lower standard than imported equivalents. Quotes an unnamed prominent Tallowhaven trader. The data is selectively presented. The conclusions are fabricated.

His manner in this scene

The controlled fury of someone whose life's work has been publicly misrepresented in the most widely read paper in Tallowhaven during the most important trading week of the year. Calla Journalist, The Trumpet — filed in good faith, was used, will give up the source
▼
Who she is

A journalist who has carved out a niche in a profession that did not initially welcome her and has become pragmatic about certain things as a result. She has filed hundreds of stories. She knows when she is being managed. She did not know it this time, which is the professional mortification she is currently sitting with.

Her initial position

Defensive. She stands by her story professionally. Her source was credible, the data was provided in good faith, she has no reason to doubt it. She will not give up a source to anyone who walks in off the street with a grievance. Protecting sources is not negotiable.

The turning point
The evidencePresent Oskar's documentation. The Western Reach Institute of Commerce and Craft does not exist. No charter. No address. No civic record. Let her sit with that.
Her reactionNot immediate capitulation. A moment of genuine professional mortification followed by cold anger. She believed the research was real. She filed in good faith. She is now the journalist who ran a fabricated story in The Trumpet with her name on the byline. She does not forgive that quickly or quietly.
Giving up the sourceShe gives them Perran Carew's name. Not as a favour to the party. As the first step in understanding what was done to her. She also tells them which establishment he tends to frequent during festival week. She is already planning what she is going to do about this. It will not be nothing.
What she cannot give them
The chain beyond PerranShe does not know who fed Perran the idea or the data. She only knows her source. The chain beyond Perran is not available to her or to the party.
Investigation Beats
I.1 Oskar finds the party ▼

Oskar does not do this lightly. The party sees him coming and something about his manner tells them before he sits down that this is not routine. He puts The Trumpet on the table without preamble. He lets them read the headline. Then he points to the fabricated research body and states the facts plainly.

He pays half upfront. He tells them to find the source and deal with it. He does not tell them how.

I.2 The Trumpet's office — Calla ▼

The byline leads them to The Trumpet's office. Calla is there. She receives them with the wariness of a journalist who has had powerful people try to lean on her before. She stands by her story initially. She will not give up a source.

Presenting the evidence about the fabricated research body changes the conversation entirely. This is not a powerful interest trying to suppress a legitimate story. This is a journalist discovering she was used. The professional fury that follows is genuine and directed at Perran rather than the party.

She gives them his name and the establishment where he spends his festival evenings.

I.3 The dead end — if the party stops at Calla ▼

If the party returns to Oskar having only dealt with Calla and the article, he receives the report with measured patience and explains why it is not enough. The source still exists. The motivation still exists. The Trumpet is not the problem. The source is the problem.

He does not send them away. He waits to see if they understand the distinction. If they do not he explains it once more and sends them back out.

Phase Two — The Establishment
The Location

The Establishment — a large, well-appointed tavern where Perran's family connections secured him a regular table during festival week. It is bigger than it sounds. A long room with round tables spread across the floor, a bar and kitchen running along the left wall, a piano in the upper left corner, and a prestige table at the top of the room under a mounted skull. Perran's table is up there. He considers this his due.

It is a busy festival evening. The room is well populated — couples, groups of traders, a few festival visitors making the most of the week. The piano player is working. The bar is doing good business.

Perran is at his table with Aldwyn, Osric, and Fenn. Brynn and Mags are at the bar on the left. Davan Holt is by the door at the bottom of the room. Jori is out the back. The party has no reason to know any of this yet.

Perran has been here for a while. The atmosphere around his table is loud and slightly unsteady. He is not incapacitated but he is not sharp. The same cannot be said for Davan Holt, who has been nursing one drink all evening and watching the room.

The fleeing patronsWhen the fight starts the room empties around the combatants. Two groups of uninvolved patrons flee — a couple near Perran's table who are on their feet and moving before Perran has finished his first swing, and a group of three or four festival visitors in the middle of the room who scatter in different directions, one of them knocking a table and sending drinks across the floor. Neither group is involved. They are environmental texture. The room thins out quickly, which means the party is fighting in an increasingly open space as the waves arrive — the opposite of the basement encounter. Use it.
Perran Carew
Load
portrait
Perran Carew Human commoner — the target, entitled, pharmacologically impacted, swings first
▼
Who he is

A young man of good Tallowhaven family whose inherited civic legacy — names etched in stone at the Aldermen's Hall, a park, a tavern, a scholarship — he has confused with personal merit. He commissioned the article deliberately, dressed the fabrication in the language of research because he is smart enough to know that is how you make something stick, and felt entirely justified doing so. The Trademasters, in his worldview, are workers who have organised above their station and need reminding of their place.

His worldview — verbatim if given the chance
Who are they really? Nobodies, that's who. Their name hasn't been etched in stone for generations at the Aldermen's Hall. They don't have a park, a tavern, and a scholarship named after them, do they. They're nobodies. They'd do well to remember it.
When challenged
His instinctPerformance of confidence rather than exercise of it. He says something dismissive. Something slightly threatening. It lands badly in front of his friends and now he cannot back down without losing face. He swings first.
After the fightFrightened underneath the bravado. He does not know who fed him the idea. He genuinely believes he thought of this himself. He was approached by a contact of a contact who mentioned the levy and its implications for families like his and happened to have very compelling data. He did not question it because it confirmed what he already believed. He agrees to retract. He is not in a position to argue.

Combat Beats
C.1 The confrontation — before initiative ▼

The party finds Perran at his table. They approach with pointed questions about the article. Perran receives them with the performance of someone who considers himself above this kind of thing. He says something dismissive. Something slightly threatening. He may deliver his worldview about the Trademasters if given the space to do so — let him, it is important that the party understands who he is before the fight.

The confrontation tips when he cannot back down in front of his friends. He swings first. Roll initiative.

C.2 Round one — the table ▼

Perran, Aldwyn, Osric, and Fenn. All impaired except Perran who is large enough that impairment does not fully neutralise him. The party handles this wave. They think they have it handled.

The room reacts immediately. The couple at the adjacent table are on their feet before Perran finishes his first swing, moving for the door without looking back. A group of festival visitors in the middle of the room scatter — one of them clips a table and sends drinks across the floor. The piano stops. The room empties around the combatants with the speed of people who have collectively decided this is not their problem.

Fenn looks for an exit in round one. Give him one if the party does not actively prevent it. He is not relevant to the mission and his departure is a small comedy beat before things get harder.

By the end of round one the room is significantly emptier. The party has more space than they did when they walked in. That space will feel very different when Davan Holt walks through the door in round three.

C.3 Round two — the bar arrives ▼

Brynn and Mags enter from the bar. Brynn is loyal and straightforward. Mags is the tactical problem — she targets the most exposed party member and uses Cunning Action to reposition between attacks. The party may not have anticipated her.

The room has shifted. What felt manageable in round one is no longer manageable in the same way.

C.4 Round three — door and back ▼

Davan Holt enters from the door. Jori enters from the back. Davan has been watching all evening. He enters with a clear professional assessment and goes directly for whoever is causing the most damage to Perran's side. He uses Action Surge in round three if he needs to make a statement immediately.

Jori is deeply unhappy. He swings once, misses, and reassesses his life choices.

This is the survival round. The party needs to end things quickly — focus Perran, drop Davan, or create enough chaos that the remaining commoners lose the will to continue.

C.5 Resolution ▼

When Perran goes down or is clearly beaten the fight loses its centre. Aldwyn stops because Perran is down. Osric stops because Aldwyn stopped. Brynn looks at the situation and makes a practical decision. Mags disappears at some point between rounds — she was never committed to anyone in this room, just to the situation, and the situation has resolved.

Davan Holt is the last to stand down. He does so when it is clear that continuing serves no purpose. He is professional enough to recognise that moment.

Perran, when he recovers enough to talk, is frightened and much quieter than he was twenty minutes ago. He gives the party what they need. He agrees to retract. He cannot trace the chain beyond the contact who approached him.

Outcomes
Source found, retraction secured, Oskar satisfied

The party reports back. Oskar pays the second half. Good work, cleanly done. The levy proposal moves forward without the public relations problem. The Trumpet will run a small correction. Nobody will read it. That is fine. The story is dead.

Source found but retraction not secured

Perran was dealt with but not convinced to retract. Oskar receives this with measured disappointment. He will pursue the retraction himself through other means. He pays the second half because the source was found. He notes that the job was half done.

Party stops at Calla

Oskar sends them back. Patiently. Once. The source is still out there. The Trumpet is not the problem.

Scene created — magistrate involvement

A pub fight that gets out of hand has consequences. The party may find themselves explaining themselves to civic authorities. Oskar will not publicly acknowledge any connection to them. He pays the second half privately and later. He is not pleased.

DM Notes
The hidden Courtesan layerThe article was commissioned by the Courtesans Network. Perran was fed the idea and the data through an intermediary who is now unreachable. The party cannot discover this. The chain ends at Perran. The connection surfaces in the finale when the party looks back at what they were part of across all three second missions.
Perran's speechGive him room to deliver it if the confrontation allows. The worldview about names etched in stone and nobodies needing to remember their place is the most important character beat in the encounter. It reframes the fight from a pub brawl into something with actual stakes. The party should want to hit him before he finishes.
Davan HoltHe is the encounter's biggest tactical surprise. Eight combatants is already a lot. Eight combatants including a sober Fighter with Action Surge entering in round three is the moment the party realises this was never going to be easy. Do not telegraph him. Let him arrive.
MagsShe was at the bar tonight by coincidence. She is not loyal to Perran. She joined because the situation presented itself and she is the kind of person who acts on situations. She disappears before the resolution. The party may or may not notice. Either way she is gone and she is not their problem.
Fenn's exitLet it happen. A small gnome finding the earliest possible exit from a pub fight he never wanted to be in is a human moment in the middle of a chaotic encounter. Play it briefly and move on. It costs nothing and gives the table something to laugh about.
Calla's next moveShe is already planning what she is going to do about being used. It will not be nothing. A future issue of The Trumpet may carry a story about a prominent Tallowhaven family whose members have a habit of manufacturing convenient research. That story does not affect this mission. It is simply the world continuing after the party has moved through it.
