Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Third Missions
The Evening Invitations
Seven o'clock. Three locations. The party can only go to one.
Choice point No combat Moral weight: extreme Seeds the finale
⬡ The choice point

Each faction delivers an invitation on the same afternoon. Same time. Different locations. The party chooses where to go — and by choosing, commits. The full briefing only happens after they arrive. They will not know what they are being asked to do until they are already in the room.

How the invitations arrive

Alric — Huw delivers a folded note to wherever the party is staying. Formal, brief. Alric has reserved a private room at the Shallow Tuber this evening and expects them at seven. No further explanation.

Oskar — Oskar finds them himself, as he always does. Direct, no ceremony. He needs an hour of their time this evening at the Honeywell office. Seven o'clock. He does not wait for a response.

Seva — Mira finds them. Warm, unhurried, as if it is a social call. Seva would like to see them this evening at a private room above the Amber Cup. Seven o'clock. Mira smiles and does not elaborate.

TimingThe three invitations arrive within the same hour. The party has time to discuss before seven but not time to attend more than one meeting. If they attempt to split the party, each faction leader notices immediately that not everyone is present and will not give the full briefing until the whole group is there.
Alric Venn — The Shallow Tuber
Wants Seva Sable removed before the signing
▼
The setting

A private room at the Shallow Tuber. His territory, his table, his terms. There is food on the table that he has not touched. He is composed and well dressed. Huw is not present.

He waits for the party to be seated before he begins. He does not offer pleasantries.

His justification

He tells them about what the Courtesans did to the Driftwood delegation. Seva's people went crawling through Tallowhaven's back streets — harassing a member of the Driftwood delegation, involving criminal muscle, creating exactly the kind of chaos that makes serious trade partners walk away. Emlyn Hale is barely holding it together. The delegation is rattled.

He is measured but the feeling underneath is genuine. Tallowhaven is stronger together. The Driftwood deal is not just about his guild — it is about the city's future. Seva and her network are a relic of a time when this city ran on whispers and leverage rather than legitimate commerce. She is the reason Tallowhaven stays fragmented when it could be unified.

She has to go before the signing. He does not use any other words for it. He does not elaborate on method. He looks at the party and waits.

Alric The Courtesans have had their time. Tallowhaven needs to decide what kind of city it wants to be. I have made my decision. I need to know if you have made yours.
What the party recognises

The chaos Alric is describing — Emlyn, the muscle, the hassle — is The Starling. That was their mission. They retrieved Elbeth for Seva. The rattled delegation, the nervous Emlyn, the criminal element that got involved — all of that traces back to what they did.

Alric does not know they were involved. He is describing the consequences of their work as the reason he needs them to commit murder.

What Seva says if the party goes to her
Seva's defence — if confronted

She listens without interrupting. When the party finishes she is quiet for a moment. Then she acknowledges that she became aware the Driftwood delegation had been destabilised and that she moved to protect one of her own people. She does not consider this threatening anyone. She considers it protection.

She then pivots. She asks the party whether Alric mentioned what the Driftwood deal actually means for anyone in Tallowhaven who earns a living outside the merchant guild structure. Because she knows what it means. And the party might want to hear it before they decide whose side they are on.

If the party confesses their involvement in The Starling she is still for a moment. Then: "So you were the ones." She does not say it with anger. She files it. The party will not know what she concludes from that until much later.

The three options
3a — Take the mission ▼

The party agrees to remove Seva before the signing. Alric pays half immediately. He tells them she will be at the Amber Cup preparing for the evening. He expects them at the signing the following day.

What remove means is left deliberately unclear. The party has to decide what they are willing to do. Alric does not clarify unless pushed, and even then he says only that she cannot be present at the meeting.

Consequences: Seva's absence from the meeting destabilises the Courtesan faction. Her network does not scatter but it loses coordination at the critical moment. The Merchants arrive at the signing in a stronger position. The collapse still happens — the Trademasters see an opportunity in the power vacuum — but the shape of it is different.

3b — Double cross — warn Seva ▼

The party takes the briefing and then goes to Seva. She listens. She already suspected something was coming — she is Seva — but confirmation changes her plans for the signing entirely.

She is not grateful in any demonstrative way. She is precise. She asks two questions: what exactly did Alric say, and what does the party want in return for the warning. She takes the answers and does not show her hand.

At the signing, Seva arrives knowing Alric tried to have her removed. She does not confront him immediately. She waits. When the collapse happens it is at a moment of her choosing rather than by accident. Alric will eventually know the party warned her. That conversation happens in the aftermath.

3c — Confess to Alric ▼

The party tells Alric that the chaos he is describing — Emlyn, the muscle, the rattled delegation — was their work. They did The Starling for Seva.

Alric is still for a moment. This is the second time in the act his composure cracks slightly. Then he says something precise and damaging: "So she used you to create the problem and now I am asking you to fix it. I wonder if she planned that."

He does not withdraw the mission. He recalibrates. He is now looking at the party differently — not with distrust exactly, but with the assessment of someone updating their model. He may adjust the offer. He will definitely remember this conversation.

Oskar Sternov — The Honeywell Office
Wants Alric Venn removed before the signing
▼
The setting

The Honeywell office, after hours. The forge below is banked but still warm. He does not offer them a seat. He stands at his desk, the annotated Trumpet article nowhere in sight tonight. He gets straight to it.

His justification

He tells them about the Driftwood Road. He does not call it a toll operation. He calls it the only thing standing between Tallowhaven craftspeople and a flood of cheap unguilded imports. The road was protecting livelihoods. Guild members. People with families. Alric knew exactly what the road meant and he sent the party to dismantle it anyway.

Tomorrow Alric signs a deal that will enrich the Merchant Guild and hollow out everything the Trademasters have spent a generation building. Prices will drop. Standards will drop. The guild certification that guarantees quality — that the party has seen in operation on the Driftwood Road — will become worthless.

He is not angry in any visible way. He is stating facts. Alric has to go before that signature goes down. He says it exactly like that.

Oskar I am not asking you to enjoy this. I am asking you to understand what is at stake. The road was protecting people. Alric knew that. So did you.
What the party recognises

The road Oskar is describing is the Driftwood Road mission. That was their work. They cleared it for Alric. Oskar is telling them that the thing they did for money is the direct cause of the harm he is now asking them to prevent — and he is telling them this while asking them to fix it.

The line so did you may or may not land depending on whether Oskar has worked out their involvement. He has not confirmed it. But the party will feel it.

What Alric says if the party goes to him
Alric's defence — if confronted

Alric does not deny that the Driftwood deal will affect local suppliers. He frames it as progress. Tallowhaven cannot remain an island. The guild protection clause the Trademasters are pushing would make the city economically uncompetitive within a generation. He is protecting the city's future at the cost of some short-term disruption to guild interests.

He is persuasive. He is not entirely wrong. The party will find it difficult to know what to believe.

If the party confesses their involvement in clearing the road he absorbs this without visible reaction. He then says: "You did what you were paid to do. That is not something I hold against you." He means it, which is almost worse.

The three options
3a — Take the mission ▼

The party agrees to remove Alric before the signing. Oskar pays half immediately. He tells them Alric will be at the Shallow Tuber making final preparations. He expects the party at the signing the following day as part of the Trademaster delegation.

He does not say kill. He says removed from the equation. He does not answer follow-up questions about method. If pushed he says: "Use your judgement. You have shown me you have some."

Consequences: Alric's absence from the meeting leaves the Merchant delegation without its architect. Huw is present but cannot lead. The Driftwood counterpart is confused and alarmed. The Trademaster clause gains ground in the vacuum. The collapse still comes — the Courtesans see an opening — but the Merchants are on the back foot from the start.

3b — Double cross — warn Alric ▼

The party takes the briefing and goes to Alric. He listens with the expression of someone who is not particularly surprised. He asks one question: what exactly did Oskar say.

He thanks them with genuine warmth that the party may find unsettling given what he will now do with the information. At the signing Alric arrives knowing Oskar tried to have him removed. He has made arrangements. The Trademaster representative at the table finds the morning considerably more difficult than anticipated.

Oskar will know the party warned Alric before the signing is over. He will not forget it.

3c — Confess to Oskar ▼

The party tells Oskar that the road he is describing — the toll operation they dismantled — was their work. They did the Driftwood Road mission for Alric.

Oskar is quiet for a long moment. Then: "I know." He has known since shortly after it happened. He waited to see if they would tell him. The fact that they have changes his assessment of them — not enough to withdraw the mission, but enough to speak more plainly than he otherwise would.

He tells them that the road was the only card the Trademasters had left. They played it and lost. Now he is asking them to make it right. Whether they consider that a reasonable ask is up to them.

Seva Sable — Private Room, Amber Cup
Wants Oskar Sternov removed before the signing
▼
The setting

A private room above the Amber Cup. Mira is outside the door. Seva is already there when the party arrives, seated, unhurried. She has been thinking about this conversation for some time.

Her justification

She tells them what Oskar did to Calla and Perran Carew. A journalist doing her job and a young man who, whatever his faults, did not deserve what happened in that establishment. Oskar sent people to intimidate, to silence, to ensure that the story about the quality of Tallowhaven goods — the unguilded imports, the inflated local prices, the protection racket dressed up as guild pride — never reached the public.

She is not emotional about it. She is precise. The Trademasters are not protecting Tallowhaven. They are protecting themselves. The guild certification system exists to exclude competition and justify prices that ordinary people in this city cannot afford. Oskar will do anything — including violence — to keep that system intact.

She needs him gone before the signing. She says it plainly and does not reach for a euphemism. She watches the party's faces as she says it.

Seva I am not asking you to enjoy this either. But I am asking you to consider what it means that he sent people to hurt a journalist and a boy. And then ask yourself what he will do next if nobody stops him.
What the party recognises

The journalist and the boy — Calla and Perran — are The Trumpet. That was their mission. They did that for Oskar. The pub fight, the intimidation, the silencing of the story — all of it was their work. Seva is describing the consequences of their second mission as the moral justification for asking them to commit murder.

This is the sharpest version of the chickens coming home to roost. Perran Carew specifically. The party put him on the floor of The Establishment. Seva is now describing what was done to him as the reason Oskar has to die.

What Oskar says if the party goes to him
Oskar's defence — if confronted

Oskar does not deny that things got out of hand at The Establishment. He says the intention was to discourage a fabricated story from running — a story commissioned by the Courtesans, incidentally, not discovered by a diligent journalist. He did not authorise anyone to be seriously hurt.

He then says something that is honest in a way that is not entirely comfortable: the Courtesans have been running Tallowhaven's information economy for twenty years. Every piece of leverage in this city passes through Seva's hands at some point. That is not a network. That is a stranglehold. And she will use it to kill the Trademaster clause because the clause limits her.

If the party confesses their involvement in The Trumpet he does not react visibly. He says: "Then you know what the story was actually about. And you know who commissioned it." He lets that sit.

The three options
3a — Take the mission ▼

The party agrees to remove Oskar before the signing. Seva pays half immediately — more than the other factions, without being asked. She tells them Oskar will be at the Honeywell office until late, finalising the Trademaster position for tomorrow. She expects the party at the signing the following day as guests of the hosting arrangement.

She does not say kill. She says make sure he is not at that table tomorrow. She does not elaborate. If pushed she meets their eyes and says nothing further. The silence is the answer.

Consequences: Oskar's absence removes the Trademaster clause's main champion from the table. The guild representatives present cannot hold the position without him. The Driftwood counterpart, relieved, moves toward Alric's terms. The Courtesans read the room and begin collecting exactly the information they came for. The collapse still comes — Alric eventually notices what the network is doing — but the Merchants and Trademasters both feel outmanoeuvred.

3b — Double cross — warn Oskar ▼

The party takes the briefing and goes to Oskar. He receives the warning with the expression of someone who suspected this was coming and is mildly interested to have it confirmed. He asks two questions: what did Seva say exactly, and what does the party want.

He does not offer money. He offers something more specific — a promise that if the Trademaster clause passes tomorrow the party will have standing with the guild that money cannot buy. Whether that means anything to them is their decision.

At the signing Oskar arrives knowing Seva tried to have him removed. He is grimly prepared. The Courtesan network finds its usual methods unusually ineffective during the meeting. Seva will know the party warned him before the morning is over. The look she gives them across the room when she realises says everything.

3c — Confess to Seva ▼

The party tells Seva that the events at The Establishment — Calla, Perran, the fight — were their work. They did The Trumpet for Oskar.

Seva looks at them for a long moment. Then she nods once, slowly, the way someone nods when they have just had a suspicion confirmed. She says: "I wondered." She does not say what she wondered, or what conclusion she drew, or what she intends to do with the information.

She does not withdraw the mission. She adjusts her read of the party significantly. From this point forward she will treat them as people whose loyalty is genuinely uncertain — which means she will be more careful, more specific, and more watchful. That is not entirely a bad thing for the party. Seva paying close attention to someone is its own kind of currency.

DM Notes
The party caused thisEvery justification in this scene traces back to a second mission the party completed. The Starling led to Alric's grievance. The Driftwood Road led to Oskar's. The Trumpet led to Seva's. The party is the mechanism through which all three faction grievances were created. They may not realise this immediately. Let them work it out. Do not point it out.
Take out is always ambiguousNone of the three faction leaders use the word kill. They say removed, gone, unable to attend, not at that table. The party has to decide what they are willing to do and live with the ambiguity of the instruction. A party that detains rather than kills has still completed the mission as far as the faction leader is concerned — as long as the target is absent from the signing.
The confess optionThis is available in every scene and changes the texture of the briefing significantly. Oskar already knows. Alric absorbs it without visible reaction. Seva files it. None of them withdraw the mission. All of them update their assessment of the party permanently.
The target's defenceNone of the three targets are innocent. All three are partially culpable for the situation that led to the assassination attempt against them. The party warning a target is not warning an innocent person — it is warning someone who played a role in creating the conditions that made the attempt inevitable. Let the party sit with that.
What happens nextWhatever the party chooses tonight, the signing happens tomorrow. The encounter file for the signing covers the collapse and its aftermath. The party's choices here determine the shape of that collapse — who is in the room, who knows what, and who has been betrayed by whom before anyone sits down.
