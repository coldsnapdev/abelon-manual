Mission Reference — Act 1 / Merchants / Second Mission
The Driftwood Point Supply Problem
Two barricades on the road — one a bluff, one a wall
Investigation then two checkpointsLevel 3 partySecond checkpoint not meant to be foughtHidden Trademaster connection (Part 3)

Alric asks the party to find out why merchants trading with Driftwood Point haven't received their goods. Three merchant interviews point the party down the road toward the real cause -- but first they hit a band of opportunists chancing a toll under a borrowed name, unconnected to the actual problem. Further on is the real obstacle: a bridge with a deliberately dropped span, held by a logistics company whose own staff know exactly how to fix it and aren't authorised to -- because someone with an interest in keeping Driftwood cut off is leaning on that authorisation. The first checkpoint is a fight or a bluff. The second isn't built to be won by force at all.

At a glance
Part 1 — Investigation
The brief: Alric names three affected merchants and pays half upfront. He doesn't tell the party where to look beyond that.
What the merchants add up to: a blocked road, running ten days, blocking both directions -- and a name, Cadog Melyn, attached to whatever's stopping traffic
Anwen: a courier who paid a toll rather than turn back -- the best source of tactical detail if the party finds her
Part 2 — The Fake Barricade
Who they are: opportunists hired by Cadog Melyn, a landowner, to chance their arm playing checkpoint -- his name is real, their authority isn't
Radek: the leader, genuinely enjoys the power more than the legal argument -- his tell is landing one extra hit he didn't need to
Escalation: starts as a transaction (Peck handles it), becomes an argument (Radek steps in), becomes a fight with backstops closing from both ends of the bottleneck
What it isn't: the actual cause of the supply problem -- clearing this doesn't fix anything further down the road
Part 3 — The Real Barricade
Who they are: Halloway Ops, a logistics company, guarding a bridge with a deliberately dropped hinge section -- not thieves, not saboteurs, a company stuck
The real problem: Ops knows exactly how to fix the bridge and doesn't have authorisation to. On the surface, bureaucratic paralysis -- a mirror of the campaign's paralysis theme.
The hidden layer: someone with a stake in keeping Driftwood cut off is leaning on that authorisation -- Trademaster-connected, and not confirmed to the party this mission unless they dig
Four gates: Perrin (entry), Devrin (the authorisation wall), Yselle (confirms the stakes), Tobin (technical detail, if engaged rather than interrogated -- his craftsmanship pride is the social route to the hidden layer)
If it comes to force: not a fair fight -- built as an unmistakable wall, numbers over quality, one clear warning before anything is loosed, nonlethal first if pushed past that
Location Descriptions
The road to Driftwood Point

Open country giving way to forest the further out you go.

The Fake Barricade

A natural bottleneck -- bridge, ford, forest track, or rocky defile depending on the map. A cart waits, paperwork changes hands, and it looks like a checkpoint rather than an ambush until it isn't.

The Real Barricade

A long wooden and stone bridge spanning a deep forested ravine. One section near the middle is hinged and dropped open, leaving a gap -- the far half hangs at an angle rather than being destroyed. A small cluster of Halloway tents, scaffolding, and storage structures sits on the near side. Steep rocky walls drop to a thin river far below. A close look at the hinge mechanism itself (DC 16–18 Investigation or Perception, harder while working through the scaffolding and barricades) reveals more than just that it was deliberately dropped -- the craftsmanship is distinctive enough to half-guess who built it before anyone confirms anything socially.

NPCs
Mared Ffion Silvermark Jeweller — composed, precise, gives the Anwen lead
▼
Personality points

Polite because the party comes from Alric, but the politeness has an edge -- precise rather than warm. States facts without embellishment; they're damning enough on their own.

What she knows

Rare coastal gems, expected ten days ago, not arrived. A Driftwood Point courier (Anwen) got through last week with someone else's goods and mentioned a checkpoint -- Mared didn't follow up herself, but tells the party now.

Goals for this encounter

Wants results, and is watching to see if the party is worth her time.

Osip Baran Butcher — direct, frustrated, seeds doubt about Alric
▼
Personality points

Not angry at the party, angry at the situation -- blunt and loud, warms up quickly if taken seriously.

What he knows

Deliveries stopped about ten days ago -- he's been counting, which pins the timeline. Recalls a previous northern supply route Alric championed that went nowhere.

What he keeps secret

Not accusing Alric of dishonesty -- just optimism that costs other people money. Line: “Not dishonest. Just optimistic in ways that cost other people money.”

Lande & Caeryn Fabled Furnitures — both directions blocked
▼
Lande

An elf furniture maker, quietly furious rather than panicking -- more unsettling than Osip's bluntness. Assesses the party before deciding how much to share. Both her outgoing shipment and an incoming one are blocked; she produces a ledger with Cadog Melyn's name written down from something overheard.

Caeryn

Lande's husband, the courier who was turned back. More openly annoyed, less guarded than Lande. Describes the checkpoint's location, people, and paperwork -- the man running it engaged with his arguments, then made clear the arguments weren't the point.

Anwen Driftwood Point courier — paid the toll, killing time before heading back
▼
Visual description

Found at lunch or dinner at a specific establishment if the party follows the lead and asks the right vendor or innkeeper.

Personality points

Guarded at first, warms up once she understands the party wants to fix the problem, not assign blame. Dark humour of someone who's had a genuinely bad week. Line, mid-vent: “That, plus the fact that every tradey in this town is looking at me like Sekarth himself, I'd say this has been a trip to quickly forget.”

What she knows

Checkpoint location, roughly how many people, the paperwork, Cadog Melyn's name (independently corroborating Lande's ledger).

Radek & Peck The Fake Barricade — leader and negotiator
▼
Radek (leader)

Genuinely enjoys having power over people at a bottleneck more than he cares about the legal argument, though he uses it. Engages seriously with counter-arguments, which is unsettling. Tell: lands one extra hit he didn't need to, early, before the fight even starts.

Combat -- Radek

AC 16, HP 52. Greataxe +6, 1d12+4 slashing (extra attack on a crit). Action Surge (1/short rest), Second Wind (1d10+4 as bonus action, 1/short rest). Intimidation +5, used actively -- failed save gives the target disadvantage on their next attack against him. Fights with enjoyment, not efficiency; last to go down, not gracefully.

Peck (negotiator)

Quick-witted, faintly absurdist gnome bard who delivers the toll demand with practiced comic timing, then steps back the moment combat starts -- not cowardice, professionalism. Exit line: something theatrical about preferring not to get blood on the ledger. If Radek falls and Peck's still standing, he puts his hands up with professional detachment -- no loyalty beyond the job.

Combat -- Peck

AC 13, HP 22. Vicious Mockery (DC 13), Dissonant Whispers (DC 13), Healing Word on self/allies, Bardic Inspiration (1d6, 1/short rest). Steps back from melee, uses Vicious Mockery from range if directly threatened -- priority is survival, not victory.

Morda, Brek & Gort The Fake Barricade — backstops and muscle
▼
Morda (Tallowhaven-side backstop)

Half-orc ranger, appears behind the party once they're committed. AC 14, HP 32. Shortsword +5 (1d6+3) / Longbow +5 (1d8+3, 150/600ft). Hunter's Mark, Colossus Slayer (+1d8 vs a target below max HP).

Brek (Driftwood-side backstop)

Older dwarf ranger holding the coast-facing end, clear professional shorthand with Morda. AC 15, HP 38. Handaxe +4 (1d6+2) / Longbow +4 (1d8+2, 150/600ft). Hunter's Mark, Dwarven Resilience.

Gort

Orc fighter standing with Radek, entirely reliable, no interest in the argument -- just the work. AC 15, HP 38. Longsword +5 (1d8+3). Relentless Endurance (drops to 1 HP instead of 0, 1/long rest), Second Wind.

Thessaly The Fake Barricade — magic user, held back, complicated feelings
▼
Personality points

Set back or concealed, may go unnoticed until a spell lands. Not cruel like Radek -- just here, doing a job, trying not to think too hard about it.

Combat

AC 12, HP 26. Thunderwave (DC 13, 2d8, pushes 10ft), Hold Person (DC 13), Misty Step (bonus action, 30ft), Magic Missile (3 darts, 1d4+1 each, auto-hit).

Perrin The Real Barricade — checkpoint guard, gates entry
▼
Visual description

Practical leather and cloth uniform in muted colours, a simple company badge -- workwear, not armour.

Personality points

Calm, slightly bored, the look of someone who's repeated the same line many times today. His patter about the bridge being out echoes Radek's crew's own line at the fake barricade -- a party who's already heard one rehearsed deflection will clock the shape of a second immediately, for entirely different reasons underneath.

What he knows

Won't confirm or deny that repair work is happening. Understated about it every time, someone who isn't going to be the one who slips.

Devrin Kass The Real Barricade — Ops lead, gates escalation
▼
Visual description

Practical fitted leather gear built for movement and authority rather than ceremony, a company crest on a shoulder strap. Early forties, sharp watchful eyes.

Goals for this encounter

Hold the checkpoint and keep the situation from escalating -- genuinely doesn't want anyone hurt on this contract.

What she knows

The actual cause: Ops knows exactly how to repair the dropped span and doesn't have sign-off to do it. Reveals this if pushed past the checkpoint patter -- competent people prevented from acting by a structure above them, not by any lack of ability or will.

Personality points

Competent and weary, the bearing of someone managing skilled people rather than commanding soldiers. Escalates deliberately and reluctantly, not eagerly -- gives one clear verbal warning, weapons drawn but held, before anything is loosed.

Yselle Corrin The Real Barricade — site manager, confirms the stakes
▼
Visual description

Well-tailored travelling clothes rather than armour, a small company emblem pin. Composed, slightly detached.

What she knows

Confirms Halloway's stake in the crossing and the Driftwood angle -- more comfortable with contracts than confrontation, and will talk business readily if approached as a business conversation rather than an interrogation.

Tobin The Real Barricade — craftsman, the social route to the hidden layer
▼
Personality points

Proud rather than cautious, the opposite of Perrin's rehearsed neutrality -- wants credit for good craftsmanship even when he probably shouldn't be discussing it. Flattery or genuine shared interest in how the hinge mechanism works can get him talking shop well past what he should share.

What he knows

The most detailed technical picture of the bridge and the dropped span available from anyone at the checkpoint. Pushed far enough on genuine shop talk, he's the party's route to an unguarded mention of who actually spec'd the mechanism's design or where the parts came from -- the Trademaster connection, if the party doesn't find it through pure inspection first.

Encounter Beats
I1 Alric's brief ▼

Alric describes it as a matter of guild reputation -- merchants who trusted him are losing money in the most visible trading week of the year. He names three merchants (Mared, Osip, Lande), mentions as an aside that Lande had issues going the other way too, pays half upfront, and leaves the legwork to the party.

I2 The merchant conversations ▼

Visitable in any order. Mared gives the Anwen lead; Osip gives the ten-day timeline and doubt about Alric; Lande and Caeryn give both-directions confirmation, Cadog Melyn's name, and a ground-level account of a checkpoint further down the road.

I3 Finding Anwen ▼

A successful conversation with a vendor or innkeeper who deals with Driftwood Point traders locates her at a meal, killing time before heading back. She fills in tactical detail -- headcount, paperwork, the checkpoint's manner -- and independently corroborates Cadog Melyn's name.

C1 The Fake Barricade -- arrival ▼

It looks like a checkpoint, not an ambush -- a cart waiting, Peck visible and official-looking. Morda is behind the party before they realise it (DC 14 Perception to spot early); Brek is ahead past the bottleneck; Thessaly isn't immediately obvious (DC 16). A prepared party gets the advantage of noticing.

C2 The Fake Barricade -- the transaction, then the argument ▼

Peck explains the toll with practiced ease -- Cadog Melyn's name, a maintenance fee, entirely standard-sounding. If the party challenges the legal basis, he handles it pleasantly; if they push harder, Radek enters and engages with the argument genuinely, which is unsettling. At some point -- ideally sparked by an impatient waiting carter -- he does something one beat more than necessary. Let that land before anything else happens.

C3 The Fake Barricade -- the fight ▼

Morda and Brek close from both ends. Thessaly's first spell lands from an unexpected angle, especially if the Perception checks failed. Gort moves with Radek; Peck steps aside and finds somewhere to be. Radek uses Action Surge when challenged or losing, talks throughout, and is the last down -- not gracefully. Clearing this checkpoint, however it's resolved, doesn't touch the real cause further down the road.

R1 The Real Barricade -- arrival ▼

The road opens onto the ravine and the bridge: a long span with one section visibly hinged and dropped, the far half hanging at an angle. Halloway tents and scaffolding cluster on the near side. Perrin meets the party at the checkpoint with the same understated refusal to confirm anything he's given everyone else today.

R2 The real cause, if pushed ▼

Past Perrin's patter, Devrin surfaces the surface-level truth: Ops knows exactly how to fix the span and doesn't have authorisation from above to do it. This isn't evasion -- it's genuinely the reason the road has been closed for ten days, as far as Devrin herself understands it. Yselle, approached as a business conversation, confirms Halloway's stake in the crossing and why Driftwood trade specifically matters to the company. Tobin, engaged rather than interrogated, is the richest source at the checkpoint for what's actually underneath the authorisation problem -- flattery or genuine curiosity about the hinge mechanism can get him talking about who actually specified the design or where the parts came from, further than he probably should.

R3 If it comes to force ▼

This is not designed to be a fair fight, and it is not designed to be winnable by force. Halloway's checkpoint is built as an unmistakable wall -- a visible line of longbow-armed personnel with real sightlines and elevation, plus enough melee-capable staff nearby to matter, reading as overwhelming the moment initiative is rolled. Give the party one clear off-ramp before it fully commits: an explicit verbal warning, weapons drawn and held rather than loosed, a genuine “you do not want to do this” moment. If the party pushes past that warning, Halloway uses nonlethal methods first wherever plausible -- subduing rather than killing, staying true to a company that avoids dead bodies on its own contract. Capture or a forced, humiliating retreat is the intended outcome of a fight here, not a body count.

The real barricade can't be solved by force at all; it needs the party to actually engage with Devrin's authorisation problem, one way or another.
The hidden Trademaster layer lives here, not at the fake barricadeDevrin's authorisation block is real from her point of view, but someone with a stake in keeping the Driftwood route cut off is applying pressure above her -- Trademaster-connected. This ties directly to what Oskar says at the Evening Invitations about the road “protecting Tallowhaven craftspeople from a flood of cheap unguilded imports”: an open route threatens Trademaster livelihoods, which is exactly why it's staying stalled. Not confirmed to the party this mission by default -- Tobin's craftsmanship pride is the social route to an unguarded mention of who actually spec'd the mechanism, and a DC 16–18 inspection of the hinge is the technical route to the same suspicion. Either, both, or neither can land depending on how the party plays it.

A party that's already heard one version should feel the shape of a second one land differently, precisely because the truth underneath is completely different.
The authorisation gate is worth exactly what it looks like -- until it isn'tOps knowing exactly how to fix the bridge but lacking sign-off reads at first as a small, direct mirror of the campaign's paralysis theme -- competent people blocked by structure, not by ability or will. Let it sit there as that on the surface. Whether the party ever learns it's being actively engineered from above is their choice to dig for, not something to volunteer.
