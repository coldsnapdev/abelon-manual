Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Act 1 Closing
The Arrest and The Gaol
Caen Rhys ends it — a night in the cells — morning and the road east
No combatAct 1 closeAct 2 seedFollows Festival Finale

Caen Rhys walks into the aftermath of the Cooper's Square fight with no army, just a frightened crowd and his own authority, and offers the party the version of this evening that ends least badly: a night in the cells, then exile with provisions rather than a trial. Marek is already in the cell when they arrive, and a quiet, unforced conversation with him plants Crossroads and the Lamplighters without ever asking the party to go there. In the morning, Caen Rhys sends them east with a cart, and Act 1 ends on the road.

At a glance
The crowd: thirty or forty ordinary Tallowhaven residents, frightened and watching, not advancing -- a moral weight, not a threat. Attacking them or Caen Rhys ends the campaign's relationship with the city permanently.
Caen Rhys's offer: come quietly, spend the night in the cells, leave in the morning with a cart and four days of provisions. “Exile without a road is abandonment. I am not that kind of magistrate.”
Marek in the cell: not performing anything -- notes the pattern of the party's third arrest-adjacent encounter with him, without lecturing. Mentions Crossroads and Turian of the Lamplighters once, in passing comparison, and moves on.
The Marek question: at dawn, Caen Rhys asks flatly whether Marek is with the party. Either answer is valid and left open for Act 2.
Location Descriptions
Cooper's Square, aftermath

Torchlight, the smell of festival still in the air, the evidence of a fight -- fallen weapons, an overturned stall. Thirty or forty frightened residents at the edges, some recognisable from the Bardic Interlude earlier that evening.

The cells, below the Aldermen's Hall

Stone, low ceilings, damp and old wood. Two cells behind iron bars, basic but not cruel -- a bench, a blanket, a bucket. Built for overnight holding, not long sentences.

NPCs
Caen Rhys Magistrate — ends Act 1, opens the road east
▼
Visual description

Arrives on foot from the civic quarter, Pren and Tev a step behind, Oswin further back, hand nowhere near his weapon.

Goals for this encounter

Remove the party from Tallowhaven before three damaged factions need somewhere to put tonight's anger -- not a punishment, in his own account, but a practical decision.

What he knows

The trade deal is dead, Aldous Crane already gone. Has no standing army and no way to compel compliance -- only legitimacy and the crowd behind him.

Personality points

Completely, actually calm -- decided before he arrived. In the square: “Are you going to kill all of them too?” ... “I think you've made your point.” ... “Come with me. Before this gets worse.” Starts walking without checking if they follow. If pressed on why he's helping them: “Exile without a road is abandonment. I am not that kind of magistrate.” If challenged on fairness: “They'll answer for what they did. In time, and in the way cities answer for things. Slowly, incompletely, and without the satisfaction of anyone who deserved better.”

Marek Talan Fallen paladin — on his third arrest with this party's involvement
▼
Visual description

Already in the cell, sitting on the bench with no particular objection to sitting on benches. Looks at the party with mild interest, then away.

What he knows

Roughly what the party has been doing all week -- word moves through a city during a festival. Not impressed, not contemptuous, just doesn't think it reaches far enough.

Personality points

Not performing anything -- not grateful, hostile, or interested. Anger sits below a calm, tired surface, aimed at the shape of things rather than the party specifically. Line, if the conversation reaches their week: “You did what you could. The same as the people in Crossroads. Good work, careful work, and it reaches as far as it reaches. Which is not very far. Which is the problem.” Then, once, unprompted further: “There were people there six months ago who thought the same way you do. Ask Turian of the Lamplighters what difference it made. If you find yourself that way.” He does not explain further and does not return to it. Answers questions about the broken-wing gesture, his missing fingers, or his faith briefly and accurately, without much investment.

Encounter Beats
1 The aftermath, and Caen Rhys arrives ▼

Give the party a quiet moment first -- the faction crew down or gone, the square quiet the way places are after something loud stops. Then the crowd becomes visible at the edges, some faces recognisable from the Bardic Interlude. Let that register before Caen Rhys crosses the square, looks at what's happened, looks at the crowd, looks at the party, and delivers his three lines without pause between them before turning to walk back toward the Aldermen's Hall -- not checking whether they follow.

2 If the party doesn't follow ▼

He stops, and when he turns his expression is unchanged -- tired, decided, unafraid: “There is no version of this evening that ends well for you if you stay in this square. I am offering you the version that ends least badly.” The offer has a short life. If they still don't follow, the crowd simply closes in by existing in greater numbers, not aggressively. The party can leave Tallowhaven that night without the cells -- but loses the gaol scene, the conversation with Marek, and the cart. Attacking Caen Rhys or the crowd is not a recoverable position and ends the campaign's relationship with Tallowhaven permanently; play the consequences honestly.

3 Arrival in the cells ▼

Tev brings the party down. Marek is already there and watches them arrive; once Tev leaves, he acknowledges the pattern in one sentence -- the third time their paths have crossed this way -- then looks away. He doesn't need the conversation to happen. If the party doesn't engage, they simply get the morning with Caen Rhys and leave.

4 The conversation, if the party engages ▼

Marek answers questions precisely and briefly, without volunteering more than asked or performing warmth. If the conversation reaches what the party actually did this week, he responds with something like his comparison to Crossroads -- not a lecture, a genuine parallel to something he's already watched happen. Crossroads and Turian arrive in that context, mentioned once and left alone. He answers anything about the gesture, his hands, or his faith plainly, without much investment either way.

5 Before dawn -- Caen Rhys's terms ▼
Cart east, Marek with themThe most complete version -- provisions, a road, and a travelling companion who's already mentioned Crossroads and Turian without quite meaning to send them there. Act 2 begins in motion, with Marek as a live wire.
Cart east, Marek not with themA clean departure. Crossroads is still in play if the party remembered the mention; Marek remains a loose thread somewhere on the road.
Left the square without the cellsNo cart, no provisions beyond what they carry, and no gaol conversation -- the road east is still there, and Crossroads can be introduced by other means, just without this context.

He arrives before first light, clearly having not slept, and states the situation plainly through the bars: “The trade deal is finished. Aldous Crane left before the square. The festival ends this morning and when it does, three factions with damaged pride and a dead agreement are going to need somewhere to put what happened. I need you not to be here when that starts.” A cart with four days of provisions waits on the south road until sunrise, then won't.

He turns to Marek, then back to the party, without addressing Marek directly: “Is he with you?” Marek doesn't answer for himself -- mildly interested, waiting to see how this gets decided for him. Either yes or no is valid; if yes, he takes the cart east with them, if no, Tev releases him separately later that morning.

DM Notes
The crowd as mechanismNot a threat -- a moral weight. The party cannot attack thirty unarmed civilians and remain the protagonists of their own story. Most players will feel this without being told.
Caen Rhys is not a villainA pragmatist doing the only available thing in an impossible political situation, and he knows the exile serves the city more than the party. He doesn't apologise or pretend otherwise -- he can say “this is not fair and it is necessary” without treating those as a contradiction.
Marek's lack of self-awarenessHe doesn't know what he actually left behind in Crossroads -- he remembers people who wanted change, and sends the party toward the consequences of his own ideas without knowing those consequences exist. Don't collapse the dramatic irony by having him hint at self-knowledge he doesn't have.
Turian and the LamplightersA name and a group, mentioned once and not repeated. If asked, Marek's answer is brief: people he met, people who believed in what they were doing, worth finding if the party ends up that way. Crossroads delivers the full weight of it later -- plant it and leave it.
The cart doesn't answer questionsFour days of provisions, road food, water, basic bedding, a working horse. No note, no explanation. Caen Rhys arranged it and has nothing more to say.
The road to CrossroadsThe cart points south out of Tallowhaven; the road east branches within a day's travel, several days at road speed from Crossroads. Use the unscripted travel time for watch rotations, camp conversations, and the first signs the landscape is changing -- Act 2 begins here, before it officially starts.
