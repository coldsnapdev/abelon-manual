Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Merchants
The Shallow Tuber & The Copper Vine
Breakfast with Alric Venn, then collecting his debt from Caspar Veld
Social encounterNo combat expectedMoral weight: highPaid in advance

Alric Venn buys the party breakfast, sizes them up over questions that feel like small talk, and offers them a straightforward-sounding job: collect an overdue levy from a wine merchant, Caspar Veld, who's been dodging him. He pays half upfront -- a deal is a deal. At the Copper Vine, Caspar runs through a practiced routine of deflection before calling the party's bluff outright, and the party has to decide how far they're actually willing to go to collect a debt that was never really about them.

Location Descriptions
The Shallow Tuber

A respectable eating establishment where guild business gets done over food without anyone calling it a meeting. Clean tablecloths, unhurried service, a menu that changes with the season -- comfortable rather than grand, and exactly the kind of place Alric uses for this purpose. Morning light, a low murmur of other diners' quiet business making private conversation easy.

The Copper Vine

A wine shop on a side street off the main trading quarter, slightly more decorated than its neighbours -- a professionally painted sign, window boxes, the smell of wine and oak reaching the party before they open the door. Dim and pleasantly cool inside. Bottles line every wall, on shelves, in racks, on the counter. Everything in here is fragile, and both the party and Caspar know it.

NPCs
Alric Venn Merchants' Guildmaster — systems thinker, fair payer
▼
Visual description

Fifties, well dressed without ostentation -- the kind of wealth that doesn't need to announce itself. Thinks in systems rather than people.

Goals for this encounter

Confirm his read on the party from the Aldermen's Hall, then get his overdue levy collected from Caspar Veld without having to deal with it personally.

What he knows

Owes the Aldermen's Hall a festival levy, and has negotiated grace from Caen Rhys pending collection from Caspar -- a known dodger. The money flows through him to the city; he isn't enriching himself.

What he keeps secret

Doesn't mention that Caspar may resist, that the party has no real leverage beyond implication, or that he's watching closely how they handle it. Describes the job only as it looks on the surface.

Personality points

Pomposity present but not aggressive -- slightly posh, slightly presumptuous (orders for the whole table without asking), and has stopped noticing how rich he is relative to most people around him. Considers prompt payment a point of personal honour and pays before anyone leaves the table. His response afterward is a mirror, not a judgement -- he reflects the temperature of what the party did rather than moralising about it, and never asks what happened.

Huw Alric's aide — present, says little
▼
Visual description

Sits slightly back from the table with documents, a cup, and a stack of papers he's pretending to read.

Goals for this encounter

Watch the party carefully -- he's been told that's part of his job.

Personality points

Polite and slightly stiff if addressed directly, refers anything substantive back to Alric.

Caspar Veld Wine merchant — the debtor
▼
Visual description

Mid-forties, well dressed in a way that suggests he considers himself a cut above the average trader. Clean hands, good boots, warmth that has a practiced quality.

Goals for this encounter

Delay and deflect for as long as possible, then pivot to a moral argument if he runs out of road -- anything to avoid actually paying.

What he knows

Knows he owes the debt, and has no genuine dispute -- only deflections. Has been through this before with Alric's people and considers it a normal part of doing business.

What he keeps secret

Nothing hidden, exactly -- his indignation and reassurance are a script he runs on autopilot, not a real objection.

Personality points

Charming and performative, greeting everyone like a potential customer; the smile fades under pressure but doesn't disappear. Cornered, his speech is his weapon, partly genuine and partly calculated: “So what happens now then? You're going to smash up my shop? Rough me up a bit? All so you can hand the money directly to a man who'll use it to pay a bill he owes to a building? You came in here as errand runners and now you're deciding how far you'll go. That's worth knowing about yourselves, isn't it?” Afterward, watches whoever leaves with the expression of a man filing something away -- not an enemy, and might even respect them depending on how it went.

Encounter Beats
1 Breakfast begins ▼

Alric is already seated when the party arrives, rises briefly, and has ordered food for everyone without asking -- considerate or presumptuous depending on disposition. Huw sits slightly back with his papers.

2 The questions ▼

Alric asks questions that feel like small talk -- where they're from, how they found the escort work, what they made of Tallowhaven -- and listens with the attentiveness of someone building a file rather than making conversation. The interest is genuine; the purpose behind it isn't announced.

3 The offer and payment ▼

Satisfied with what he's heard, Alric makes the offer: the Merchants' Guild collects levies from sanctioned traders, and Caspar Veld at the Copper Vine has an outstanding balance he's been dodging by letter. A personal visit is required. Alric describes Caspar as a known rascal who needs chasing -- without heat, as fact, and not wrong.

He pays before anyone leaves the table, taking a small purse from Huw and setting it down without ceremony. A deal is a deal, and this is the most important mechanical moment of the morning: the party now has money they haven't earned, and no clean way to walk away from the job.

4 The entrance at the Copper Vine ▼

The shop smells good. Caspar emerges from the back expecting to be pleased by whoever's arrived, friendly and performative, gesturing at a bottle he's proud of this week. The moment Alric's name comes up, the warmth doesn't disappear -- it becomes a different, more practiced kind of warm.

5 The routine, and the push ▼

Caspar runs his script: mild surprise it's come to this, mild indignation Alric didn't just send a message, mild reassurance it'll be sorted -- he's been meaning to get to it, festival prep has been chaotic, surely they understand. Let him finish before the party cuts through it.

Pushed past reassurance, he recalibrates -- disputes the exact amount to buy a moment, then claims he doesn't have the sum liquid right now. Perhaps next week. The party has reasons not to accept that: Alric's payment to the city, the chain of obligation, the fact that Caspar's been next-week-ing this for longer than reasonable. He runs out of road, and knows it.

6 The speech, and the decision ▼
Success -- money retrieved cleanlyAlric pays the second half without ceremony: “Good. I had a feeling about you.” More work if they want it.
Success -- creative solutionAlric is quietly more interested than he expected, files the approach away without comment, and pays promptly.
Partial -- money retrieved, something brokenAlric pays without ceremony, but mentions carefully that Caspar is a known figure and visibility has costs. Not a reprimand -- information.
Partial -- Caspar seriously hurtAlric goes quiet, asks precise questions, tells them to say nothing and that he'll handle it. The thread is complicated but not necessarily closed.
Caspar deadThe room changes. Alric asks one or two precise questions, tells them to stay available and say nothing to anyone. Not dismissed warmly -- the path isn't closed, but it looks different from here.
Failure -- empty-handedAlric tells them to keep the advance -- it's already gone in his accounting. “I didn't need you to frighten him. I needed to know whether you would when it mattered. Caspar asks that question of everyone eventually. Now I know your answer.” Polite, no scene, door not permanently closed but noticeably less open.

Caspar gestures at the shop, the bottles, the party -- calm, almost reasonable, slightly theatrical: “So what happens now then? You're going to smash up my shop? Rough me up a bit? All so you can hand the money directly to a man who'll use it to pay a bill he owes to a building? You came in here as errand runners and now you're deciding how far you'll go. That's worth knowing about yourselves, isn't it?” He's not wrong. He's also not paying.

The decision belongs entirely to the party. Roughly: back down and leave empty-handed (they still have Alric's advance, and that conversation still has to happen); an implicit threat (pick up a bottle, look at it, put it down somewhere else, say nothing -- costs them something about themselves); an explicit threat (works with higher probability, costs more); or a creative solution -- collateral, a witnessed payment schedule, a third angle Caspar will find harder to argue with than a threat. Reward the unexpected.

DM Notes
Alric's pomposityPlay it as texture, not a flaw -- slightly posh, slightly presumptuous, slightly used to being the most important person in the room. Entirely human, not villainous.
The upfront paymentThe most important mechanical moment in the whole encounter. Don't draw attention to it beyond Alric setting the purse down -- the weight lands later, at the Copper Vine, when Caspar asks what they're going to do about it.
The bottlesNever mention them unless a player does. Their presence is the implicit threat. If a player picks one up deliberately, let the moment breathe -- you don't need to narrate that Caspar noticed.
Alric does not ask howWhen the party reports back, he doesn't ask what happened. If they did something they're not proud of, that stays with them rather than being acknowledged or judged -- the moral weight belongs to the party, not to his response.
Caspar is not a villainA man who's been pushing his luck long enough that it's become his default mode. His speech is partly genuine and he's not wrong about the system -- he's also using the moral argument cynically to avoid a legitimate debt. Both are true at once.
The chain of obligationAlric owes the Aldermen's Hall; Caspar owes Alric; the party is the mechanism that completes the chain. Nobody in it is simply wrong -- the party feels the weight of being a link in it standing in front of Caspar's bottles.
