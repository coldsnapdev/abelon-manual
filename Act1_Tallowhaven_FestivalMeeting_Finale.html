Part One — The Meeting (Aldermen's Hall)
How to run the meetingDo not roleplay the meeting. Set the scene, establish who is in the room, then skip forward. Say something like: "The meeting begins and appears to be progressing smoothly. Aldous Crane presides over the table with the particular authority of someone who considers this a formality. And then — " and drop straight into the accusation.
— The room — Aldermen's Hall, back chambers ▼
Signing roomThe formal chamber with the long table. Blue-floored, well appointed. Alric at one end, Aldous Crane at the other. Caen Rhys presides from the side. Oskar and Anneth seated to one side. Seva's Sables moving quietly as waiting staff.
Waiting roomRight of the signing room. Empty during the meeting — everyone has been called in. Available as an exit route during the collapse.
Kitchen annexUpper right. Where the Sables have been operating. Natural exit for network members when things go wrong — they begin filtering out before the argument has fully escalated.
AnteroomUpper left. Comfortable seating, oval table. The emergency exit is here. Caen Rhys leads the party through this room and out when he decides the situation is irretrievable.
Food prep areaLower left. Where additional faction members have been waiting — catering staff, guild support, network-adjacent personnel. The staging area for everyone not at the table.
The partyStanding with their chosen faction. Visible to everyone in the room from the moment they arrive. Every faction representative begins recalculating the moment they walk in.

The accusation
The triggerThe party walked in standing with one faction. Every other faction representative has been recalculating since that moment. The accusation comes when the accuser finds the right opening — something said by the faction the party sided with that gives them the perfect moment. It should feel inevitable rather than sudden.
A Oskar accuses — party sided with Alric ▼

Alric says something about integrity. About doing things properly, for the good of the city. Oskar sets down what he is holding and turns.

OskarBefore we go any further. Those people standing with Alric. I know them. They came to me. They worked for me. And then they dismantled the only protection my guild members had on that road — on Alric's coin. So when Alric speaks about what is good for this city, perhaps someone should ask him what kind of man hires the same hands to face in different directions.

A pause. Then, to the party directly.

OskarI'll ask you directly. Who are you here for?
A Seva accuses — party sided with Oskar ▼

Seva sets down what she is carrying. Steps out of the waiting staff role entirely. The room notices the shift before she speaks.

SevaI want to name something. Those people — standing there with the Trademasters — came to me not long ago. They sat across from me and took my money. And then they went to The Establishment and put a young man on the floor to silence a story that I had commissioned. A story that was true. I don't know what Oskar paid them. I imagine it was enough.

She waits. Then, to the party.

SevaI'm curious. Was it?
A Alric accuses — party sided with Seva ▼

Alric sets down his papers. Does not lose composure. Addresses the room as host — which makes it worse.

AlricI want to be transparent with our guests from Driftwood. This week, a member of their delegation was caught up in a situation that I can only describe as deliberately engineered. Criminal elements were involved. The matter was resolved — but by whom, I now find myself wondering. Because the people who resolved it are in this room today. As guests of the Courtesans Network. Which raises a question about who created the situation in the first place.

He looks at the party.

AlricI think the room deserves an answer. What exactly is your connection to Seva Sable?
Assassination paths — replacement accusersIf the party killed or warned a faction leader before the meeting, a replacement is present. The accusation still comes — but from a different voice, and with a different quality. Elbeth accuses on suspicion without evidence and knows it. Huw whispers something he did not intend to be heard. Cors is disappointed in Seva and finds his own opening. These accusations are messier than the standard three — which makes them land differently.
A Elbeth accuses — party killed Seva, now stands with Alric (Path 1) ▼

Elbeth is leading the Courtesan delegation in Seva's place. She has been watching the party since they walked in. She does not have evidence — she has suspicion, and suspicion is enough.

She finds her opening when Alric makes a point about order and legitimate commerce. She sets down what she is holding.

ElbethI want to ask something. Those people standing with Alric — I know them. I met them not long ago, in considerably worse circumstances than this. And Seva Sable, who should be standing where I am standing, is not here tonight. I do not know why. But I think someone in this room might.

A pause. She looks at the party directly. She is not certain. She wants them to know she is not certain. She also wants them to know she is watching.

Elbeth — to the partyWhere is Seva?
A Oskar accuses — party warned Alric, now stands with Alric (Path 4) ▼

This is the standard Oskar accusation — the party warned Alric of the assassination attempt, which placed them firmly on Alric's side. Oskar knows what was communicated and he knows what the party did with the information. He is angrier than usual because the warning means he arrived at this table disadvantaged.

Use the standard Oskar accusation text. His delivery has more edge than normal — he has been outplayed and he knows it.

OskarBefore we go any further. Those people standing with Alric. I know them. They came to me. They worked for me. And then they dismantled the only protection my guild members had on that road — on Alric's coin. So when Alric speaks about what is good for this city, perhaps someone should ask him what kind of man hires the same hands to face in different directions.

A pause. Then, to the party.

OskarI'll ask you directly. Who are you here for?
A Huw whispers too loudly — party killed Alric, Caen replaces him (Path 3) ▼

Caen Rhys is present as a civic anchor rather than a merchant — he is holding the deal together in Alric's absence. Huw is at the table beside him, carrying the Merchant Guild's actual position. Huw knows what happened to Alric. He has been managing that knowledge all evening.

During a tense moment in the negotiations, Huw leans toward Caen Rhys and says something intended to be quiet. It is not quiet enough.

Huw — to Caen Rhys, not quite quietly enoughMagistrate — those people standing with Oskar. They were the last ones to see Alric tonight. I need you to understand what I am telling you.

The room goes very still. Huw realises what he has done. Caen Rhys's expression does not change. The accusation is now in the air without anyone having made it formally.

What happens next is chaos — pointed, political, fast. The room does not need Huw to elaborate. They heard enough.

A Cors accuses — party killed Oskar, Anneth leads Trademasters (Path 5) ▼

Anneth Holdworn is leading the Trademaster delegation in Oskar's absence. Cors is beside her. Cors has secret loyalties to the Courtesans — but he is disappointed in Seva for ordering this. He is angry at the party, and he has an opening.

When Seva makes a point about the Trademaster guild's suppression of information, Cors sets down his knife on the table. Deliberately. He looks at the party.

Cors — quietly, but to the roomThe people standing with the Courtesans tonight. Oskar Sternov, who should be sitting where my grandmother is sitting, is dead. I know that. And I know who was seen leaving the Honeywell building last night.

He does not look at Seva. He does not need to. The room reacts before he has finished speaking.

Cors — to the party, flatWas it worth it?
A Seva accuses — party warned Oskar, now stands with Oskar (Path 6) ▼

This is the standard Seva accusation — the party warned Oskar, which placed them on the Trademaster side. Seva arrived knowing what the party did. She has been waiting for her moment.

Use the standard Seva accusation text. Her delivery is colder than usual — she trusted them with a briefing and they used it to protect her target.

SevaI want to name something. Those people — standing there with the Trademasters — came to me not long ago. They sat across from me and took my money. And then they went to The Establishment and put a young man on the floor to silence a story that I had commissioned. A story that was true. I don't know what Oskar paid them. I imagine it was enough.

She waits. Then, to the party.

SevaI'm curious. Was it?
A Alric accuses — party warned Seva, now stands with Seva (Path 2) ▼

This is the standard Alric accusation — the party warned Seva, which placed them on the Courtesan side. Alric arrived knowing what the party did. He has been composing himself all evening.

Use the standard Alric accusation text. His composure is more effortful than usual — he hired them, they warned his target, and they are now standing in his meeting room on the other side.

AlricI want to be transparent with our guests from Driftwood. This week, a member of their delegation was caught up in a situation that I can only describe as deliberately engineered. Criminal elements were involved. The matter was resolved — but by whom, I now find myself wondering. Because the people who resolved it are in this room today. As guests of the Courtesans Network. The collapse
B The room reacts ▼

The party has a moment to respond. It does not matter what they say. The damage is done. The faction leaders begin arguing — pointed, political, escalating. Aldous Crane watches for exactly long enough to confirm his assessment of Tallowhaven, then stands. Calder is already moving. The delegation files out through the main entrance with quiet and permanent finality. Emlyn, if present, goes with them.

The Sables begin filtering out through the kitchen annex. Quietly, efficiently, before the argument has fully escalated. By the time Caen Rhys acts, they are already gone.

Caen Rhys calls for calm. He is ignored. He calls again. He makes a decision — the meeting is lost — and turns to the party.

C The faction they sided with turns on them ▼

As Caen Rhys moves toward the party, the faction that hired them turns. Not physically. A look. A word. Quiet enough that only the party hears it.

Alric — quietlyYou have cost me everything I have spent a year building. I will remember that.
Oskar — quietlyI knew what you were. I gave you the benefit of the doubt. That was my mistake.
Seva — barely audible, from across the roomLeave Tallowhaven tonight. If you are still here tomorrow, that becomes my problem to solve.
D Caen Rhys leads them out ▼
Caen RhysCome with me. Now. This is not a request.

He leads them through the anteroom and out through the emergency exit. He stops at the door.

Caen RhysKeep moving. I will find you before the night is out.

The door closes. The party is in Cooper's Square. The city is going about its evening. For a moment nothing happens. Then the faction comes.

The spaceThe party was here for the Bardic Interlude. Sylas's Step is at the north end. Walls run along the sides. Open at both ends — it channels rather than contains. The faction knows this city. They know where the party will be.

F The faction leader in combat — all paths ▼

The pattern is the same across all three paths. The leader hangs back while their people engage. Once the crew takes significant losses they commit — anger overcoming caution. If they are the last one standing they attempt to flee. Whether the party lets them go carries weight into Act 2.

Alric — picks up a fallen weapon. Has never done this himself. Frightened underneath the fury but will not show it. Fights with desperate energy. Rapier if one is available, otherwise whatever is on the ground.

Oskar — does not need a weapon. Built like someone who has worked with their hands for forty years. Fights close and dirty — grabs, shoves, uses his weight. Cold anger rather than hot. Harder to read than the others.

Seva — exactly as dangerous as the party always suspected. Dagger, Deflect reaction, Uncanny Poise. Does not fight recklessly — fights efficiently. Every movement has a purpose.

Part Three — The Arrest
G Caen Rhys arrives with guards ▼

Caen Rhys arrives in Cooper's Square on foot — Pren and Tev a step behind, Oswin further back, the only armed person present. He does not need more than this. The fight is over. The party is standing in its aftermath. He walks to the centre of the square and looks at what has happened without visible reaction. He has already decided.

The crowd is at the edges — ordinary people drawn by noise and festival light. They are the mechanism. Caen Rhys is the authority. Together they are enough.

Caen RhysI am not going to ask what happened here. I am going to tell you what happens next. You will come with me. You will spend the night in the cells. And in the morning you will leave Tallowhaven. Whatever you were doing in this city is finished.

He does not wait for a response. The guards move. The party is taken to the Aldermen's Hall cells. Marek is already there.

DM Notes
Aldous CraneHe does not react to the accusation or the argument. He watches. When he has seen enough he stands and leaves without a word. His departure is heavier than anything said in the room — the deal is dead, and everyone knows it the moment he moves toward the door.
Cors HoldwornHis Courtesan loyalty is a background detail. In the teal path he fights for Oskar. In the pink path he steps toward the Sables during wave 2 — a quiet repositioning that most parties will miss. Do not draw attention to it. If a player notices and asks about it later, it is a thread available to pull in Act 2.
Ysolde's ThunderwaveThe master weaver casting a spell is the most surprising moment of the teal path fight. She casts once, walks away, and does not look back. She is not a combatant — she is a woman who has spent sixty years building something and spent one spell defending it.
Anneth HoldwornShe does not fight. She may remain in Cooper's Square and watch what happens to the party with the quiet satisfaction of someone who has been patient for a very long time. Or she may leave with Siw. Either works — let the mood of the session decide.
The escape attemptIf the faction leader flees and the party lets them go, that decision carries into Act 2. They are still out there. They know the party's faces. They know what was done. Leave it deliberately loose.
Replacement leaders — who sits whereIf Seva was killed, Elbeth leads the Courtesans. She is acting head, not a trained negotiator — she is angrier and less composed than Seva would have been, which makes the signing room more volatile. If Alric was killed, Caen Rhys holds the Merchant seat as a civic anchor — he is not a guild member, and the Driftwood delegation notices. If Oskar was killed, Anneth Holdworn leads the Trademasters. She is patient and controlled — the signing room is quieter than it would have been with Oskar, which is almost worse.
The assassination paths and the faction tabsThe faction tab selector above controls which accusation and Cooper's Square content is visible. For assassination paths: Path 1 (killed Seva, sided with Alric) and Path 4 (warned Alric, sided with Alric) both use the blue tab. Path 3 (killed Alric, sided with Oskar) and Path 6 (warned Oskar, sided with Oskar) both use the teal tab. Path 2 (warned Seva, sided with Seva) and Path 5 (killed Oskar, sided with Seva) both use the pink tab. Within each tab, the specific accusation beat is labelled by path number.
Cors Holdworn — assassination path exceptionIn Path 5 (Oskar killed), Cors does not step sideways to the Sables. He is grieving Oskar in his own way and his loyalties are temporarily suspended. He fights in the Trademaster formation through the whole fight — which is unusual and may prompt a player question worth leaving open.
Caen Rhys — no guardsCaen Rhys arrives with Pren, Tev, and Oswin only. There is no standing guard force in Tallowhaven. His authority comes from the crowd at the edges and from the legitimacy of his position — not from swords. The party can see exactly who is behind him. That is the point.
