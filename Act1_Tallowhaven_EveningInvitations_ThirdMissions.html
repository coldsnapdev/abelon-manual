Encounter Reference — Act 1 / Tallowhaven / Third Missions
The Evening Invitations
Seven o'clock. Two invitations. Three plots. The party only sees two of them.
Choice pointNo combatMoral weight: extremeSeeds the finale

On the eve of the signing, two guild leaders invite the party to meet at seven o'clock, in two different places. Alric wants Seva removed. Seva wants Oskar removed, as a way to ruin Alric. Oskar sends no invitation at all: the party never earned his trust, so he sends Bram after Alric instead. Each leader believes they alone see the whole picture and are acting first. None of them suspects the others are doing the same, and the party are pawns in all three plots.

At a glance
The invitations: Alric's arrives by note, addressed to “Danzel and company”. Danzel being named is odd, since he's the newest member; the party will read into it whatever they like. Seva's comes through Mira. Both say seven o'clock.
Seven o'clock only: The offer exists only in that meeting. If the party, or the leader, leaves before the ask is made, it never gets made. Alric in particular has a busy evening before the signing.
Who turns up: If the party splits, each leader notices who is missing, comments, and carries on. Each says something like “Ah... good... that might be a good thing actually...”, trails off, and returns to the point. Asked what they mean: “Nothing at all. Now, where was I?”
Never quite “kill”: Alric heavily implies it but never says it. Seva slips once: “before his body is cold”.
Mostly right: Each leader's case is reasonable until the last step. The villainy is in the method, not the argument.
Location Descriptions
The Shallow Tuber (Alric)

Busy, as it always is in the evening. Alric meets the party in the common room and pulls them into a private area for the discussion: his territory and terms. Food on the table, untouched. Composed, well dressed, no pleasantries before he begins. Huw is not with him. In the common room as the party arrives: Bram, the big man who keeps leaving whenever the party talk in public, drink half-finished with a coin beside it. He is there to watch Alric, not them.

The Amber Cup (Seva)

Busy, as it always is in the evening. Seva meets the party in the crowd and pulls them into a private area for the discussion. Unhurried; she has been thinking about this conversation for some time. Mira comes and goes.

NPCs
Alric Venn Wants Seva Sable removed before the signing
▼
Goal

Break Seva's hold on the Sable network. He sees she can undermine his goals and move against him without him noticing. Mira is the replacement he'd prefer to deal with. He wants Seva dead, and never says so.

Why Danzel

He addresses the invitation to Danzel on purpose and says so openly. He thinks a Sable might be ambitious enough, or “wise” enough, to see that Seva's intractability is holding the network and the city back. He isn't trying to slip anything past Danzel; he's betting Danzel will listen anyway.

His case, straight bat

The network benefits enormously from Tallowhaven's growth: more people, more money, more work. Why shouldn't it pay back to the city like every other business? Guild membership is nothing more than registering as a legitimate business, and with it comes protection in numbers. Seva's refusal isn't keeping her people safe, it's pushing them underground. “Seva will take the network down with her, unless we act decisively now.” Is that a prediction or a threat? He'd say prediction. Insight, or Danzel's own instincts, may read it as both.

The Elbeth question

He fishes rather than accuses, and every answer the party gives makes his case: “Now, who was it who sent you looking for that girl? Was it Seva? No? Hmm... how strange that someone primarily interested in the safety of courtesans seemed so disinterested in the safety of a courtesan. Still, when you came back I'm sure she made sure to tell you how grateful she was, right? She wouldn't have left that to someone who actually cares, would she? Anyway... how's Mira doing?” If they lie or deflect, the line misses and he moves on. Also: “They had nobody to run to but a group of outsider adventurers, after all.”

Pre-empting refusal

“If you take this to her, you'll tell her nothing she doesn't know. She knows I'd rather she wasn't at that table. So the question isn't whether you'll warn her. It's whether you think she deserves the seat.” Asking costs him nothing.

If challenged about Oskar

He has every reason to oppose Oskar, who has been working against him. Asked why he isn't going after Oskar instead, he says Oskar has his own strings that others are pulling, gestures vaguely, and says that bill will come due, after the deal is done.

If Danzel isn't there

Notices, comments, then: “Ah... good... that might be a good thing actually...” He means the others can hear the case without one of Seva's own in the room. He makes the pitch to whoever came.

Seva Sable Wants Oskar Sternov removed, to ruin Alric
▼
Goal

Seva knows the trade deal can't be killed: stop it now and it comes back in a few years. She wants it done in a way that protects the network, without being seen anywhere near it. Her real target is Alric's reputation, not Oskar.

Surface reason

Nobody will miss Oskar. The Trumpet can come up in passing, for colour (the party never worked for Oskar, so they won't know the details): he had people intimidate a journalist and hurt a young man to protect his certification system.

The real plan

Nobody will care, or even know, that people working on the deal attacked the network. But if the Trademasters are attacked too, people will seriously question whether Alric's soft, collaborative approach gave away too much, and whether the deal is right for Tallowhaven at all. Is the benefit worth the cost when it's tearing the city apart? She doesn't want him seen as a pantomime villain (“as Danzel once played so brilliantly”) or a misunderstood renaissance man, but as someone who is just bad at his job. In a city that runs on reputation and influence, that's worse than death.

It mustn't look like Alric

If Oskar dies and it looks like Alric's doing, Alric gains a ruthless reputation, which Tallowhaven grudgingly respects. So it has to look like Driftwood: leave Emlyn Hale's note on the body, and her disinformation will hang rumours and innuendo on it.

The guild won't collapse

Cors Holdworn (Danzel knows he's allied to the network, though not a courtesan) assures her that his mother, Anneth, would be a natural, trusted steward for the Trademasters in the short term. A sharp player may notice that gives Seva a seat at that table too.

The slip

“His replacement will arrive before his body is cold.” Said lightly, never taken back. It's the only time any leader effectively says kill.

If challenged

If someone points out that her plan tears the city apart just as much as Alric's, she doesn't deny it: “The city was always going to tear. I'd just rather its fabric is in my hands when it does.” She's counting on them not noticing.

If Danzel isn't there

Notices, comments, then: “Ah... good... that might be a good thing actually...” She means nothing ties the job to the network. She's pleased, and slightly relieved.

Bram Tallis Oskar's fixer, sent after Alric. Off camera.
▼
Who he is

Oskar's lifelong friend, who never passed the certifications, and his right-hand man ever since. Physically imposing, smart and cunning, but not brave: he could win most fights and would still run. He gathers information the way Seva's people do, but he's too big and too obvious to linger, which is why the party keep seeing him leave.

Tonight

Oskar didn't trust the party with the job, so he sent Bram. Bram is in the Shallow Tuber when the party arrive. He makes his attempt on Alric off camera, at whatever time the party act on their own choice. He lands a punch, loses his nerve and runs.

What the party sees

Nothing, until the morning: Alric arrives at the signing with a bruise under his left eye. He never explains it, and Bram now knows better than to try again.

Hidden, permanently

Bram is chronically in debt to the people who pull Oskar's strings. Oskar doesn't know. The party almost certainly never will.

Oskar Sternov Sends no invitation
▼
Why not

The party turned down his warehouse job and never got close to him. He doesn't trust them with this, so he sends Bram after Alric instead.

Encounter Beats
A The invitations arrive ▼

Within the same hour: a note from Alric addressed to “Danzel and company”, and a message from Seva through Mira. Both name seven o'clock. Neither says why.

From the party's side these are just two meetings. They might guess at a performance, a courtesan booking or curiosity about the Sables. Whoever goes where is an ordinary practical choice, not a judgement about loyalty.

B Alric at the Shallow Tuber -- Seva must go ▼

Bram is in the common room as they come in. Alric waits for whoever has come to sit down, notices who hasn't, and makes his case. Business first, then the Elbeth questions, then the ask: he never says kill, only that she can't be at that table.

Take the jobAlric pays half immediately and tells them Seva will be at the Amber Cup tonight. He expects them at the signing as his guests.
RefuseHe takes it calmly. He's already priced in the refusal, and if it reaches Seva he's planted doubt about her own people either way.
Warn SevaShe learns nothing she didn't know about Alric's dislike of her. What unsettles her is why he thought one of her own might say yes.
C Seva at the Amber Cup -- Oskar must go ▼

Seva draws them into a private area off the busy room. She starts with the surface reason and only reveals the strategy about Alric if pressed, or if someone asks the right question: “And what do you get from this?”

Take the jobSeva pays half immediately, more than they'd expect, and says Oskar will be at the Honeywell office tonight. She wants a note in Emlyn Hale's hand left on the body.
RefuseShe accepts it without fuss and watches them more closely from now on.
Warn OskarOskar receives it like someone who suspected something was coming. He offers standing with the guild rather than money, and arrives at the signing grimly prepared.
D The morning after ▼

Alric arrives at the signing with a bruise under his left eye and never says why. If the party compare notes, they will have seen two of the three plots and the bruise hints at a third. The people who each think they see the whole picture are the ones who can't. The pawns are the only ones who saw more than one room.

DM Notes
The shared flawEvery leader underestimates the others. Each is generally suspicious, but none suspects the specific move being made against them. Each believes they're acting first and fastest, taking the brave or necessary step nobody else could. Don't let any of them guess what the other is asking the party to do.
Let them be mostly rightAlric's argument for regulating the network is reasonable; Seva's distrust of guild registers is too. The party should notice they agreed with each leader right up to the last step.
The offer is now or neverNo second chances: if the meeting ends before the ask, there is no ask. Neither leader chases the party afterwards.
Huw isn't thereHe's unconscious in Serastra's Rest after the Spine collapsed. Alric is alone.
What happens nextWhatever the party chooses tonight plays out at the signing tomorrow: who's in the room, who knows what, and who's already been betrayed before anyone sits down.
